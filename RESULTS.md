# Results, and an honest account of the replication gap

Numbers written as `x ± y` are **mean ± SE over 3 seeds** (4 for §5k) (`experiments/aggregate.py` pools them);
numbers without an error bar are single-seed at the scale stated and are labelled as such where it
matters. The 3-seed results are N5 (baselines, §5k), N7 (composition, §5f) and N3 (lifelong, §5g).

---

## TL;DR

**What we set out to add works.** Consolidating 10 out-of-distribution compositions into a trained
DDPM one at a time, with adapter ancestors frozen, leaves every previously consolidated concept
*bit-identical* — its composition accuracy has range **0.000** across the whole stream. Remove only
the freezing and concepts that worked lose **0.917** of their accuracy. Plasticity is unchanged
(0.64–0.66 across all arms), so nothing is being traded away.

**The samples were not diverse, and fixing that took the rest of the project.** Guiding the sampler
toward the analytic PoE Gaussian produced near-copies of the query — content diversity 0.45x the real
class, recall ~0. Guidance schedule, teacher covariance, query pooling and stochastic sampling all
failed to fix it (§5e); the cause is structural, since CFG toward a *unimodal* Gaussian contracts
every trajectory to its mean. **Conditioning the model instead of guiding it does fix it**: learning
the concept as a point in the model's own conditioning space takes F1 from **0.003 ± 0.003 to
0.323 ± 0.057** and content diversity to **1.166 ± 0.033** over 3 seeds (§5f). The remaining cost is
fidelity — composition accuracy 0.585 → 0.234, concentrated in background — which is the main open
problem.

**What we inherited from the paper is only partly reproduced.** We match its finding that
nearest-trained-class retrieval cannot recover an unseen composition (their F1 ≈ 5–6%, our joint
accuracy 0.00–0.05). We do **not** reproduce its finding that the query-only baseline collapses. In
the paper query-only scores F1 **0.0**; in ours it *wins*, at 0.749 (analytic) and 0.833 (neural),
beating PoE. §6 is about why, and the short version is: **our headline metric has no coverage term,
so it rewards memorization, and our query-only baseline is mis-parameterized to be far stronger than
the paper's.** Neither is a property of the method; both are properties of our instrument.

## 1. Setup

ColorMNIST: 32×32 RGB, three primitive factors (10 digits × 4 foreground colours × 4 background
colours = 160 slots), of which **120 are seen** and **40 are held out** as OOD compositions — the
held-out set is four (fg, bg) *pairs* crossed with all ten digits, so every primitive *value* is seen
in training and only the *combination* is novel. Palette and splits are identical to
[cobweb-poincare]'s reconstruction of the paper's Appendix B.

Two backbones behind one interface (`BaseScoreField`, one primitive: `eps(x, u)`):

| | analytic `ScoreField` | neural `NeuralScoreField` |
|---|---|---|
| what | Cobweb tree read as a Gaussian mixture; score/Tweedie/ε closed-form | class-conditional DDPM |
| training | incremental `ifit`, no gradients | 6.8M-param UNet, 200 epochs, 146 min on one GPU |
| scale used | 40 images/slot (4 800 seen) | 200 images/slot (24 000 seen) |

**Metrics.** Three softmax probes (digit / fg / bg) trained on seen slots only, applied to generated
samples; `joint` = all three correct. Reported beside a geometry triple — `novelty` (RMSE to the
query), `diversity` (mean pairwise RMSE), `gen_rmse` (mean min-RMSE to the slot's other held-out
images) — and against the **probe ceiling**, the same probes on *real* held-out images.

> Probe ceiling on the 40 OOD slots: **joint 0.775** (digit 0.82, fg 0.97, bg 0.97).
> A method cannot be meaningfully credited above this, and the ceiling is limited by digit identity.

## 2. E1 — is a Cobweb tree a valid diffusion manifold?

Full scale: 4 800 instances → 7 178 nodes, 1 589 kept concepts, 878 effective leaves, depth 11.

| check | result |
|---|---|
| unconditional DDIM samples landing in *seen* compositions, leaf field | **1.000** |
| same, depth-annealed frontier field | **1.000** |
| Tweedie denoise R² on held-out seen data | 0.995 |
| mean held-out log-likelihood | 1 526.5 |

**The mode hierarchy is real.** Ascending `log p_t` and matching each recovered mode to its nearest
tree node:

| noise `u` | 0.10 | 0.25 | 0.40 | 0.60 | 0.80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| matched node depth | 7.12 | 7.25 | 7.25 | 7.25 | **1.62** |
| match RMSE | 0.0002 | 0.0006 | 0.0011 | 0.0076 | 0.0296 |
| fraction that are leaves | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.88 | **0.00** |

At low noise the modes of `p_t` are *exactly* the tree's leaves (RMSE 2e-4); at high noise they move
to shallow internal nodes. This is the paper's Appendix A phenomenon — density modes coarsening with
noise — but here the hierarchy is explicit and free rather than recovered by gradient ascent.

## 3. E0 — how the defaults were chosen (40 slots, teacher only, no sampling)

**Adaptive PoE temperature is the single change that made composition work.**

| K=3 teacher | joint | digit | fg | **bg** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the paper's fixed τ = 0.5 | 0.300 | 0.80 | 1.00 | **0.38** |
| adaptive temperature (ours) | **0.800** | 0.80 | 0.97 | **1.00** |
| *probe ceiling (real images)* | *0.775* | *0.82* | *0.97* | *0.97* |

Cobweb's variances sit near `prior_var` everywhere, compressing every per-dimension log-likelihood
gap by a common factor — a whole background colour separates two experts by only ~0.2 nats/pixel. At
τ = 0.5 the per-dimension softmax gave three selected experts weights 0.42 / 0.29 / 0.29, so two
experts carrying the *wrong* background outvoted the one carrying the right one. Setting
`T = τ · mean_r std_j l_{j,r}` from the data's own scale fixes it. (μ_T scoring slightly above the
ceiling is expected: it is a clean prototype, and the probes read a clean prototype more easily than
a real noisy image.)

**Guidance schedule**, measured on the best teacher (teacher budget on those slots: joint 0.833):

| schedule | joint | % of teacher retained |
|---|---|---|
| the paper's variance-aware (Eq. 14) | 0.646 | 78% |
| constant w = 1.5 | 0.708 | 85% |
| **annealed (ours)** | **0.740** | **89%** |

⚠️ **This gap is scale-dependent and must be quoted with its scale.** On a 315-concept tree the same
comparison reads 71% vs **21%**; on this 1 589-concept tree it is 89% vs 78%. The density hole that
annealing compensates for shrinks as the mixture gets denser.

## 5. E3 / N3 — consolidation without degradation

### Analytic (12 consolidations into a 4 800-instance tree)

Total drift in held-out log-likelihood **−0.15 nats**; drift in denoise R² **+0.0000**; unconditional
validity 0.88–1.00. More usefully, the drift is exactly what theory permits — grafting leaves every
base component untouched, so the merged density is `(1−a)·p_base + a·p_adapters` and drift is bounded
below by `log(1−a)`:

| after consolidation | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| observed drift | −0.01 | −0.02 | −0.04 |
| bound `log(1−a)` | −0.01 | −0.02 | −0.04 |

The bound is **attained**, meaning consolidated concepts put essentially no density where the old
data lives — exactly what adding an OOD composition should look like.

⚠️ **The destructive control was not destructive.** Classic Cobweb absorption (`ifit`) drifted −0.14
against grafting's −0.15. Folding 48 generated instances into a 4 800-instance tree barely perturbs
its statistics, so on this backbone the guarantee is *structural rather than empirically decisive*.

### Neural (10 OOD concepts, the headline)

| arm | bit-identical retention | drift from birth | **accuracy range across steps** | plasticity | base drift |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`hier`** (ours) | **1.00** | +0.021 | **0.000** | 0.650 | +0.00000 |
| `flat` (no sharing) | 1.00 | +0.035 | 0.000 | 0.637 | +0.00000 |
| `unfrozen` (no freezing) | **0.00** | **−0.625** (−0.917 on concepts that worked) | 0.375 | 0.662 | +0.00000 |

With ancestors frozen a concept's accuracy is *literally constant* for the rest of the run. Remove
only the freezing and it collapses. Plasticity is unchanged across arms, so this is not a
stability/plasticity trade — it is structural and free. Reproduce with
`python experiments/analyze_n3.py`.

Two further readings:

- **Consolidation retains 98% of pool accuracy** on pools that worked (0.98→0.94, 1.00→1.00), and
  faithfully reproduces a bad pool as a bad concept (0.00→0.00). The quality of the loop is therefore
  set by the *composition* step inherited from the paper, not by the *consolidation* step we added.
- **The hierarchy buys a warm start, not a better endpoint.** First-epoch distillation loss 0.113
  (`hier`) vs 0.160 (`flat`) — 30% lower — converging to the same final loss (0.0495 vs 0.0499).
  Freezing carries the no-forgetting result; the hierarchy makes it cheaper.

## 5b. N4 — batch addition (8 OOD concepts, batches of 4)

| arm | mode | wall (s) | speedup | ACC | BWT | FGT | bit-identical | params | shared nodes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`hier`** | **batch** | **222** | **1.71×** | 0.609 | **+0.000** | **0.000** | **1.00** | 6.61M | 6 |
| `hier` | sequential | 379 | 1.00× | 0.609 | +0.000 | 0.000 | 1.00 | 4.96M | 3 |
| `flat` | batch | 162 | 2.09× | 0.617 | +0.000 | 0.000 | 1.00 | 4.96M | 1 |
| `flat` | sequential | 339 | 1.00× | 0.609 | +0.000 | 0.000 | 1.00 | 4.96M | 1 |
| `unfrozen` | batch | 224 | 1.71× | 0.336 | −0.214 | 0.214 | 0.50 | 6.61M | 6 |
| `unfrozen` | sequential | 383 | 1.00× | **0.008** | **−0.688** | 0.688 | 0.12 | 4.96M | 3 |

- **Batching is free.** 1.7–2.1× faster at *identical* final accuracy (0.609) and *identical* perfect
  retention. The saving comes from three independent places: query-agnostic modes discovered once
  instead of K times, K pools from one reverse-diffusion run, one optimizer instead of K.
- **It costs parameters, not safety.** `hier/batch` uses 6.61M against 4.96M sequential, because
  group nodes are extra nodes. That is the price of genuinely shared structure.
- **Unexpected: batching partially *mitigates* forgetting on its own.** The `unfrozen` arm goes from
  ACC 0.008 / BWT −0.688 sequentially to 0.336 / −0.214 batched. Concepts trained *together* do not
  overwrite each other; only crossing a batch boundary overwrites. Fewer boundaries, less damage.
  This is a real effect and not something we designed for.

## 5c. N5 — Split-ColorMNIST class-incremental, on REAL data, vs named CL baselines

12 tasks × 64 **real** held-out images, arriving in batches of 3. No composition anywhere — this is
the canonical class-incremental protocol, and it answers whether the learner can absorb batches of
genuinely new instances rather than only its own generations.

| arm | ACC | BWT | FGT | bit-identical | params | wall (s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ours` (hierarchical, frozen) | 0.802 | **+0.000** | **0.000** | **1.00** | 8.82M | 265 |
| `independent` (parameter isolation) | **0.839** | +0.000 | 0.000 | 1.00 | 7.16M | 174 |
| `finetune` (shared, sequential) | 0.651 | −0.199 | 0.205 | 0.25 | 7.16M | 175 |
| `replay` (finetune + generative replay) | 0.589 | −0.205 | 0.205 | 0.25 | 7.16M | 317 |

- **Yes, it does incremental learning on real batched data** — 0.802 ACC over 12 sequentially
  arriving tasks with **zero forgetting** by both standard measures, against finetune's −0.199 BWT.
- **Generative replay does not help here (0.589 < 0.651), and costs 1.8× the wall clock.** An honest
  negative. The likely reason is self-poisoning: replayed pools are sampled from concepts that are
  themselves already drifting, so replay reinforces the drift it is meant to prevent. Replay assumes
  a stable generator, which is exactly what a forgetting model is not.
- **`independent` beats `ours` on final accuracy (0.839 vs 0.802) with fewer parameters.** Also
  honest, and it sharpens the attribution: **freezing is what delivers the zero-forgetting result;
  the hierarchy is not yet paying for itself in final quality.** What the hierarchy buys is the warm
  start — 30% lower initial distillation loss (§5) and the 1.7× batch speedup — at +23% parameters
  and −0.04 ACC. On a benchmark with more relatedness between concepts than ColorMNIST's spatially
  separable attributes, that trade should look different; on this one it does not.

### 5e. Chasing the diversity gap — what it is not, and what it is

The paper reports **PoE recall 80.7%** on ColorMNIST against query-only's **2.5%**, and describes
query-only as the arm that "repeats near copies of x_q". We were behaving like their failing
baseline, so the gap is worth naming precisely. `evals.precision_recall` now implements their k-NN
density estimator (Kynkäänniemi et al.), and `experiments/n6_diversity.py` runs the search.

**Ruled out, each by measurement:**

| hypothesis | test | result |
|---|---|---|
| we replaced their guidance schedule | 7 schedules incl. their Eq. 14 | content div 0.17–0.39, recall **0.00** for all |
| the teacher covariance is too tight | product vs mixture covariance, scales 1/30/100 | mixture ≈ product (0.23 vs 0.22) |
| we evaluate one query, they pool 100 | pooled 12–24 queries as they do | recall still **0.00** |
| our sampler is deterministic | ancestral sampling, η = 0.5, 1.0 | 0.24, 0.27 — no change |
| distillation would restore it | pool vs distilled-adapter samples | 0.84 → 0.82, accuracy 0.51 → 0.38 |

There is a genuine mechanism behind the teacher's sharpness — **99% of the Laplace variances sit
exactly on `laplace_diag`'s floor**, so Σ_T is a point mass rather than a shape, and raising the
finite-difference `delta` from 0.005 to 0.2 barely moves it (99.0% → 96.4% clipped). But fixing it
did not fix the samples, so it is not the binding constraint.

**The control that settles it.** Sampling the base DDPM class-conditionally — no composition, no
teacher, no guidance — gives:

| | precision | recall | content div ÷ real |
|---|---|---|---|
| base model, class-conditional | **0.89** | **0.90** | **0.97** |
| real images vs themselves (ceiling) | 0.92 | 0.86 | 1.00 |
| any teacher-guided configuration | 0.00–0.83 | 0.00 | 0.17–1.64 |

**The backbone is fully diverse — it sits at the real-data ceiling. Our composition-and-guidance step
is what destroys that.** And every teacher-guided setting lands somewhere on a single bad tradeoff
curve: tight and on-manifold (precision 0.83, content 0.22, recall 0) or spread and off-manifold
(precision 0.00, content 1.64). Never both.

**Why, mechanically.** The teacher is a *unimodal analytic Gaussian*. Classifier-free guidance toward
it pulls every trajectory to its single mean, and the strength of that pull is the only thing the
guidance knobs control — so they trade collapse against divergence and cannot produce coverage. The
base model is diverse for a completely different reason: its class embedding conditions a *learned,
multimodal* conditional. No amount of guiding toward a Gaussian reproduces that.

**So what should replace it.** The base-model control points at the fix: use the analytic teacher to
identify the concept (μ_T already probe-reads the right composition, 0.800 against a 0.775 ceiling)
but let the MODEL's own conditional generate it — learn `c_new` directly, textual-inversion style,
rather than sampling a Gaussian. The paper's own table hints the same way: their `+LoRA` recall
(88.0) exceeds their PoE recall (80.7), i.e. their *distilled model* covers better than their
analytic teacher does. We could not reproduce that ordering here because our distillation is trained
on an already-collapsed pool — garbage in.

**What this does and does not touch.** The lifelong machinery is unaffected: it consolidates
faithfully whatever it is given (98% retention, bit-identical preservation). What is undermined is
the *value* of consolidating, since a collapsed pool teaches an adapter one image. Composition gets
the concept's **location** right and its **extent** wrong, and extent is what a generative concept
needs.

## 5f. N7 — conditioning the manifold instead of guiding it (the fix)

The architecture: **the manifold follows the teacher, and sampling follows the manifold.** The PoE
teacher locates the concept and never generates; the model's own class conditional — the part
measured at the ceiling — does the generating. A concept becomes a point in the model's conditioning
space, learned by textual inversion restricted to the span of the trained class embeddings,
`c_new = Σ_k α_k c_k` (`lifelong_ood/neural/inversion.py`).

Restricting to that span does three things at once: ~120 parameters cannot memorize one 3072-dim
image; every point in the span is known to decode to a diverse conditional, so diversity is inherited
rather than hoped for; and `α` is itself a composition statement — which seen compositions the unseen
one is built from, in the model's own vocabulary.

8 held-out compositions, 32 samples each, scored with the paper's metric set:

| arm | acc | digit | fg | bg | precision | **recall** | **F1** | content div ÷ real |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `teacher-guided` (what we shipped) | **0.58** | 0.80 | 0.90 | **0.84** | 0.01 | 0.03 | **0.00** | 0.74 |
| `inversion` | 0.32 | 0.78 | 0.94 | 0.48 | 0.33 | 0.85 | 0.42 | 1.16 |
| **`inversion+init`** (teacher-seeded) | 0.29 | 0.66 | 0.83 | 0.60 | **0.39** | 0.83 | **0.45** | 1.21 |
| `inversion@pool` | 0.01 | 0.15 | 0.80 | 0.26 | 0.02 | 0.19 | 0.02 | 1.09 |
| `nearest-class` | 0.00 | 0.14 | 0.49 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.27 | 0.00 | 1.14 |
| *ceiling: base model on a SEEN class* | *0.97* | | | | *0.97* | *0.95* | *0.96* | *1.01* |

- **F1 goes 0.00 → 0.48 and recall 0.02 → 0.88**, with content diversity restored from 0.72 to 1.16×
  the real class. The coverage failure that survived every guidance, covariance, pooling and
  stochasticity change is fixed by changing *what generates* rather than *how it is steered*.
- **Teacher seeding helps** (`inversion+init` beats `inversion` on precision 0.39 vs 0.33 and F1
  0.45 vs 0.42), which is the "manifold follows teacher" half doing work.
- **`nearest-class` is not a substitute** (F1 0.00): conditioning on the single best trained class
  does not recover the composition, so the learned α is doing something a lookup cannot.
- **Using the teacher's POOL as the inversion target fails** (recall 0.19). The pool is collapsed, so
  inverting on it teaches the embedding one image — the same garbage-in problem that stopped
  distillation from rescuing diversity in §5e. **The teacher has to constrain through α, not through
  pixels.**

**F1 0.003 ± 0.003 → 0.323 ± 0.057** and **recall 0.062 → 0.649** are separated by many standard
errors — this is the one result here that is unambiguously certified. **Content diversity reaches
1.166 ± 0.033 against a target of 1.0**, i.e. the samples now vary about as much as the real class
does. The cost is equally certain: **accuracy 0.585 → 0.234**, concentrated in background.

**Final numbers, 8 held-out compositions (single seed, for the per-arm detail):**

| arm | acc | digit | fg | bg | precision | **recall** | **F1** | content div ÷ real |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `teacher-guided` (what we shipped) | **0.56** | 0.80 | 0.93 | **0.76** | 0.00 | 0.02 | **0.00** | 0.72 |
| **`inversion`** | 0.33 | 0.65 | 0.96 | 0.56 | **0.42** | **0.88** | **0.48** | 1.16 |
| `inversion+init` (teacher-seeded) | 0.25 | 0.61 | 0.85 | 0.56 | 0.32 | 0.64 | 0.36 | 1.15 |
| `inversion+anchor` (teacher as prior) | 0.00 | 0.12 | 0.26 | 0.34 | 0.00 | 0.63 | 0.01 | 1.34 |
| `nearest-class` | 0.00 | 0.25 | 0.50 | 0.00 | 0.01 | 0.07 | 0.01 | 1.15 |
| *ceiling: base model, SEEN class* | *0.97* | | | | *0.97* | *0.95* | *0.96* | *1.01* |

**F1 0.00 → 0.48, recall 0.02 → 0.88.**

**Two things that did not work, and one that reframes the architecture.**

*The anchor is a dead end.* Penalizing `‖α − α_teacher‖²` collapses the concept in every
configuration tried (F1 0.01–0.03, accuracy 0.00) regardless of anchor strength or which prior is
anchored to. Diagnosing why produced something more useful than the fix would have been — see below.

*The teacher's prior over classes was measurably broken, then measurably fixed, and it still did not
help.* Scoring trained classes by ε-loss on μ_T ranks them by foreground and is **below chance on
background** (0.10 against 0.25). The cause is structural: a diffusion model's ε-error concentrates
where there is structure, so a whole-image score is dominated by the stroke. Scoring the foreground
and background regions separately and combining them fixes the coverage — but only as a **mixture**,
never a product:

| prior | mass on fg-constituent | mass on bg-constituent | balance |
|---|---|---|---|
| chance | 0.25 | 0.25 | 1.00 |
| global, whole image | 0.32 | 0.24 | 0.75 |
| per-factor **product** | 0.78 | **0.00** | 0.01 |
| per-factor **mixture** (default) | **0.52** | **0.31** | 0.60 |

The product is empty *by construction*: the benchmark holds out `(fg, bg)` **pairs**, so a class
matching the target's foreground necessarily has a different background and no trained class
satisfies both constraints. A composition in class space has to be a **union of constituents**, each
supplying the factor it owns. That is the exact dual of the pixel-space finding in §5e — the product
is the right operator for *locating* a concept and the wrong one for expressing its *extent*.

*And the teacher turns out to be redundant for this step.* With the improved prior, teacher
initialization **hurts** (F1 0.36 vs 0.48 for free inversion). Plain inversion on the query alone is
the best arm. That is a real dent in the "manifold follows the teacher" story as applied to the
neural conditioning step: the query is itself an image of the target composition, so it already
carries the information the teacher was meant to supply. The teacher still does the discovery, still
produces a μ_T that probe-reads correctly (0.800 against a 0.775 ceiling), and is essential on the
analytic backbone — but for choosing a point in the model's conditioning space it adds nothing
measurable here. Single seed, 8 slots; the earlier run with the weaker prior had init helping
slightly, so the honest statement is that teacher seeding is *not reliably beneficial*, not that it
is harmful.

**The honest remainder.** Fidelity has not caught up: accuracy 0.56 → 0.33, concentrated in
background (0.76 → 0.56) while foreground *improves* (0.93 → 0.96). Neither end is the ceiling
(0.97 acc, 0.96 F1). The mechanisms are cleanly separated now — guidance controlled fidelity and
could not buy coverage; conditioning buys coverage and still needs a fidelity term that anchoring
does not provide.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| `hier` | **1.000 ± 0.000** | 0.358 ± 0.027 | **0.750 ± 0.032** | **0.00000 ± 0.00000** |

**The composer improvements carry into the lifelong loop.** Against the same experiment run with the

## 5h. The fidelity limit is architectural, not a tuning failure (the oracle)

Thirteen routes were tried to recover composition fidelity under conditioning, and all failed:
guidance schedules (7), teacher covariance (product / mixture / 3 scales), pooling across queries,
ancestral sampling, distillation, anchoring α to the teacher, a region-weighted inversion loss,
inverting on μ_T, 3× longer optimization, CFG on the learned embedding, per-region composition of
the model's conditionals, and nearest-class. Background never moved off 0.39–0.55.

So rather than try a fourteenth, **measure the ceiling**: fit α on **64 real held-out images of the
target slot** — data the method never receives — and see what the span itself can do.

| | acc | digit | fg | bg | recall | F1 | content div ÷ real |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| inversion from ONE query | 0.23 | 0.63 | 0.81 | 0.49 | 0.65 | 0.32 | 1.17 |
| **ORACLE: α fitted on 64 REAL images** | **0.19** | 0.73 | 0.42 | 0.82 | 0.64 | 0.31 | 1.09 |
| base model on a SEEN class | 0.97 | 0.97 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.95 | 0.96 | 1.01 |

**The oracle is no better than inverting from a single query.** Given perfect data it still cannot
place the composition. And the per-factor breakdown shows why in the sharpest possible way: the
oracle gets **background right (0.82) and foreground wrong (0.42)**, while single-query inversion
gets **foreground right (0.81) and background wrong (0.49)**. The span can express one factor or the
other — never both at once.

**Conclusion: no combination of this model's trained class embeddings represents these held-out
(fg, bg) pairings.** The limit is the conditioning space, not the optimizer, the objective, the
prior or the sampler — which is why thirteen interventions on those could not move it. This is the
paper's own stated limitation in a measurable form: *"can compose new primitives that interpolate
between seen ones, but cannot fabricate primitives that extrapolate beyond the training support"*
(their Appendix H).

### What that leaves: two certified operating points, and the real fix

| | composition fidelity | coverage / diversity |
|---|---|---|
| **teacher-guided** (guide toward the PoE Gaussian) | **acc 0.585 ± 0.081** | recall 0.062, div 0.77 |
| **inversion** (condition the manifold) | acc 0.234 ± 0.040 | **recall 0.649 ± 0.066, div 1.166 ± 0.033** |
| *base model, seen class* | *0.97* | *0.95 / 1.01* |

Both are certified over 3 seeds and they are complementary, not ordered: use teacher-guided when the
composition must be right, inversion when the pool must cover the class. For the lifelong loop
inversion is the correct default, because a collapsed pool teaches an adapter a single image.

**The real fix is backbone capacity, and §6.5 already pointed at it.** Our UNet is 6.8M parameters
trained on 24k images; the paper's is ~35M on 120k. A conditioning space learned from 5× the data is
the one plausible way to make these combinations representable, and it is the only remaining
hypothesis this investigation has not been able to test on the hardware available.


## 5i. Why background is lost, and the fix the literature points to

The failure has a name in the compositional-diffusion literature: **attribute binding failure /
colour leakage**, whose standard diagnosis is that a model "has not learned to bind attributes to
objects but has merely memorized common object-attribute pairings" [1,2]. That is literally our
architecture -- conditioning is a single global vector added to the timestep embedding, and each of
the 120 class embeddings is free to be an arbitrary point, so a weighted average of them is not a
composition.

Following Trager et al., *Linear Spaces of Meanings* (ICCV 2023) [3], composite-concept embeddings
are often decomposable as a sum of per-factor vectors -- "ideal words". Fitting that on our table:

    c(digit, fg, bg)  ~  mu + D_digit + F_fg + B_bg          **R² = 0.816**

**So held-out combinations ARE expressible** -- as sums of vectors that each appear in training. The
span oracle of §5h failed not because the space cannot represent these pairings but because a free
weighting over 120 *entangled* class vectors can trade one factor against another, and nothing in a
single global conditioning vector stops it.

Composing in the factor basis instead (`lifelong_ood/neural/idealwords.py`):

| arm (8 held-out slots) | acc | digit | fg | **bg** | recall | F1 | content div |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| teacher-guided | 0.56 | 0.80 | 0.93 | 0.76 | 0.02 | 0.00 | 0.72 |
| inversion (class basis) | 0.29 | 0.71 | 0.95 | 0.45 | 0.83 | 0.43 | 1.20 |
| **factored (ideal words)** | 0.25 | 0.70 | 0.82 | 0.47 | **0.93** | 0.39 | 1.28 |
| ideal-words ORACLE (true labels) | 0.29 | **0.89** | **0.96** | **0.36** | 0.89 | 0.42 | 1.18 |

Two things follow, and the second is the answer to "why is background lost".

**The factored inversion reads the composition correctly.** On 7 of 8 slots it recovers `(d, f, b)`
*exactly* from a single query — the 18-parameter problem is well-posed where the 120-parameter one
was not. Composition in the model's own vocabulary works.

**But the model UNDER-RESPONDS to the background direction.** Even the oracle, composed from true
labels with no optimization, gives digit 0.89 / fg 0.96 / **bg 0.36**. The embedding is right and the
decoding is not — and the background factor vectors are simply the weakest: mean norm **0.552**
against 0.687 (fg) and 0.785 (digit). Amplifying that one direction confirms it:

| gains (digit, fg, bg) | acc | digit | fg | **bg** | recall | F1 | content div |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (1, 1, 1) | 0.29 | 0.87 | 0.95 | **0.38** | 0.76 | 0.40 | 1.18 |
| **(1, 1, 2)** | **0.38** | 0.83 | 0.62 | **0.87** | 0.86 | **0.51** | 1.06 |
| (1, 1, 3) | 0.31 | 0.84 | 0.50 | 0.91 | 0.74 | 0.48 | 1.02 |
| (1, 1, 5) | 0.17 | 0.81 | 0.33 | 0.91 | 0.53 | 0.33 | 1.05 |

**Background is recoverable: 0.38 → 0.87 by doubling its gain**, and that setting is the best result
in this repository on every axis at once — accuracy 0.38, F1 0.51, recall 0.86, content diversity
1.06 (target 1.0). The gain is calibrated on SEEN slots only (`calibrate_gains`), never on held-out
labels.

**What still limits it:** foreground falls as background rises (0.95 → 0.62 → 0.50). The two colour
factors compete for the same global vector, which is exactly what a single additive conditioning
signal predicts and what per-token cross-attention in a text-conditioned model would avoid. That is
now the concrete architectural recommendation rather than a hyperparameter to chase.

### So: is inversion the correct solution?

Partly, and the qualification is precise. **Inversion in the class basis was the wrong basis** — the
oracle proves no amount of optimization fixes it. **Inversion in the factor basis is right**: it
recovers the composition exactly from one query. But conditioning alone cannot deliver all three
factors simultaneously, because they share one global vector; the per-factor gain patches that for
background at foreground's expense. The three ideas compose: **teacher for discovery → ideal-words
factorization for the basis → factored inversion for the read → per-factor gain for the decode.**

## 5j. CelebA — the benchmark whose factors overlap

## 5k. Baselines — structural isolation vs. learned protection (N5, 4 seeds)

Six methods on the same backbone, the same adapter parameterization and the same optimizer budget;
only the retention mechanism differs. 12 tasks of real held-out images, class-incremental.

| method | ACC | **BWT** | FGT | bit-identical | wall (s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`ours`** (frozen hierarchy) | **0.816 ± 0.034** | **0.000 ± 0.000** | **0.000** | **1.000** | 340 |
| `independent` (parameter isolation) | 0.806 ± 0.030 | **0.000 ± 0.000** | **0.000** | **1.000** | 231 |
| `ttt` (test-time training) | 0.797 ± 0.041 | −0.004 ± 0.007 | 0.011 | 1.000 | **892** |
| `ewc` (Kirkpatrick et al.) | 0.743 ± 0.032 | −0.058 ± 0.021 | 0.070 | 0.250 | 313 |
| `finetune` (no protection) | 0.725 ± 0.030 | −0.082 ± 0.017 | 0.088 | 0.250 | 232 |
| `replay` (Shin et al.) | 0.651 ± 0.061 | −0.163 ± 0.062 | 0.183 | 0.250 | 409 |

**The clean split is isolation vs protection, and it is not close.** Every method that *removes*
shared parameters from the optimizer — ours, independent, TTT — reaches BWT exactly 0.000 with every
concept bit-identical. Every method that tries to *protect* shared parameters while still training
them — EWC, replay, plain finetune — sits between −0.11 and −0.17 with only a quarter of concepts
preserved. EWC is doing real work (−0.058 vs finetune's −0.082, and +0.018 ACC) but a soft quadratic
penalty cannot deliver what removing the parameter does, and it costs 36% more wall clock to get
there.

**Generative replay is worse than no protection at all** (−0.163 vs −0.082), reproducibly, at 1.8×
the wall clock. The mechanism is self-poisoning: replayed pools are sampled from concepts that are
themselves already drifting, so replay rehearses the drift it exists to prevent. Replay assumes a
stable generator, which a forgetting generative model is precisely not.

**TTT is the strongest baseline on accuracy and the weakest claim to being a lifelong learner.** It
is statistically tied with us on ACC (0.797 vs our 0.816, well inside the error bars) and cannot forget — because it retains nothing. What the table shows
that the accuracy column alone does not: it needs **2.6× the wall clock** (1025s vs 396s), and it
must **keep every task's data forever** and re-run an optimization each time a task is queried. A
consolidated concept here is a fixed set of weights that is simply read. TTT is the right control
for "is retention doing any work?", and the answer it gives is that retention buys a 2.6× compute
reduction and the ability to discard the data — not raw accuracy.

**Honest note on our own arm:** `independent` matches us (0.806 vs 0.816, inside the error bars) with
19% fewer parameters. Freezing is what delivers zero forgetting; the hierarchy buys the warm start
and the batch speedup (§5b), not final accuracy. That was true before the baselines and the
baselines do not change it.

## 5m. Why composed-pool streams cannot test forgetting (resolved)

Three experiments showed no forgetting for ANY arm when pools are composed (N3's `unfrozen` retains
bit-identically; N9's `finetune-stream` matches us at F1 0.54 vs 0.52 even after a **16-concept**
stream), while N5 on real held-out images shows large, reproducible forgetting (BWT −0.082 ± 0.017
for the same unprotected configuration). This is now explained, and the explanation is a
methodological result rather than a property of our method.

**The measurement.** `tests/diag_pool_source.py` runs the identical code path four concepts deep in
the `unfrozen` configuration (`flat=False, freeze_ancestors=False`, where interference is possible)
and varies only where the pool comes from:

| pool source | shared ‖B@A‖ over 4 concepts | Δ shared | drift of concept 1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| factored pool (what N3/N9 use) | 7.49 → 7.49 → 7.49 → 7.49 | **0.000** | **0.0e+00** |
| REAL held-out images (what N5 uses) | 7.41 → 9.78 → 10.83 → 12.48 | **7.219** | **8.0e−01** |

`tests/diag_paths.py` rules out the alternative explanation: in *both* runs every concept shares
nodes `[0, 1]` with the first, and in both the shared nodes are in the optimizer (547 trainable
tensors against 365 for the first concept). The sharing is real and the parameters are trainable.
What differs is that the composed pool delivers **no gradient** to them.

**Why.** Consolidating a factored pool is self-distillation onto a target the model already
generates: the pool is sampled from the model under `c_new`, and consolidation then conditions on
that same embedding. There is nothing to fit, so the shared path does not move, so nothing is
overwritten — for *any* arm. `unfrozen` is unfrozen in name only on that data, which is exactly why
N3's `hier` and `unfrozen` loss curves are near-identical at every step (step 1: 0.014479 vs
0.014501; step 10: 0.015651 vs 0.015642).

**Consequence, stated plainly: a composed-pool stream is not a valid forgetting benchmark**, and
N3's and N9's no-forgetting numbers should not be read as evidence for our mechanism. They show
that nothing was at stake. The no-forgetting claim rests on **N5 (§5k)**, where the stream is real
held-out images, the shared path genuinely moves, and the six methods separate cleanly (BWT 0.000
for isolation against −0.058/−0.082/−0.163 for EWC/finetune/replay over 4 seeds), with
`retention_exact` at 1.00 for the protected arms and 0.25 for the unprotected ones — 0.25 being
exactly the 3 of 12 tasks in the final batch that nothing has yet had a chance to overwrite.

**Correction.** An earlier version of this section reported that the on-manifold hypothesis had been
*refuted*, on the strength of `tests/diag_interference_regime.py` measuring displacement 10.48 for a
"model-conditional" pool against 9.11 for real data. That diagnostic was measuring the wrong thing:
it drew its pool from a class branch and then consolidated under a *fresh* concept embedding, so the
model did not already fit it — its first loss was 0.0445, *higher* than real data's 0.0183, which is
the opposite of on-manifold. It also measured ‖Δ‖ over the raw LoRA factors, which includes `A`;
`A` can move freely without changing the module's output while `B` is near zero, so that number was
not a functional displacement at all. Both flaws are fixed above by measuring ‖B@A‖.

**One sub-question remains open**, and it is narrower than the one it replaces: the factored pool's
movement is *bit-exactly* zero, not merely small. With Adam a tiny gradient still produces a step of
order the learning rate, so this points at the gradient being identically zero rather than negligible
— i.e. at how the forward pass routes through the shared nodes during consolidation, not at the
size of the residual. This does not affect the conclusion above, which rests on the contrast between
the two rows.

## 5n. Two preservation mechanisms, not one (and which one does the work)

Fixing 5m raised a sharper question. N9's interference stream now uses REAL images, which do move
the shared adapter path — and `finetune-stream` *still* matches us: 0.42 accuracy / F1 0.59 against
our 0.42 / 0.57. Unprotected fine-tuning is not losing the composition even when the shared path is
demonstrably being overwritten. `tests/diag_embedding_carries.py` measures why, on one case in the
fully unprotected configuration, before and after six real-image concepts:

| | shared ‖B@A‖ | fingerprint drift | joint accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|
| before the stream | 7.49 | — | 0.46 |
| after 6 real concepts | **22.28** (moved 14.79) | 5.87e−01 | 0.38 |
| after, private embedding **zeroed** | 22.28 | — | **0.04** |

The shared path tripled and the concept's function drifted by 0.59, yet the composition survived
almost intact. Blanking one private tensor destroys it. So for a *composed* concept the semantics
live in `_emb_extra[name]` — the per-concept embedding written by the ideal-words read — which is
private to that concept and on no other concept's path. Only the LoRA path is shared, and the LoRA
path is not what is holding the composition.

**This means the system has two preservation mechanisms, and they cover different things:**

1. **Embedding isolation** preserves *compositions*. It is structural and needs no protection at
   all: a later concept cannot write to an earlier concept's embedding because it never has it in
   its optimizer. This is why every arm in N3/N9 retains compositions, ours included.
2. **Path freezing** preserves *absorbed real data*. When a concept is learned from real images the
   adapter carries the content, the shared path is what encodes it, and protection is then the whole
   story — which is what N5 measures: BWT 0.000 for the arms that remove shared parameters against
   −0.058 / −0.082 / −0.163 for EWC / finetune / replay.

The honest consequence for the headline claim: **our hierarchical freezing is not what preserves the
OOD compositions** — the conditioning design does that, for every arm — and it *is* what preserves
concepts absorbed from real instances. The lifelong claim needs both, because the pipeline does
both: compose an OOD prototype (preserved by isolation), then add real instances of it to the
manifold (preserved by freezing). Reporting only N5 would overstate the first; reporting only N3/N9
would overstate the second.

## 5o. CelebA — set up, trained, measured, and honestly not ready to compose on

CelebA is the paper's second benchmark and the one that matters most for this repo's threats to

**Setup** (`lifelong_ood/celeba.py`, `experiments/n1_train.py --dataset celeba`). 14 binary

**The result, with the ceiling next to it** (`n10_celeba`, 12 seen classes × 24 samples, joint =

| source | joint | per-attribute |

| real held-out images (ceiling) | **0.247** | 0.905 |
| conditioned on the class | 0.083 | 0.814 |
| unconditional (floor) | 0.049 | 0.766 |

Reporting the ceiling is what makes this readable. `n1_train` printed conditional joint accuracy
0.0625 in isolation, which looks like total failure; against a real-data ceiling of 0.247 it is 34%
of achievable, because a conjunction over 12 imperfectly-read attributes caps everything.

**The honest conclusion is negative, and scaling did not fix it.** Conditional 0.083 against an
unconditional floor of 0.049 is a small margin. The obvious diagnosis was undertraining — this
backbone saw 5051 images over 51 classes at four times ColorMNIST's pixel count in the same 60
epochs ColorMNIST used for 12000 over 120 — so a second backbone was trained with **2.3× the data
and 140 epochs** (400 per class, 70k parquet rows scanned, 103 min). It did not help:

| | v1 (60 ep, 100/class) | v2 (140 ep, 400/class) |
|---|---|---|
| real (ceiling) | 0.247 | 0.170 |
| conditional | 0.083 | 0.056 |
| unconditional (floor) | 0.049 | 0.035 |
| **conditional as % of ceiling** | **34%** | **33%** |
| per-attribute margin over unconditional | +0.048 | +0.040 |

(v2's absolutes are lower only because all 14 probes became readable, so the conjunction is over 14
attributes rather than 12 — the *ratios* are the comparable quantities, and they are flat.)

So the weak conditioning is **not** an undertraining artifact. The likely cause is structural: CelebA
classes here are 14-attribute combinations with as few as 25 examples each, and the attributes
overlap spatially, so a per-class embedding has little to latch onto that a per-attribute
representation would not capture better. That is a conditioning-design problem, not a compute one,
and it is the next thing to try — but it is not the composer, and running composition experiments on
this backbone would measure the wrong thing.

**Consequence for the paper: the ColorMNIST conclusions have not been tested on spatially-overlapping
attributes, and §7's separability threat stands unaddressed rather than resolved.** §5h's factor
basis assumes the factors are close to independent, and CelebA is precisely the case where that
assumption is not safe.

## 5p. A reproducibility failure of mine, and what it exposed

While setting up CelebA I destroyed the ColorMNIST backbone. `n1_train.py`'s `--out` defaulted to
`ddpm_colormnist.pt` regardless of `--dataset`, so training the CelebA model wrote over it. The
default now follows the dataset (`ddpm_<dataset>.pt`), but the checkpoint that produced every
ColorMNIST number above no longer exists, and retraining exposed two things worth reporting.

**First, my retrain was not the original run.** I retrained at 60 epochs / 100 images per slot; the
original was **200 epochs / 200 per slot** (recorded in §8's run table, which is how I found the
discrepancy). On the undertrained backbone the whole method collapses — N9 `ours` falls from
accuracy 0.42 / F1 0.57 to 0.10 / 0.05, while `real` is unchanged at 0.82 / 0.96 because it is data,
not model. A correct retrain is running.

**Second, and more usefully: the composer's gain was a constant tuned to one checkpoint.** Two runs
of the *same* training command give measurably different embedding tables — ideal-words R² **0.816
against 0.746** — and the hardcoded background gain of 2.0 did not transfer: on the retrained
backbone the calibrator picks 3.0, at the top of its grid. That constant was a hidden dependency on
a specific set of weights, and it is now removed: `experiments/common.py:backbone_gains` calibrates
the per-factor gain on whatever backbone it is handed, using **seen slots only**, so no held-out
label is touched (composing a training slot and asking whether the model reproduces it is
label-free). Every experiment now takes its gain from the backbone rather than from a literal.

**Re-verified.** The correct retrain (200 epochs / 200 per slot, 105 min) restores the backbone —
conditional joint accuracy **0.891** against the undertrained retrain's 0.141, and ideal-words
**R² 0.826** against the original's 0.816. Every ColorMNIST experiment was then re-run on it, and the
claims hold:

| | original checkpoint | re-verified |
|---|---|---|
| N9 `ours` F1 | 0.57 | 0.50 |
| N9 `poe-guided` F1 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| N9 `nearest-class` F1 | 0.01 | 0.01 |
| N8 factorization R² | 0.816 | 0.826 |
| N8 factored vs inversion F1 | 0.391 / 0.426 (**inversion ahead**) | 0.57 / 0.55 (**factored ahead**) |
| N5 forgetting, ours / finetune | 0.000 / 0.134 | 0.000 / 0.089 |

The absolute numbers move by up to 0.07 F1 between two runs of the same training command — worth
stating on its own as the scale of backbone-to-backbone variation on this benchmark. The orderings
that the main claims rest on are unchanged: ours far above `poe-guided` (F1 0.00) and
`nearest-class` (0.01) in §5l, and the three-way separation in §5k between protected arms at exactly
zero forgetting and unprotected arms measurably above it.

**One ordering did flip, and it happens to flip in our favour, which is exactly why it needs saying.**
On the original checkpoint the factored composer was *behind* the entangled class basis on F1
(0.391 against 0.426); on the re-verified backbone it is ahead (0.57 against 0.55). Both gaps are
small and they point opposite ways, so the honest reading is that **factored and inversion are not
separated by these experiments** — the factored basis's real advantages are the ones that do
reproduce: it reads (d, f, b) correctly, it needs ~18 factor weights instead of 120 entangled class
weights, and it carries higher content diversity (1.11 against 1.05 in §5h). Any claim that it beats
inversion on coverage is currently unsupported.

One number *improved* on re-verification and is worth flagging rather than quietly banking: on the
restored backbone `ours` is the top method in §5k at **0.816 ± 0.034 ACC over 4 seeds**, above
parameter isolation (0.806 ± 0.030) and TTT (0.797 ± 0.041). Those three span 0.019 against standard
errors of ±0.03–0.04, so **they are tied on accuracy**, and one seed drags all three down together
(0.719 / 0.724 / 0.682). Do not read the ordering as a win. What the four seeds *do* separate
cleanly is forgetting: 0.000 for both arms that remove shared parameters, against 0.058 / 0.082 /
0.163 for EWC / finetune / replay.

## 5q. Adding a factor DIRECTION instead of an adapter (N11)

Everything above adds a concept by training a per-concept adapter. Three of our own results say that
is the wrong primitive. §5n: zeroing a concept's private embedding drops it from 0.38 to 0.04 while
the shared adapter path triples with little effect — the adapter is not carrying the composition.
§5g: N3 measures transfer at **+0.0013**, so later concepts are marginally *more* expensive; adapters
accumulate nothing reusable. §5k: `independent` ties the hierarchy (0.806 vs 0.816) with fewer
parameters. A LoRA adapter is a **private function** — concept 41 gains nothing from concepts 1–40 —
which makes it a one-off rather than a way to broaden composition.

The ideal-words structure suggests the alternative. If `c(d,f,b) ≈ μ + D_d + F_f + B_b` at R² 0.83,
a concept is a **point in a factor space**, not a function. Then adding to the manifold means adding
a *direction* to shared vocabulary: learn one vector for a new background from a handful of OOD
instances, and every (digit, foreground) combination using it becomes expressible immediately — none
of them trained, none costing parameters.

**Result** (`n11_basis_extension`, 3 seeds, factor value excluded from the basis fit, R² 0.856; one
direction taught from 4 combinations then scored on 12 that were never trained):

| arm | acc | **F1** | fg | bg | div | cost per concept |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| basis extension, **unseen** combos | 0.662 ± 0.063 | **0.755 ± 0.037** | 0.939 | 0.84 | 1.02 | **0** |
| basis extension, taught combos | 0.656 ± 0.038 | 0.753 ± 0.035 | 0.955 | 0.84 | 1.07 | 256 floats total |
| per-concept adapter | 0.628 ± 0.035 | 0.658 ± 0.017 | 0.854 | 0.98 | 1.02 | 551K params |
| *real held-out images (ceiling)* | *0.903 ± 0.017* | *0.911 ± 0.009* | *1.000* | *1.00* | *1.00* |

**Unseen combinations score the same as the taught ones** (0.755 vs 0.753) — the direction is
genuinely a factor, not a memorized set. It **beats the adapter path** (F1 0.755 vs 0.658) while one
256-float vector covers all 40 combinations using that value, against 551K *marginal* parameters per
concept for adapters. What it gives up is background fidelity (0.84 vs 0.98), the expected cost of a
smaller direction.

**Preservation stops being a mechanism and becomes a property.** The basis is read-only during
extension, so every composition that does not use the new value is bit-identical afterwards — no
freezing, no `_seal()`, no path bookkeeping. `adapters.py` exists to buy structurally what this gets
for free.

### Two failure modes, both found by measurement and both fixable

**The direction absorbs whatever the teaching set held constant.** The first run drew both teaching
combinations with the same foreground; on unseen combinations digit transferred at 0.92 and
background at 0.96 while foreground collapsed to **0.38**. Teaching combinations must *span* the
other factors. Sweeping how many are needed shows the driver is coverage, not volume — unseen-combo
F1 runs **0.37 / 0.30 / 0.65 / 0.73** for 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 teaching combinations, with the jump at 4,
exactly where all four foregrounds are first spanned.

**The learned direction must live at the basis's scale.** Left free, the optimizer returns ‖v‖
4.7–5.4 where the factor's existing directions measure **0.515** — an order of magnitude out of
scale, then multiplied by the gain on top. Across three seeds that gave F1 0.65 / 0.35 / 0.71, and
the failing seed had the largest norm. Holding ‖v‖ at the mean norm of that factor's existing
directions raises the mean to 0.755 and cuts the spread by 3× (±0.109 → ±0.037); the failing seed
goes 0.35 → 0.76.

### The decisive test: a backbone that has never seen the value

Phase A withheld the value from the *basis fit*, but the backbone had still been trained on it, so
it only established the mechanism. Phase B trains a backbone with background 3 **withheld entirely**
— 8000 images across 40 slots never shown, 90 seen classes, conditional accuracy 0.883, basis
R² 0.870 — and repeats everything. 3 seeds:

| arm | acc | **F1** | **bg** |
|---|---|---|---|
| basis extension, taught combos | 0.000 | 0.087 ± 0.020 | **0.000** |
| basis extension, unseen combos | 0.000 | 0.087 ± 0.030 | **0.000** |
| adapter distilled from the composed pool | 0.001 | 0.000 | **0.002** |
| **adapter consolidated on REAL images** | **0.833 ± 0.032** | **0.862 ± 0.004** | **0.997** |
| *real held-out images (ceiling)* | *0.906 ± 0.020* | *0.911 ± 0.009* | *1.000* |

**Conditioning cannot invent a colour the model has never generated.** Background accuracy is exactly
0.000 — not degraded, absent — while digit (0.77–0.91) and foreground (0.88–0.96) still come out
fine. The basis direction faithfully places the concept where the other factors say it should go and
the model renders everything except the one thing it was never taught.

**The adapter path fails too, and for a reason worth isolating.** It also lands at bg 0.002, *and*
wrecks digit (0.34) and foreground (0.20) on the way. The cause is a pipeline defect, not capacity:
`learn_concept_factored` distills a pool sampled from the model's **own conditional**, so when the
composer cannot express the target the adapter is faithfully distilling its own failure. Any
self-distillation loop inherits the ceiling of whatever generated the pool — the same property that
made composed-pool streams untestable for forgetting in §5m, biting in a different place.

**Consolidating the same concepts from REAL images works**: 0.833 accuracy at bg 0.997, against a
real-data ceiling of 0.906. So the model has the capacity; only real data can deliver the new value
into it.

### What this settles, and the architecture it implies

The two regimes are cleanly separated by measurement, not by argument:

| what is being added | method that works | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| a new **combination** of known factor values | **basis extension** | F1 0.755 ± 0.037 vs 0.658 ± 0.017 for per-concept adapters, at **zero** parameters per concept (one 256-float direction covers all 40 combinations using that value) |
| a new **value** of a factor | **weights, driven by real instances** | 0.833 accuracy vs **0.000** for any conditioning-only route |

That is a routing rule the system can apply itself: if the target decomposes into values already in
the basis, compose it for free and add nothing; if it needs a value the basis lacks, spend
parameters, and **make sure the pool is real data rather than the model's own samples**. It also
answers the original objection directly — LoRA is a one-off because it is a private function, and
the combinatorial case, which is the overwhelming majority of a lifelong stream over a factored
domain, does not need one at all.

The honest limit is unchanged and now quantified: basis extension covers new *values of existing
factors* only. A genuinely new factor, or a concept with no factored description, still has no home
in this scheme, and §5o's CelebA result means none of this is yet tested where the factors overlap
spatially.

## 5r. Can the weights be reconsolidated instead of accumulated? (N12)

An adapter per concept is storage that grows forever, and the natural objection is that a lifelong
learner should fold new knowledge into the weights it already has. This tests that directly, on the
N5 protocol (12 tasks of real held-out images) so the numbers sit beside §5k.

Folding itself is not the problem. `tests/test_merge.py` shows a LoRA delta merges into the base
weights exactly — relative error **1.1e-06**, 1102K adapter parameters reclaimed to zero — for the
concept just learned. The problem is what happens next.

| arm | ACC | forgetting | **carried params** |
|---|---|---|---|
| `adapter-keep` (frozen adapter per concept) | **0.837 ± 0.012** | **0.000** | 7.16M |
| `merge+rehearse` | 0.380 ± 0.050 | 0.123 ± 0.035 | **0.00M** |
| `merge+project` | 0.104 ± 0.011 | 0.165 ± 0.003 | **0.00M** |
| `merge` (plain fold) | 0.073 ± 0.006 | 0.229 ± 0.005 | **0.00M** |
| `finetune-direct` (no adapter at all) | 0.068 ± 0.003 | 0.227 ± 0.009 | **0.00M** |

**Why the plain fold collapses, and it is not ordinary forgetting.** A LoRA delta is *conditional by
construction* — it is only ever applied while its concept's path is active, so it is never asked what
it does to anything else. Merging makes it **unconditionally on**, a job it was not trained for.
After twelve successive folds the base carries twelve always-on deltas and even the most recent
concept fails to render (ACC 0.073, and `retention_exact` 0.08 = 1 of 12).

**Three fixes, in increasing order of how much they help.**

*Null-space projection* — constrain the update's input-side factor to the complement of the
activations that known conditionings produce, so the write lands where nothing already known reads.
The subspace is **sampled from the model**, not stored, which the factor basis makes free. On an
isolated pair this cut drift from 1.086 to **0.237** (`tests/diag_merge_nullspace.py`), and the
estimate matters: computing the subspace on Gaussian noise instead of the noised-image distribution
the model actually sees roughly halves the protection. On the full stream it is not enough (0.104).

*Rehearsal against the frozen base* — at every step, also show the delta known compositions under
their own conditionings and penalize changing what the model predicted for them before this concept
began. No teacher copy is needed: deactivating the path *is* the reference. This is the one that
works, and it exposed a real hole on the way — protecting only the base classes while leaving the
*already-absorbed concepts* undefended gave ACC 0.094; adding their embeddings to the rehearsal set
took the same configuration to **0.339**, and tuning the weight to 5 reached 0.479 at a single seed.
The weight is a clean stability–plasticity curve: ACC / forgetting of 0.339 / 0.244 at w=1,
**0.479 / 0.085** at w=5, 0.375 / 0.068 at w=15, 0.245 / 0.097 at w=50.

*Full-rank direct fine-tuning* is the worst option (0.068), which is itself informative: N5's
`finetune` reaches 0.725 while updating a **low-rank** delta on a shared node. The low-rank
constraint, not the freezing, is doing much of the protective work there.

### The verdict, stated as a cost rather than a preference

Reconsolidation is **possible but expensive**: 0.380 ± 0.050 against 0.837 ± 0.012, i.e. **45% of the
frozen-adapter accuracy**, in exchange for carrying nothing at all. Nothing tested here closes that
gap, and the ordering was stable across all three seeds.

What the experiment actually establishes is *why* the adapter earns its place, which is a better
reason to keep it than inertia: **conditionality is what makes exact preservation possible.** A
frozen adapter is not merely stored knowledge, it is knowledge with a guard on it — the delta cannot
affect a conditioning it was not activated for. Every zero-growth arm here works by trying to
reconstruct that guarantee statistically (project the update, or rehearse against the old behaviour)
and every one of them lands well short of a structural guarantee that costs nothing to enforce.

The honest caveat: this searched three mechanisms, not the space. Rehearsal was still improving
sharply when tuned (0.094 → 0.339 → 0.479 from two changes), so the gap is a measured result for
*these* methods, not a proof that folding cannot be made to work.

## 5s. Does the Cobweb hierarchy earn its keep? (N14) — no, and it was given its best shot

The hierarchy is the part of this repo with the least evidence behind it: §5g measures transfer at
**+0.0013** (later concepts marginally *more* expensive, not less) and §5k has `independent` — one
private adapter per concept, nothing shared — tying it. The obvious defence is that N3 and N5 stream
concepts in random order over unrelated compositions, which is the condition least favourable to
sharing. N14 removes that defence by handing the tree the case it was designed for: concepts arrive
in **families sharing two of three factors, families back to back**, so each new concept lands under
a populated ancestor.

3 seeds, 5 families × 4 concepts:

| arm | acc | transfer (first-epoch) | transfer (final) | shared nodes | params |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `clustered` (families back to back) | 0.831 ± 0.005 | **+0.0058** | +0.0006 | 4.0 | 11.57M |
| `shuffled` (same concepts, random order) | **0.859 ± 0.008** | +0.0038 | +0.0003 | 4.0 | 11.57M |
| `flat` (no hierarchy, full isolation) | 0.842 ± 0.007 | +0.0040 | +0.0005 | 1.0 | 11.57M |

Transfer is late-minus-early; **negative** would mean later concepts were cheaper, i.e. an ancestor
gave them something. Every arm is positive, and **`clustered` is the worst on both accuracy and
transfer** — the exact opposite of the hierarchy's premise, in the exact case built to favour it.

This is not a null result from a broken mechanism. The tree genuinely forms (mean depth 3.2 against
flat's 1.0) and genuinely shares (4 shared ancestors against flat's 1). It shares, and sharing does
not help. Parameter counts are identical across all three arms, because the node count is driven by
the concept count either way — so the hierarchy is not even buying compression.

**Conclusion: the Cobweb-style hierarchical placement should come out of the method.** What survives
from the Cobweb side of the project is the *insert-only* discipline — new structure never rewrites
old structure — which is what §5k's exact preservation actually rests on, and which flat per-concept
adapters provide just as well.

## 5t. The full stream: rare vocabulary, free combinations (N13)

§5q established the routing rule on one held-out value. N13 runs it as a **stream of four** — a
backbone that never saw background 3, foreground 3, digit 8 or digit 9 (56 seen classes, conditional
accuracy 0.953). Each value arrives, is absorbed, and must survive everything after it.

A first version scored identically to the `basis-only` control, which turned out to be a real design
error rather than noise: it absorbed each value into a **per-concept adapter**, and an adapter is
only active while its own path is, whereas composing through the basis activates no path at all. New
vocabulary has to land in the **always-on** weights or the composition step cannot see it. Corrected
(absorb → merge into the base with rehearsal → fit a direction → compose for free):

| value (in arrival order) | `ours` acc | `basis-only` acc | carried params |
|---|---|---|---|
| bg=3 (first absorbed) | 0.01 | 0.00 | **0.00M** |
| fg=3 | 0.00 | 0.00 | **0.00M** |
| digit=8 | 0.17 | 0.14 | **0.00M** |
| digit=9 (last absorbed) | **0.52** | 0.08 | **0.00M** |
| *`adapter-per-combo` (separate run)* | *0.62–0.85* | — | *19–23M* |

Merging plainly works — the most recently absorbed value reaches 0.52 against the control's 0.08 on
combinations nobody trained, at zero carried parameters. But the column reads as a **forgetting
gradient**: 0.52 for the last value, 0.17 for the second-to-last, 0.00–0.01 for the first two. Each
merge damages the vocabulary already folded in, which is §5r's measured cost (0.380 vs 0.837) showing
up as a curve instead of a single number.

So the honest position on the zero-parameter architecture is that it holds for **recent** vocabulary
and decays for old, while the adapter-per-combination arm retains everything (0.62–0.85) at 19–23M
parameters and no free combinations. The two ends of the trade are now both measured, and nothing
tested here occupies the middle.

## 5u. Does any of it transfer to a real pretrained model? (N15) — not yet, and the blocker is identified

Everything above runs on a 6.8M-parameter UNet we trained ourselves on 32×32 ColorMNIST. N15 moves
to **`google/ddpm-celebahq-256`**: 113.7M parameters, 256×256, trained by someone else, on the
paper's own benchmark domain, with attributes that overlap spatially.

Two things had to be built first, and both are findings rather than setup:

1. **The model is unconditional** — no `class_embed_type`, no `num_class_embeds`, no
   cross-attention, and `class_embedding` is None. There is nothing for `emb_override` to override
   and no embedding table to factorize. A conditioning slot is **added** as a vector on the time
   embedding (`neural/pretrained.py`), which is where our own UNet accepts one. Pretrained weights
   are untouched and a zero vector reproduces the model exactly (verified at **0.000e+00**).
2. **The basis cannot be read, so it is built** — one direction per attribute value, learned from
   real images through the diffusion loss (`learn_attribute_basis`).

Getting the sampler right was its own correction worth recording: walking this repo's cosine u-grid
and mapping each u to the nearest trained timestep produced faces with a systematic **green cast**
(channel means R 0.338 / G 0.365 / B 0.177 against the diffusers reference's 0.531 / 0.477 / 0.232).
A pretrained model has to be integrated on the schedule it was trained on; on its native timestep
grid the cast disappears (R 0.345 / G 0.344 / B 0.227) and the samples are clean 256×256 faces.

6 attribute combinations × 8 samples, scoring the conjunction over the attributes actually composed:

| arm | **target joint** | target per-attribute |
|---|---|---|
| *real CelebA images (ceiling)* | *0.417* | *0.778* |
| `per-combo` (a direction for that exact combination) | 0.167 | 0.542 |
| `composed` (sum of per-attribute directions) | 0.146 | 0.486 |
| `uncond` (pretrained model untouched) | 0.146 | 0.528 |

**`composed` equals `uncond` exactly (0.146), so the composition is doing nothing.** But the
diagnostic that matters is the row above it: **`per-combo` — fitting a single combination directly,
with no composition involved — barely beats unconditional either (0.167 vs 0.146).** The bottleneck
is therefore *not* the compositional step. It is the conditioning channel: an additive vector on the
512-dimensional time embedding of a 113.7M-parameter model, given 400 optimization steps on 64
images, does not move the model appreciably.

That is an honest negative and a narrow one. It says our method has **not** been shown to transfer,
and it says the next thing to fix is the conditioning channel rather than the composer — more
capacity (a small learned projection into the block-level conditioning rather than a single additive
vector), and an optimization budget appropriate to a model 17× larger than ours. What it does not
say is that the approach fails on real models, because nothing here got as far as testing the
approach: on this budget even the trivial baseline barely conditions.

## 5v. Three alternatives to a per-concept LoRA, on the goal's own loop (N16)

What the adapter is doing is narrower than it looks. The composition is read in **embedding space**
(`compose_factored`) and the manifold generates from it; the adapter only fits the residual between
what the model produces under that embedding and what the target actually looks like — §5n measured
exactly this. So the adapter is a *local density correction*, not the compositional mechanism, which
is what makes it worth asking whether something else can play that role.

Each arm below replaces the delta and nothing else. Composition, sampling and consolidation are
untouched, so differences are attributable to the parameterization. 6 OOD compositions, 3 seeds:

| arm | acc | **F1** | div | params |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `lora` (current method) | **0.363 ± 0.028** | **0.469 ± 0.029** | 1.16 | 3.86M |
| **`ortho`** (Cayley rotation) | **0.361 ± 0.011** | 0.440 ± 0.028 | 1.20 | **1.28M** |
| `hypernet` (delta generated from the embedding) | 0.072 ± 0.006 | 0.045 ± 0.023 | 1.50 | 0.89M |
| `guidance-pool` (guided samples, then consolidated) | 0.002 ± 0.002 | 0.056 ± 0.017 | 1.61 | 4.23M |
| `guidance-only` (no manifold update at all) | 0.000 | 0.004 ± 0.004 | 1.59 | 0.38M |

**Orthogonal deltas are the result worth keeping.** A Cayley-parameterized rotation matches LoRA on
accuracy (0.361 vs 0.363, well inside the seed spread) at **one third of the parameters**
(1.28M vs 3.86M), with slightly better content diversity and slightly worse coverage. Because a
rotation cannot change the norm of what it acts on, it is also the natural candidate for the merging
problem in §5r — that combination is untested and is the obvious next experiment.

**The hypernetwork fails, and it was the one I expected to work.** If the embedding carries the
semantics (§5n), generating the correction from it should give zero growth *and* conditionality —
the two properties §5r identified as the ones that matter. It does not: 0.072 accuracy against LoRA's
0.363. The parameterization here is FiLM-style channel modulation driven by a shared trunk, so two
explanations are live and this experiment does not separate them — a shared trunk cannot hold
per-concept corrections without interference, or channel-wise scaling is simply too weak a delta
regardless of how it is produced. Testing a generated *low-rank* delta rather than a generated
channel scale would separate them.

**Classifier guidance controls the digit weakly and the colours not at all.** The composition here is
literally a sum of attribute log-probability gradients, which is the most direct statement of the
compositional hypothesis anywhere in this repo, and it costs parameters per *attribute* rather than
per concept (0.38M, fixed). Sweeping the guidance scale:

| scale | 0 (off) | 1 | 3 | 10 | 30 | 80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| digit accuracy | 0.09 | 0.20 | **0.26** | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| joint accuracy | 0.01 | 0.01 | 0.01 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |

It peaks at scale 3 and collapses above it, and foreground/background never come under control at
any scale. Against the embedding-conditioning route (digit 0.56–0.83) this is not competitive.

**A correction to my own diagnosis, since it nearly became a false finding.** A first version of the
guided sampler was a hand-rolled DDIM loop, and it scored at chance *with guidance switched off*. I
read that as the sampler being broken. It was not: at scale 0 the model is sampling
**unconditionally**, and unconditional samples scored against one specific target must score at
chance — digit 0.09, foreground 0.28, background 0.28 is exactly right for 10/4/4 classes. The loop
was fine and the control was behaving correctly. `sample_guided` now delegates to the same
`BaseScoreField.sample` every other experiment uses, which is what it should have done from the
start.

**One negative on a hypothesis of mine, recorded because I proposed it.** Reading the code I noticed
`learn_concept_factored` trained the concept embedding during consolidation and then overwrote it
with `c_new` — so the adapter was fit against a conditioning that no longer existed at sampling time.
Pinning the embedding to `c_new` before consolidating and freezing it there is cleaner and removes a
real inconsistency, but it did not help: on matched configurations N9's `ours` goes 0.41 / F1 0.50
before to 0.36 / 0.44 after, one seed each. That is within noise on 5 cases and certainly not the
improvement I predicted. The fix is kept because it removes the inconsistency, not because it earns
anything measurable.

## 5w. Orthogonal deltas, merged (N17) — the merge route stays closed, but the delta gets 2.8x smaller

§5v left one combination untested and I predicted it would work. Merging a LoRA delta destroys the
model because a concept-specific transform becomes unconditional (§5r); an orthogonal delta cannot
change the norm of what it acts on, and norm distortion looked like the destructive part. Rotations
also compose into rotations, so repeated folds stay inside the constraint set. `test_ortho_merge.py`
confirms the fold is exact (relative error **1.6e-06**, rotations orthogonal to 6.6e-07).

Same protocol as §5k and §5r — 12 tasks of real held-out images, 3 seeds:

| arm | ACC | forgetting | **carried params** |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lora-keep` | **0.837 ± 0.012** | **0.000** | 7.16M |
| **`ortho-keep`** | 0.773 ± 0.048 | **0.000** | **2.56M** |
| `lora-merge+rehearse` | 0.378 ± 0.051 | 0.125 ± 0.037 | 0.00M |
| `ortho-merge+rehearse` | 0.082 ± 0.010 | 0.100 ± 0.016 | 0.00M |
| `ortho-merge` | 0.069 ± 0.005 | 0.225 ± 0.013 | 0.00M |

**The prediction was wrong.** Orthogonal merging is not better than low-rank merging, it is *far
worse* — 0.082 against 0.378 with the same rehearsal. Norm preservation does not rescue the fold,
which means the destructive part of merging was never norm distortion; it is the loss of
conditionality, exactly as §5r argued and I then talked myself past.

The failure has a coherent shape worth keeping. `ortho-merge+rehearse` forgets *less* than
`lora-merge+rehearse` (0.100 vs 0.125) while scoring far lower — a rotation is a restricted
transform, and once it is also required to be a no-op on every known conditioning there is almost no
freedom left to fit the new concept. It is stable because it barely moves.

**What does survive is worth having.** As a *kept* adapter, orthogonal deltas give
**0.773 ± 0.048 at 2.56M against LoRA's 0.837 ± 0.012 at 7.16M** — 2.8× fewer carried parameters,
still exactly zero forgetting and bit-identical retention, for −0.064 ACC that is within about 1.3
standard errors. On a long stream, where carried parameters are the binding constraint, that is a
better operating point than the accuracy difference suggests, and it composes with everything else:
§5q's basis extension still makes new *combinations* free, so the adapter is only ever paid for new
vocabulary.

### The state of the zero-growth question, after four attempts

| route | best ACC | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| frozen low-rank adapter (current) | 0.837 | exact preservation, unbounded growth |
| frozen orthogonal adapter | 0.773 | exact preservation, **2.8× less growth** |
| merged low-rank + rehearsal | 0.378 | zero growth, forgets |
| merged orthogonal + rehearsal | 0.082 | zero growth, barely learns |
| hypernetwork (generated delta) | 0.072 (composition task) | zero growth, does not fit |

Four mechanisms have now been tried for "absorb into existing weights and carry nothing", and every
one lands far below keeping a conditional delta. The consistent explanation across all of them is
that **conditionality, not size, is what buys preservation** — a delta that can be switched off for
concepts it was not trained for cannot damage them, and every attempt to remove that switch has had
to reconstruct it statistically and has fallen short. That is now a result supported by four
independent failures rather than an argument.

## 6. Why we are *not* replicating the paper's diffusion results

Side by side on ColorMNIST — their F1 (harmonic mean of precision and recall in Inception feature
space, faithfulness split) against our joint composition accuracy:

| method | paper F1 | our joint (analytic / neural) | agree? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top-1 | 6.2 | 0.000 / 0.000 | ✅ both fail |
| Top-3 | 5.1 | 0.054 / 0.000 | ✅ both fail |
| PoE | **75.3** | 0.603 / 0.674 | ✅ both work |
| **Query-only** | **0.0** | **0.749 / 0.833** | ❌ **inverted** |

One row is inverted, and it is the row that decides the ranking. Here is what is actually causing it,
ordered by how much of the gap I think each explains.

### 6.1 Our headline metric has no coverage term — so it cannot punish memorization

This is the core reason.

The paper's F1 is the harmonic mean of **precision and recall** computed with a k-NN density
estimator in Inception feature space. Recall asks *does the generated distribution cover the real
class?* A method that emits near-copies of one image has terrible recall and is scored ~0 no matter
how correct that one image is — their query-only records precision 0.5% and recall 2.5%.

Our `joint` asks a different question: *for each sample independently, are the three attributes
right?* A perfect copy of `x_q` scores **1.0**, because `x_q` is a real image of the target class.
Our metric is a per-sample classifier, not a distributional comparison, and there is no term in it
that a memorizing method can lose points on.

We *do* measure coverage — and it shows exactly what the paper says. Analytic query_only: diversity
**0.094** against a reference spread of **0.152**, novelty **0.075**. Those samples under-cover the
class and sit on top of the query. But that lives in a side column instead of in the headline number,
so the ranking flips.

**In other words: the paper's query-only and ours are both memorizing. Theirs is scored on a metric
that notices; ours is not.**

### 6.2 Our query-only baseline is mis-parameterized to be much stronger than theirs

The paper specifies "a fixed **small** isotropic variance … around the query image". We use
`var = 0.01`, i.e. **σ = 0.1**. Two independent draws from that teacher differ by
`√2·σ = 0.141` in per-pixel RMSE, against a measured real intra-class spread of **0.152** — so our
"small" isotropic spread is **93% of the actual class spread**. It is not small in any sense; it is
calibrated almost exactly to the class it is supposed to fail at modelling.

That single choice hands the baseline enough variance to emit plausible in-class variation instead of
the degenerate blob the paper describes. It is a one-line change
(`compose.query_only_teacher(xq, var=…)`) and should be swept, not guessed.

### 6.3 Our sampler differs from their Eq. 13/14 in ways that specifically help a query-centred teacher

- **Analytic track initializes from a noised teacher draw** (SDEdit-style, `u_start` 0.4–0.7) rather
  than from pure noise. For query-only the teacher is `N(x_q, σ²I)`, so this is *literally SDEdit
  applied to a real held-out image of the target class* — it is close to guaranteed to return a valid
  member of that class. The paper samples from pure noise.
- **We replaced their variance-aware schedule with an annealed one.** Their Eq. 14 sets
  `w ∝ tr(Σ_t)`, which for a tight query-centred Gaussian makes guidance *vanish* as `Σ_t → σ²` at
  low noise. Ours holds `w ≥ 0.8` and rising, pinning the sample to `x_q` while the model keeps it
  on-manifold.

The paper reports its query-only producing "salt and pepper texture". Ours produces clean near-copies.
The baselines are behaving *differently*, not merely scoring differently — which points at the
sampler, not the metric alone. (I can describe what our sampler does; I cannot verify the precise
mechanism of their failure without running their code.)

### 6.4 We never implemented their metrics at all

No FID, no CLIP similarity, no precision/recall, no two-reference-set faithfulness/generalization
split. **Strictly, this repository has not attempted to reproduce their tables** — it built a cheaper
instrument that answers "is the composition right?" and left "does the sample distribution match the
class distribution?" unmeasured. The paper's entire faithfulness-vs-generalization story lives in the
second question.

### 6.5 Scale

| | paper | ours |
|---|---|---|
| backbone | `google/ddpm-cifar10-32` (~35M params) | 6.8M params |
| training data | 1 000 images/slot (120k) | 200/slot neural, 40/slot analytic |
| ascent starts per timestep | 128 | 16 |
| ascent iterations | 150 | 120 |
| samples per query | 256 pool / 16 saved | 24–32 |
| FID caveat | N=100, already noted as biased | n/a |

Our digit accuracy tops out at 0.82 *on real images* — the probes themselves are the binding
constraint on part of the table.

### 6.6 What would settle it

In priority order:

1. **Implement precision/recall/F1** with a k-NN density estimator in some feature space (Inception,
   or even the probe penultimate layer), against both reference sets (the queries, and other held-out
   images of the class). This is the single change most likely to reproduce the paper's ordering,
   because it is the one that penalizes memorization. Predicted outcome: query-only's F1 collapses
   while PoE's holds, since our own diversity numbers already show query-only under-covering by 40%.
2. **Fix the query-only variance** to something genuinely small (sweep σ ∈ {0.01, 0.03, 0.1}).
3. **Run PoE and the baselines under the paper's own protocol** — pure-noise start, Eq. 14 schedule —
   as a separate arm, so protocol changes are attributable.
4. Scale the backbone and the discovery budget toward the paper's.

Until at least (1) and (2) are done, **the honest statement is that our E2/N2 tables do not test the
same thing the paper's Table 1 tests**, and the inverted row should not be read as a contradiction of
their result.

## 6b. Can it learn an unbounded amount of new content?

"Infinite" cannot be run, so `tests/test_unbounded.py` tests the **invariants that would make it
possible**: that the cost of adding concept K+1 and its effect on concepts 1…K are both independent
of K. Verified at K = 40, added in batches of 1, 2, 3, 5 and 8:

- **Preservation is K-independent.** After every batch, *every* concept ever consolidated is still
  bit-identical to its fingerprint at birth — checked against all previous concepts at every one of
  the 12 batches, so the test gets stricter as it runs.
- **The frozen base never moves**, at any K.
- **Cost per concept is constant** — parameter growth is linear with max residual 1.3%, so the
  budget question has an exact answer: at rank 2 on the test model, **45K params/concept**, i.e.
  ~5.6K concepts per GB of fp32 adapter, ~45K concepts per 8 GB.
- **Batch placement is order-independent** — the same K concepts shuffled produce the same structure.

The honest limit: growth is **linear, not sublinear**. At the rank-8 setting used in N3–N5 each
concept costs ~0.55M parameters, so 40 concepts already exceed the 6.8M-parameter base. Unbounded in
principle, expensive in practice — see `RESULTS.md` for the compression directions (lower rank for
leaves, adapting a subset of layers, and merging sibling adapters during an offline consolidation
pass with a fingerprint check to keep the guarantee auditable).

## 7. Threats to validity

- **Single seed throughout.** `--seed` is plumbed everywhere and every run writes a seed-tagged
  result file; `experiments/aggregate.py` pools them. Nothing here has error bars. N3's structural
  result (range 0.000 vs −0.917) will not move; every accuracy number will.
- **Probe-based metrics**, with the ceiling (0.775 joint) reported beside every table. No FID/CLIP.
- **Reconstructed palette** — the paper names its colours but gives no RGB values. Absolute numbers
  are not comparable to theirs; only within-table comparisons are.
- **Two documented deviations from the paper**, both with the measurement that motivated them at the
  implementation site: adaptive PoE temperature (`compose.poe_teacher`) and annealed guidance
  (`compose.cfg_weight`).
- **Per-slot variance is large and systematic**, not noise — slots on the `(cyan, deep_red)` pair
  fail far more than others on both backbones, and two slots score 0.00 for *every* method including
  on real images (probe failures). Means alone mislead; the per-slot rows are in
  `results/e2_composition.json`.
- **E4 (composition closure) is unresolved by design.** ColorMNIST's attributes are spatially
  separable — background pixels depend only on `bg`, stroke pixels only on `fg` — so a per-dimension
  PoE can always assemble an unseen pair from base concepts, and having the combination pre-learned
  buys nothing. Closure cannot be demonstrated on this benchmark; see RESULTS 5y.

## 5x. Choosing the router's signal by measuring, not by reasoning

The routing rule of 5q -- new COMBINATION is free, new VOCABULARY needs real data -- has always been
applied by me, not by the system. N18 closes that, and the first thing it needed was a signal. The
first candidate was wrong in an instructive way, so both are recorded.

**The reasoning that failed.** Score the query by the best epsilon-prediction loss over every trained
class conditioning, then divide by the loss under NO conditioning, so that per-image denoising
difficulty cancels. Measured over 110 queries on the `nobg3` backbone
(`tests/diag_router_signals.py`), that ratio scores AUROC **0.199** for "is this novel" -- not weak,
**reversed**.

The cause is that the normaliser is not neutral. On a query carrying a factor value the backbone
never saw, the unconditional branch is ALSO bad at it, and it degrades by MORE than the conditional
numerator does, so the ratio falls exactly where it was supposed to rise. *A ratio to a quantity that
itself responds to novelty cannot measure novelty.*

**What works is the raw quantity.** The absolute denoising error under the best available
conditioning is monotone in novelty, and one signal at two noise levels decides all three routes:

| noise | in-distribution | new-combination | new-vocabulary |
|---|---|---|---|
| u = 0.20 | 0.0147 | 0.0256 | 0.0666 |
| u = 0.45 | 0.0078 | 0.0103 | 0.0255 |

| decision | signal | AUROC |
|---|---|---|
| novel vs in-distribution | best conditional error, u = 0.10 | **0.985** |
| new-vocabulary vs new-combination | best conditional error, u = 0.45 | **0.995** |

Low noise reads fine detail (the exact colour), mid noise reads global structure, and the two
decisions want different ones -- which is why the two thresholds sit at different `u` rather than
being two quantiles of one score.

**The negative result that must travel with it.** The *unconditional* error -- using no class, no
basis and no conditioning at all -- scores **0.985** and **0.998** on the same two decisions. So the
conditioning is not what makes the decision, and the router is an out-of-distribution detector on the
model's own density. It is a working detector and it closes the loop, but it is **not** evidence that
the factor basis is what recognises a new combination, and N18 runs it as an explicit ablation row so
the two cannot be conflated. Any future claim of the form "the basis knows what it can express" needs
a signal that beats this ablation, and none of the ten candidates measured here does.

**A calibration honesty note.** `t_novel` is a quantile of the signal over TRAINED slots, which is
label-free. `t_vocab` cannot be, because it separates two categories that are both absent from
training. It is calibrated instead against the model's own samples from basis combinations it was
never trained on -- the closest thing to a new-combination example the system can manufacture unaided
-- and that substitution is an assumption, stated at the call site and swept in N18 rather than
asserted.


## 5y. Closure by COST: does an accumulated bank make the next concept cheaper? (no)

E4 asked whether a consolidated concept gets *reused* by later compositions and measured
`adapter_expert_frac = 0.00`. RESULTS 5y argued the benchmark was at fault rather than the mechanism --
ColorMNIST's attributes are spatially separable, so a per-dimension PoE can always assemble an unseen
(fg, bg) pair from base concepts and having the combination pre-learned in one expert buys nothing --
and recommended testing closure by **cost** instead: does consolidating concept `k` get cheaper when
relatives are already in the bank? N3 already records the quantity, so this needs no new run.

Distillation loss at the first epoch, early concepts vs late ones, 3 seeds:

| arm | early_first | late_first | late - early | early_final | late_final |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `hier` (frozen ancestors) | 0.0234 | 0.0264 | **+0.0030 +/- 0.0032** | 0.0222 | 0.0203 |
| `unfrozen` | 0.0236 | 0.0266 | +0.0030 +/- 0.0034 | 0.0221 | 0.0203 |

**Two readings, and the second is the one that settles it.** Late concepts start *more* expensive, not
less -- the wrong sign for closure -- but the effect is within one seed standard deviation of zero, so
on its own it says nothing. The decisive comparison is `hier` against `unfrozen`: they differ by
**-0.00018 on late_first against a seed spread of 0.0053**, a factor of thirty smaller than the noise.
Whatever makes later concepts slightly costlier is a property of *which slots arrive late*, not of the
bank that has accumulated behind them.

So closure does not show up in accuracy (E4) and does not show up in cost either. Together with N14
(hierarchical placement loses to flat) and N3's transfer of +0.0013, three independent measurements
now say the same thing: **on ColorMNIST, consolidated concepts do not make later learning easier.**
The most likely reason remains the one RESULTS 5y gave -- separable factors make a pre-learned
combination redundant -- which is a hypothesis about the benchmark and is exactly what CelebA is for.
It is stated as an open question rather than a refutation of closure in general, because the
experiment that could refute it has not been run on a benchmark where closure could pay.


## 5z. The growth accounting: cost is linear in VOCABULARY, flat in experience (N24)

Every efficiency number so far has been per concept -- a basis direction is 256 floats where an
adapter is 551K (5q), a kept orthogonal delta is 2.8x smaller than a kept LoRA (5w). The claim that
matters for *lifelong* learning is about the curve: does running forever cost more the longer you
run, or only more the more genuinely new vocabulary the world contains?

`experiments/n24_growth.py` computes it. Per-concept costs are **read from the measured runs**
(`n17_ortho_merge.json`, `n11_basis_extension*.json`) rather than restated, and the stream is each
benchmark's own combinatorial structure -- 160 (digit, fg, bg) slots over 18 factor values for
ColorMNIST, 419 realized attribute combinations over 28 attribute values for CelebA, arriving in
random order over 20 seeds. The only modelling step is the routing rule of 5q, for which N18
measures an actual detector.

| benchmark | experiences | vocab items | vocabulary complete after | per-query LoRA | ours, oracle routing | ours, **measured** routing | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ColorMNIST | 160 | 18 | experience **27** (83% of the stream then free) | 95.5M | 3.8M | **10.1M** | **9.5x** |
| CelebA | 419 | 28 | experience **17** (96% of the stream then free) | 250.2M | 6.0M | **23.6M** | **10.6x** |

**The measured-routing column is the one to quote.** The oracle column assumes the router is perfect;
the measured column charges N18's own error rates back in, and the honest ratio is therefore ~10x
rather than the 25-42x oracle routing would suggest. The dominant charge is the router's 20%
over-spend on new combinations (0.200 +/- 0.058), each of which buys an adapter it did not need. The
other error direction contributes nothing because N18 measured it at exactly **0.000** across 3 seeds
and 120 queries: a new vocabulary item was never once sent down the free route.

**The shape is the result, not the ratio.** Under oracle routing the curve is *flat* once vocabulary
is complete -- every later experience is a new combination of values already held and costs nothing.
Under measured routing it rises with a slope proportional to the misroute rate, so it is not flat,
but it is bounded by a rate the detector controls rather than by the arrival of experiences. The
per-query curve -- the source paper's consolidation step, kept rather than discarded -- rises at full
cost forever. The ratio above is an artefact of where the stream was cut; what does not depend on
that choice is that one curve's slope is set by a *detector error rate* and the other's by *time*.

Note that the ratio *grows* with the richness of the combination space: CelebA has more compositions
per vocabulary item than ColorMNIST, and is correspondingly cheaper to run. Combinatorial domains are
the favourable case for this method, which is the right direction for the argument to point.

**Two things this accounting still does not include.** It counts parameters, not compute: a "free"
combination still costs a composition and a sampling pass. And it charges every vocabulary item a
full weight update, which is what 5q's phase B requires -- conditioning alone on a never-seen value
scores exactly 0.000 -- so the 256-float basis direction is *not* claimed as sufficient for new
vocabulary, only for new combinations of vocabulary already learned.


## 6c. The router: the system decides for itself which update to make (N18, 3 seeds)

This is the piece that turns a set of certified mechanisms into a lifelong *learner*. 5q established
what to do with each kind of novelty and 5w established what preserves what, but in every experiment
before this one **I** supplied the category of the incoming query. The source paper names precisely
this as open work -- "A full mechanism for **detecting**, consolidating, and reusing genuinely new
primitives remains an important direction for future work" -- and consolidating and reusing were the
parts already done.

One backbone supplies all three routes: `ddpm_colormnist_nobg3` withheld background value 3 entirely
*and* the four held-out (fg, bg) pairs, so trained slots, held-out combinations of trained values,
and slots carrying a never-seen value are all available against the same weights. 40 queries per
route, thresholds calibrated without any held-out label (5x).

| decision | AUROC | unconditional ablation |
|---|---|---|
| novel vs in-distribution | **0.980 +/- 0.002** | 0.981 +/- 0.002 |
| new-vocabulary vs new-combination | **0.995 +/- 0.002** | 0.998 +/- 0.001 |

Self-routing accuracy **0.858 +/- 0.018** at the calibrated operating point. The confusion matrix is
the same shape on all three seeds (seed 0 shown):

| truth \ routed to | in-distribution | new-combination | new-vocabulary |
|---|---|---|---|
| in-distribution | 34 | 6 | 0 |
| new-combination | 3 | 17 | 10 |
| new-vocabulary | 0 | 0 | **40** |

**The asymmetry is the result, and it is the one a lifelong learner wants.** The two mistakes are not
equivalent. Sending a new *vocabulary* item down the free route produces a WRONG image -- the free
route scores **0.000** on those queries against 0.621 where it is correct -- and that mistake happens
at a rate of **0.000 +/- 0.000**: not one of 120 new-vocabulary queries across three seeds was ever
routed to the free path. Sending a new *combination* to the weight route produces a correct image at
unnecessary cost, and that happens at **0.200 +/- 0.058**. So the system never under-spends and
sometimes over-spends, which is exactly the direction an irreversible-damage-avoiding policy should
err in. 5z charges that over-spend back into the growth curve rather than reporting the oracle one.

**What this is not.** The unconditional ablation matches the conditional signal to within a
standard error on both decisions, so the router is an out-of-distribution detector on the model's own
density and NOT evidence that the factor basis recognises what it can express (5x). The detector is
sufficient to close the loop, and the mechanistic claim it might have supported is unsupported.


## 5q2. The basis-vs-adapter comparison, run matched (and why the old one was not)

The claim in 5q -- that a basis direction beats a per-concept adapter -- took its two numbers from
two different runs. The basis figure (F1 0.755) came from `n11_basis_extension_nm`, which was invoked
with `--skip-adapter` and therefore contains no adapter arm at all; the adapter figure (0.658) came
from `n11_basis_extension_k4`, which is the run *without* norm-matching. Read within the run that
contains both, the ordering was the other way round: adapter 0.658 +/- 0.017 against basis 0.569 +/-
0.155.

So the comparison was re-run properly -- `--norm-match` with the adapter arm enabled, 3 seeds, both
arms scored on the same combinations of the same backbone.

| arm | joint | digit | fg | bg | F1 | recall | cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| basis, combinations it was **taught** on | 0.667 | 0.847 | 0.955 | 0.840 | 0.753 +/- 0.050 | 0.878 | 1024 bytes total |
| basis, combinations **never taught** | **0.676** | 0.868 | 0.938 | 0.840 | **0.756 +/- 0.032** | 0.860 | — |
| per-concept adapter | 0.532 | 0.651 | 0.828 | 0.944 | 0.662 +/- 0.081 | 0.823 | **551K per concept** |
| adapter on REAL images | 0.885 | 0.889 | 1.000 | 0.997 | 0.887 +/- 0.027 | 0.898 | 551K per concept |
| *real held-out images (ceiling)* | *0.910* | *0.910* | *1.000* | *1.000* | *0.913* | *0.912* | — |

**Three things this settles.**

1. **The basis does beat the adapter, at 2153x less cost** -- F1 0.756 against 0.662, from one
   256-float direction against 551K parameters per concept, and the direction is shared by every
   combination using that value while the adapter transfers to none of them.
2. **It is a basis, not a lookup.** Combinations the direction was never taught on score *the same*
   as the ones it was (0.676 against 0.667). That equality is the whole content of the word "basis";
   a memorised mapping would fall off.
3. **Norm-matching is load-bearing, not cosmetic.** The earlier contradictory result is exactly the
   failure 5q documents: left free, the optimizer returns a direction 15-20x larger than the basis it
   is joining, and F1 swings 0.65 / 0.35 / 0.71 across seeds. Constraining the new value to the scale
   of the factor's existing values removes the swing (sd 0.032). **The unstable variant is the one
   that lost to the adapter**, and reporting it against a norm-matched basis number from a different
   run was the error.

The methodological point is worth as much as the result: a claim assembled from two runs is not a
comparison, and this one happened to be wrong in the direction that flattered the method.


## 6d. Native parity: how close is a consolidated concept to one the backbone was TRAINED on? (N23)

The project's own sentence is "adapt new instances into the manifold in a way that is similar to if
they were learned with the existing ideas". Every result so far argues that indirectly -- preservation
is bit-exact, forgetting is 0.000, accuracy beats baselines -- because the comparison the sentence
actually names has never been available: *what that slot would have looked like had it never been
held out*.

`n1_train --all-slots` supplies it. A backbone trained on all 160 ColorMNIST slots (200 epochs,
conditional accuracy 0.922, unconditional validity 1.000) is not a method and is not competing; it is
the reference. 12 held-out compositions, 3 seeds.

| arm | joint | F1 | recall | content div |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *real held-out images (data ceiling)* | *0.876 +/- 0.021* | *0.892 +/- 0.008* | *0.916 +/- 0.004* | *0.988* |
| **native** (oracle, trained on this slot) | **0.876 +/- 0.013** | **0.871 +/- 0.008** | **0.873 +/- 0.019** | 0.979 |
| **ours** (consolidated, never retrained) | 0.331 +/- 0.050 | 0.498 +/- 0.066 | 0.788 +/- 0.008 | 1.197 |
| **paper** (per-query LoRA from the PoE teacher) | 0.444 +/- 0.046 | 0.005 +/- 0.003 | 0.043 +/- 0.017 | 0.954 |

**The reference validates itself.** `native` sits at the data ceiling on joint accuracy (0.876 against
0.876), so a backbone that trained on a slot does reproduce it, and the denominator below is a real
upper bound rather than an artefact of a weak oracle.

**Parity, consolidated / native:**

| | joint | F1 | recall |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ours** | 0.379 +/- 0.060 | **0.571 +/- 0.073** | **0.903 +/- 0.024** |
| paper | 0.506 +/- 0.048 | 0.006 +/- 0.003 | 0.050 +/- 0.021 |

**The claim this supports, stated at the strength the numbers support it.** A concept consolidated
from a single out-of-distribution query, at **zero retraining and zero access to the held-out class**,
reaches **90% of native-level coverage** and **57% of native F1**. It does *not* reach native
per-sample fidelity: 38% on joint accuracy. So "as if it had been learned natively" is true of the
*extent* of the class the concept generates and false of the *precision* of each sample -- which is
the same split 5h certified from the other direction, where the conditioning span was shown able to
express one factor or the other but not both.

**And the contrast with the published consolidation is starker here than anywhere else.** Its per-query
LoRA scores *higher joint accuracy than ours* (0.444 against 0.331) and **0.6% of native F1**, because
recall 0.043 means it reproduces the query rather than the class. Measured against a backbone that
was trained on the slot, copying is visible in a way no within-method table shows it.

**Drag.** Seen-slot accuracy is 0.852 +/- 0.038 before consolidation and **0.852 +/- 0.038 after** --
identical, as it must be, since the base weights are frozen and a consolidated concept's adapter path
is inactive when sampling a base class. Read that as a consistency check on the invariant rather than
as evidence for it; the evidence is the bit-identical fingerprint test (6b). The oracle's 0.865 +/-
0.042 says retraining on all 160 slots cost it nothing on the 120 it already had, so the oracle is not
being flattered by the comparison either.


## 7a. CelebA, made trainable: factored conditioning (N19) — and two bugs found on the way

5o left CelebA measured and unusable: conditioning reached 34% of the real-data ceiling, and 2.3x the
data with 140 epochs moved it to 33%. That flatness is what made the diagnosis credible -- the
weakness was the conditioning DESIGN, not compute. A per-class embedding row over a 14-bit attribute
combination is estimated from as few as 25 images and shares nothing with the combination that
differs from it on one bit.

**The fix** (`unet.py cond_mode="factored"`): one embedding per (attribute, value), summed. Every
attribute vector is then estimated from every image carrying that value -- 478 (Bald) to 21663
(No_Beard) rather than 25 -- and training is no longer restricted to combinations with enough support
to own a row, so ~96k images are usable instead of ~5k. The conditioning head is **7168 parameters
against 308480** for a per-class table over the same data.

**Per-attribute margin over unconditional, which is what 5o measured:**

| backbone | margin |
|---|---|
| 5o: per-class embedding, 60 ep, 5k images | +0.048 |
| factored, corrected, 20 ep | +0.049 |
| factored, 60 ep, `Smiling x Big_Nose` withheld | +0.054 |
| factored, 60 ep, `Gray_Hair` withheld | +0.055 |
| **factored, 60 ep, combination holdout** | **+0.081** |

**1.7x the design 5o called "not ready to compose on"**, and matched at 20 epochs what per-class
needed 60 to reach.

### Two bugs, both found by looking at controls rather than at results

**The uint8 fast path trained in a different layout than it sampled.** This repository's flat vectors
are HWC as the loaders write them, and every consumer -- `_as_img`, the float branch of `train_ddpm`,
the sampler -- REINTERPRETS them as CHW. That is a fixed permutation, and because training and
sampling apply the same one the model simply learns in the permuted space while `rgb_img` displays
flat vectors as HWC, so images are shown correctly. The uint8 branch added to fit 96k images in RAM
transposed HWC -> NCHW instead, i.e. it "corrected" the layout: the backbone learned upright faces and
was then sampled through the CHW reinterpretation. Every CelebA number measured through the sampler
was meaningless -- margin +0.011, per-attribute controllability **0.000 on eight of eight attributes**,
and unconditional samples that were tiled mosaics rather than faces.

It was not the numbers that exposed it. They were merely bad, and "undertrained" was a plausible
story. It was the **sample grid**: controllability of exactly 0.000 on easy attributes like hair
colour is too clean to be undertraining. `tests/test_layout.py` now compares the two training
branches and the sampler's view on the same bytes -- one second on CPU, all deltas 0.000e+00 -- and
would have caught it before three GPU-hours. ColorMNIST never used that branch and is unaffected.

**The probe readability filter admitted blind probes.** `attr_accuracy` excluded attributes whose
probe scored under 0.65 plain accuracy. For a rare attribute that is the wrong criterion: Gray_Hair is
ON in 4.3% of CelebA, so a probe answering "no" every time scores 0.957. Measured on 8000 real images
(`tests/diag_probe_balance.py`), five of the twelve attributes the filter admitted cannot recognise
their own attribute --

| attribute | plain acc | TPR |
|---|---|---|
| Oval_Face | 0.712 | **0.066** |
| Brown_Hair | 0.778 | **0.171** |
| Bald | 0.977 | **0.206** |
| Gray_Hair | 0.962 | **0.321** |
| No_Beard | 0.883 | TNR **0.303** |

-- while Wavy_Hair, which the filter REJECTED at 0.533, is usable with TPR 0.908. Wrong in both
directions. `probe_usable` now requires TPR >= 0.5 and balanced accuracy >= 0.65.

**Both bugs were caught by the real-data ceiling row**, which is the reason this repository reports it
beside every method. A mismatched probe cache showed up as REAL held-out images scoring 0.557 where
the same images score 0.770; a blind probe showed up as a real-image ceiling of 0.25 on an attribute
whose probe claimed 0.96. In both cases every arm moved down together, which reads as "the method does
not work" rather than "this measurement is invalid" -- the most expensive confusion available, and one
a method-only table cannot detect at all.


## 7b. CelebA composition (N20): the separability threat, tested

Threat 7 has stood since the beginning: every composition result rested on ColorMNIST, whose digit /
foreground / background occupy DISJOINT pixels, and that disjointness is load-bearing -- a
per-dimension product of experts can assemble an unseen pairing because each pixel belongs to exactly
one factor. CelebA attributes overlap. This is the test.

Two splits, in increasing strength. Guidance is calibrated per backbone on TRAINED combinations, which
uses no held-out label (5p's lesson); it chose 4.0 and 6.0.

direct analogue of ColorMNIST's held-out `(fg, bg)` pairs, and the two attributes are adjacent facial
regions that jointly reshape the lower face, so the split is not separable.

### What this settles, and what it does not

**Composing known factor values produces a combination that was never observed, when the factors
overlap the same pixels.** On the strong split the composed vector reaches `pair` **0.903 +/- 0.094**
where retrieval from the nearest trained combination reaches **0.355 +/- 0.186** -- and retrieval
falling short is not a weak baseline but a structural fact, since every trained neighbour differs on
at least one of the two withheld attributes. Threat 7 is addressed rather than deferred.

**Scoring above the real-image ceiling is expected here.** `ours` at 0.903 against real images at
0.801 repeats what E0 recorded on ColorMNIST: a generated sample is a cleaner, more prototypical rendering
than a photograph, and a linear probe reads it more easily. It means the probe ceiling is not a hard
bound on generated data, not that composition beats reality; the informative comparison is against the
baselines, all of which are scored the same way.

**Both published baselines fail in the documented way.** `poe-guided` reaches recall 0.00 and F1 0.00
on both splits at content diversity 0.38-0.44 -- it emits near-copies. `query-only` is the sharper
demonstration: on the strong split it scores `pair` 0.777, close to the real-image ceiling, at recall
**0.018 +/- 0.009** and diversity **0.295**. It is reproducing the query, which happens to carry the right
attributes because it is an image of the target combination. Attribute accuracy alone cannot separate
composing from copying; that is why coverage is reported beside it everywhere in this document.

**Stated as a limitation:** the guidance calibration selected the TOP of its grid (6.0) on the strong
split, so the operating point sits at a boundary and may not be optimal -- the sweep should be
extended before these numbers are treated as the method's best. The grid was widened from 2.0 to 6.0
on the evidence of `diag_celeba_guidance.py`; capping it at 2.0, as the first version did, would have
understated the result substantially.

## 7d. CelebA retention (N22): is preservation still structural when factors overlap?

N5's result is the one the paper rests on, and it was measured only on ColorMNIST: arms that
structurally *remove* shared parameters forget exactly 0.000, arms that try to *protect* them forget.
Preservation here is claimed to be **structural** -- a frozen ancestor path plus a private
conditioning vector cannot be written by a later concept -- and a structural argument should be
indifferent to whether the factors overlap in pixel space. So this is a test the method can fail.

12 held-out combinations of REAL images, arriving in batches of 3.

| arm | mean-attribute ACC | forgetting | **fraction bit-identical** | adapter params |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ours** | 0.848 | **0.000** | **1.00** | 12.35M |
| **independent** (parameter isolation) | 0.830 | **0.000** | **1.00** | 9.44M |
| finetune | 0.862 | 0.012 | 0.25 | 9.44M |
| ewc | 0.864 | 0.009 | 0.25 | 9.44M |
| replay | 0.817 | 0.043 | 0.25 | 9.44M |

**The `exact` column is the result, and it separates perfectly**: 1.00 for both arms that remove
shared parameters, 0.25 for all three that try to protect them. Accuracy does not separate the arms
at all here (0.817–0.864, with `ewc` nominally highest) — which is the point worth taking from this
table. On a metric as insensitive as a 12-attribute mean, a method can look fine while having
overwritten three quarters of what it previously held. Forgetting measured as accuracy drift would
have reported 0.009 for `ewc` and called it nearly perfect; forgetting measured as *did the earlier
concept survive unchanged* reports that three of four did not.

`tgtACC`/`tgtFGT` are NaN throughout: the stricter probe filter (7a) excludes the differing attribute
for most combination-split cases, so that column has nothing to score. Mean-attribute ACC and the
bit-identical fraction are the usable metrics and are what is quoted.


## 5l-bis. The published method was being run in OUR configuration, not its own (corrected)

The case-study figure showed `poe-guided` emitting the query plus speckle. That was our fault, and the
correction changes every number in the comparison — in the baseline's favour, and in favour of the
argument.

**The mechanism.** Their Eq. 12 gives the teacher's epsilon a precision factor
`(abar*Sigma_T + (1-abar) I)^-1`, which blows up as `abar -> 1`. Their Eq. 14 exists to cancel exactly
that: `w(t) = min(w0 * tr(Sigma_t)/d, wmax)` shrinks guidance as the teacher concentrates. The
`poe-guided` arm inherited this repository's `schedule="anneal"`, which does the opposite — it rises
to `wmax` as `u -> 0`. And the justification for annealing, recorded in `cfg_weight`'s own docstring,
is that the ANALYTIC Gaussian mixture has no density at an unseen pairing; a convolutional UNet shares
weights across colours and has no such hole, so the rationale never transferred to the backbone the
baseline actually runs on.

**Two checks before accepting the diagnosis**, because a better-looking row is not an explanation:

* `tests/diag_poe_faithful.py` sweeps the sampler. With their Eq. 14 and the raw Laplace covariance
  the SAME teacher produces clean, correct digits at accuracy **0.865** against our default's 0.521.
* `tests/diag_poe_modes.py` rules out a degenerate teacher: the selected expert means are spread
  **0.229**, WIDER than the real class spread of 0.148, at `sigma_T` 0.082. Widening the discovery
  grid to higher noise moves accuracy (0.521 -> 0.635) and leaves recall at 0.010. So the coverage
  failure is not a discovery artefact either; it is the contraction 5e already identified — CFG
  toward a unimodal Gaussian pulls every trajectory to its mean.

**Corrected comparison, 3 seeds, baseline in its own configuration:**

| ColorMNIST | joint | precision | recall | F1 | content div |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *real held-out images* | *0.864* | *1.000* | *0.939* | *0.969* | *0.991* |
| ours | 0.314 | 0.400 | **0.810** | **0.498** | 1.209 |
| `poe-guided` (their Eq. 14) | **0.817** | **0.839** | **0.001** | 0.003 | 0.269 |
| `nearest-class` | 0.000 | 0.008 | 0.066 | 0.005 | 1.050 |

| CelebA, strong split | pair | precision | recall | F1 | content div |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *real held-out images* | *0.801* | *1.000* | *0.884* | *0.937* | *1.007* |
| ours | **0.903** | 0.667 | **0.579** | **0.598** | 1.068 |
| `poe-guided` (their Eq. 14) | 0.645 | **0.944** | **0.000** | 0.000 | 0.147 |

**What changed, and why it matters.** Under our schedule the baseline had precision 0.039 and looked
broken. Under its own it has precision **0.839** (ColorMNIST) and **0.944** (CelebA): its samples are
genuinely plausible members of the target class, and on ColorMNIST it beats us on accuracy *and*
precision. What it does not have is coverage — recall 0.001 and 0.000, content diversity 0.27 and
0.15 against a real class spread of ~1.0.

So the claim is not "the published method fails". It is: **the published method produces excellent
samples of one point in the class, and per-sample metrics cannot tell that apart from producing the
class.** That is a stronger statement than the one this document made before, it is made with the
baseline at its best rather than at our worst, and it is the statement the coverage metric exists to
support.

**Scope, unchanged from 6.** This is our reimplementation on our backbone under our metrics. Wang et
al. report recall 80.7% on their own setup with Inception-feature precision/recall and a ~35M model
on 120k images; nothing here contradicts that, and nothing here is a reproduction of their tables.
What is now fair is the *internal* comparison: every arm in these tables is sampled from the same
backbone, scored by the same probes, and configured the way its authors specify.

## 9. Baselines added after a systematic literature review — and three defects it exposed in ours

Two structured reviews of the composition and continual-learning literatures were run against this
repository. The useful output was not the list of new arms; it was a list of ways we were running
*existing* baselines weakly. Those come first, because they are the same failure mode as §5l-bis.

### 9a. Three defects in our own code

**(1) `consolidate()` raised `NameError` on every single-concept call.** The regularizer hook
(`begin_task`) was added at both optimizer sites, but the single-concept `consolidate()` never
declared the `ewc` parameter it referenced. Only `consolidate_batch()` was exercised by the CL
experiments, so nothing caught it. Fixed, and the penalty plus the SI step hooks are now wired
through that path too.

**(2) Composable Diffusion was run at `w_i = 1/n`.** Liu et al.'s `w_i` are per-concept *guidance
scales* meant to add — the sum of residuals is what makes the operator a product rather than a mean
— and they run values around 5–7.5. Dividing by `n` pins total guidance at 1.0 regardless of how
many concepts are composed, which is approximately their **average**, and gets weaker as more
concepts are added. This is the identical error to §5l-bis, where running the published method on
our schedule instead of its own Eq. 14 took its precision from 0.039 to 0.839. The weight is now
swept, not defaulted (§9b).

**(3) The textual-inversion baseline was confined to `span(C)`.** `learn_embedding` optimizes
`alpha` over trained class embeddings. That restriction is a *claim our method makes* — that an
unseen composition is expressible in the model's existing vocabulary — so imposing it on the
baseline assumes exactly what is under test, and handicaps it precisely when the held-out
combination falls outside the hull of seen classes. Textual Inversion as published (Gal et al.)
optimizes a **free vector in R^d**. Added as `free=True` and reported as a separate
`inversion-free` arm; the span-restricted version stays as ours.

### 9b. Composable Diffusion, swept rather than defaulted

`tests/diag_composable_scale.py`, ColorMNIST, 3 held-out compositions, top-3 slots, 16 samples:

| scale | joint | digit | fg | bg | precision | recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.33 | **0.000** | 0.062 | 0.62 | 0.27 | 0.000 | 0.630 |
| 1.00 | **0.000** | 0.000 | 0.48 | 0.23 | 0.000 | 0.120 |
| 2.00 | **0.000** | 0.146 | 0.10 | 0.42 | 0.000 | 0.367 |
| 3.00 | **0.000** | 0.167 | 0.04 | 0.27 | 0.000 | 0.007 |
| 5.00 | **0.000** | 0.104 | 0.04 | 0.31 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| 7.50 | **0.000** | 0.000 | 0.10 | 0.33 | 0.000 | 0.000 |

Joint accuracy is **0.000 at every scale**, and precision is 0.000 at every scale. What the scale
buys is the usual guidance-versus-diversity trade in the *wrong* place: at 0.33 the arm has recall
0.630 with precision 0.000, i.e. it is diverse and wrong.

This is the operator's own precondition failing, not a tuning failure, and it is worth stating
plainly because it bounds what score addition can do here. Composable Diffusion composes *trained
conditionals*. ColorMNIST's backbone has no conditional for "cyan foreground" and none for
"deep-red background" — it has 120 entangled slot conditionals. Adding their scores cannot isolate a
factor that no conditional represents. Having swept it across a 23× range, the row can no longer be
read as a strawman.

**CelebA is the case where the method genuinely applies**, because the factored head provides a real
per-attribute conditional. There it composes exactly the conditionals our method composes, in score
space rather than embedding space — the sharpest available comparison of the two operators — and it
is reported at the scale chosen by its own sweep.

### 9c. The strongest objection to our own operator, now an arm

Du et al. (ICML 2023) prove that for a product `q^1 · q^2`,

```
grad log q~_t^prod(x_t)  !=  grad log q_t^1(x_t) + grad log q_t^2(x_t)
```

The sum of diffused scores is the score of the **product of diffused marginals**, not the
**diffusion of the product**. Every method that pushes a composed score through DDIM or ancestral
sampling — Composable Diffusion, the PoE teacher, and *ours* — is therefore targeting the wrong
distribution, and the blame is the **sampler's**, not the model's. Their fix is extra MCMC
transitions per noise level, which takes CLEVR 5-cube composition from 70.8% to 91.6% and ImageNet
classifier guidance from 18.6% to 94.6%, at ~5× sampling cost.

It is training-free and exemplar-free, so it is unambiguously fair here. Implemented as annealed
unadjusted Langevin (`score.py`, `mcmc=dict(steps, lam)`) — MALA's accept/reject would need an
energy parameterization a pretrained eps-net does not provide — and reported as `composable-mcmc`.

### 9d. Continual-learning arms added

The retention tables previously compared only against CL methods derived from the *classifier*
literature. Added, all on the same backbone and optimizer budget:

- **SI** (Zenke et al. 2017) — path-integral importance accumulated online. The cleanest transfer in
  its family: it inspects only gradients and weight trajectories, never output semantics, so it
  applies to an eps-regression objective without reinterpretation.
- **MAS** (Aljundi et al. 2018) — importance as the sensitivity of the squared output norm. Derived
  for an *arbitrary* vector output with no labels and no softmax, so setting `F = eps_theta` is not
  an adaptation of the method, it *is* the method.
- **DER++** (Buzzega et al. 2020) — the strongest simple baseline in modern CL. Its alpha term
  matches a prediction recorded mid-trajectory; its beta term is plain exemplar replay.
- **generative distillation** (Masip et al., CoLLAs 2025) — see below.

**Why `gen-distill` had to be added rather than just reporting `replay`.** Naive generative replay
is a *documented pathology* for diffusion: Masip et al. measure it at **FID 211.8 against plain
finetuning's 68.8** — nearly 3× worse than no protection at all — because self-generated samples are
regressed onto the noise drawn for them, so their errors are treated as ground truth and compound.
Distillation instead matches the **pre-task model's own eps-prediction** on the same `(x_t, t)`,
transferring the whole reverse process, and recovers FID 23.5 against a joint bound of 15.5.
Reporting `replay` alone would be reporting a known-broken configuration as a baseline.

### 9e. DER++ at ACC 0.250 was our bug, not a result

The first DER++ run came in at **ACC 0.250, BWT −0.263** — far below plain finetuning's 0.750. That
reads as a damning result for the strongest baseline in the CL literature, which is exactly why it
was worth not believing.

The cause: DER's stored target `z = h(x)` is a function of a **fixed** input. Here the network's
input is `x_t = sqrt(abar) x0 + sqrt(1-abar) e`, and the buffer was redrawing `e` at replay time.
The stored eps-prediction therefore described a *different image* than the one being presented — an
incoherent target, and a strong one, applied at every step. Storing the noise draw alongside the
sample fixes it:

| DER++ | ACC | BWT | FGT |
|---|---|---|---|
| redrawn noise (wrong) | 0.250 | −0.263 | 0.263 |
| stored noise (correct) | **0.781** | −0.025 | 0.025 |

Third measurement bug in this project found by a control row rather than by a result.

### 9f. Two columns the tables were missing

**Generation quality.** Probe accuracy is *fidelity-blind*: an arm can keep the attributes legible
while its images degrade, and the column will not show it. Masip et al.'s numbers are the proof —
generative replay scores *higher* classifier accuracy than finetuning (0.50 vs 0.17) at 3× worse
FID, so an accuracy-only table ranks the broken arm first. Both retention tables now track a
per-concept Frechet distance on the same schedule as the accuracy matrix and report CLoG's **AFQ**
(final quality) and **FR** (its degradation).

**What each arm must keep.** A `stored` column counts the real images an arm retains forever: 0 for
ours and for every regularizer, the buffer size for DER++, and the entire stream for TTT. This is
the axis on which rehearsal methods lose to an insert-only bank, and it was invisible in a table of
accuracies.

**Reference-free diversity and a copying check.** Every other metric here is anchored to real
images, and for a held-out combination that anchoring is weakest exactly where our claim is
strongest — a *correct* novel sample is absent from the training manifold by construction, so
precision and density measured against it are anti-correlated with the property under test. The
**Vendi score** (Friedman & Dieng, TMLR 2023) needs no reference at all. **`memorization()`** is
MOSAIC's `d_NN1/d_NN2 < 1/3` test against the images the backbone actually trained on, because a
method that scores well by *retrieving* a training image is indistinguishable from one that composes
in every aggregate metric above.

At `w = 1/n` — the configuration we had — it scores `pair` 0.38 and looks comfortably beaten. At its
own best scale it scores **0.73 against our 0.71**. A 2022 method with **zero parameters and no
training** matches us on the metric this benchmark was built to test.

**What the extra columns show, and why they are the answer rather than an excuse.** Read across the
scale sweep: as `w` rises, precision climbs 0.38 → 0.92 while recall falls 0.61 → 0.30 and content
diversity falls 1.25 → 0.77, passing *through* the real data's 1.01 and out the other side. That is
the same accuracy-for-coverage trade `poe-guided` makes in §5e, just at a gentler slope — strong
guidance contracts trajectories, and the pool stops covering the composition. At its most accurate
setting the baseline emits samples that are 92% correct and half as varied as real data.

Ours sits at `pair` 0.71 with recall 0.60 and diversity 1.06 — matching real data's spread. The
defensible claim is therefore **not** that our composition operator is better on CelebA. It is that
it reaches equivalent composition accuracy at roughly **twice the coverage**, and that is only
visible because we report recall and diversity next to accuracy.

**The honest bottom line for the composition claim, which is now benchmark-dependent:**

- **CelebA** — our operator does **not** beat score addition. Where the model has genuine
  per-attribute conditionals, adding their scores works about as well as composing in embedding
  space. We hold a coverage advantage at equal accuracy, and that is the whole of it.
- **ColorMNIST** — our operator **strictly dominates**: composable-diff is at 0.000 joint accuracy at
  every scale across a 23× range (§9b), because there is no per-factor conditional to add. Ours is
  not, because it composes in a factor basis recovered from the entangled embeddings rather than in
  score space.

That contrast is a *result*, not a hedge: it identifies the precondition under which the cheaper
operator suffices, and score addition is strictly cheaper when it does. What it costs us is the
claim to a better composition operator in general.

**None of this touches the lifelong claims.** Composable Diffusion produces a sample and keeps
nothing — no concept is registered, nothing is preserved, nothing accumulates, and the next query
starts from the frozen model again. Retention (§7d, N22), bit-exact preservation, the routing
decision (§6c, N18), growth accounting (§5z, N24) and native parity (§6d, N23) are all measured
against arms that *do* retain, and are unaffected. The paper's spine is the lifelong mechanism; the
composition operator is inherited, and on one of two benchmarks it is not improved.

## 10. The metric audit: what each arm is allowed to be measured on

Prompted by a simple question — are we reporting the axes this method actually wins on, and are we
reporting the ones it loses on? Two columns were missing in each direction.

### 10a. Missing columns that favour us

| metric | what it asks | why the old table could not show it |
|---|---|---|
| **A** (Díaz-Rodríguez et al. 2018) | mean accuracy over the WHOLE lower triangle | final-row `ACC` cannot distinguish *never dropped* from *dropped and recovered by the end* |
| **REM** = 1 − \|min(BWT,0)\| | remembering, normalized to [0,1] | same information as BWT, in the direction where 1.0 is the good end |
| **SSS** = 1 − Mem(buffer)/Mem(stream) | what the arm must KEEP | invisible in a table of accuracies; ours is exactly **1.0**, DER++ and TTT are not |
| **stored images** | the same thing, unnormalized | — |

`SSS` is the sharpest of these. The n5 table shows `der++` at ACC 0.792 and `ttt` at 0.797 —
statistically indistinguishable from our 0.792 — but `der++` holds a permanent 200-image buffer and
`ttt` keeps all **768** images of the stream forever, while ours keeps none. An accuracy-only table
reports those three arms as equivalent. They are not.

### 10b. A missing column that does NOT favour us

**MS** (model-size efficiency) is reported for the same reason. An insert-only bank grows, so MS is
the one axis where the honest number is against us, and the n5 table makes it concrete: `independent`
reaches **ACC 0.839** with **7.16M** adapter parameters where ours reaches **0.792** with **8.82M**.
Parameter isolation without a hierarchy is *better on both axes here*.

That is not a new finding — §5s already established the hierarchy does not earn its keep on this
dataset — but it does bound what n5 can be used to claim. **n5 does not show our method beating
parameter isolation; it shows both of them beating everything that tries to protect shared weights.**
The differentiator against `independent` is not in this table at all — it is the routing rule (§6c)
and the growth accounting (§5z), where an *arrangement* costs a 1024-byte basis direction instead of
a 551K-parameter adapter. A reader who takes n5 alone would be entitled to prefer `independent`, and
the paper should say so rather than let the ACC column imply otherwise.

### 10c. What the new quality columns did and did not separate

`AFQ`/`FR` (CLoG's generative-quality protocol on a pixel-PCA Fréchet distance) came out at
0.030–0.040 for **every** arm with `FR` at +0.000 throughout. So on this benchmark the arms are not
distinguishable by sample fidelity — the differences are in *which* attributes get generated, not in
image quality. That is a null result, it is reported as one, and it means the accuracy columns are
not hiding a fidelity collapse the way Masip et al. found for generative replay on Fashion-MNIST.

The one arm where it *should* have bitten is `replay`, which collapses to **ACC 0.479 / BWT −0.307**
— and its AFQ is 0.032, in line with everything else. Its failure is compositional, not visual.

### 10d. Where each claim is actually settled

Written down because the audit's main output was realising that three different tables were being
asked to support the same sentence:

| claim | the table that settles it | the table that does NOT |
|---|---|---|
| concepts are preserved exactly | n5/n22 `retention_exact` = 1.00 vs 0.25 | `ACC`, where `mas` also reaches FGT 0.000 |
| retention costs no stored data | n5/n22 `SSS` = 1.0 vs `der++`, `ttt` | `ACC`, which ranks them equal |

| a consolidated concept is usable | n23 native parity | n9 composition accuracy alone |

## 12. The pretrained backbone (N25): what unblocked it

N15 left this open with a diagnosis — conditioning a 113.7M-parameter unconditional model through a
single additive vector on its time embedding does not move it. Giving the bank real capacity turned
out to be necessary but not sufficient, and the path to a working configuration went through three
distinct faults, each found by looking at samples rather than at numbers.

### 12a. Preservation transfers immediately

`tests/test_pretrained_bank.py`, on **google/ddpm-celebahq-256** (113.7M params) and
**google/ddpm-cifar10-32** (35.7M):

| assertion | result |
|---|---|
| injecting the bank changes the pretrained model | **0.000e+00** |
| a freshly registered concept is a no-op | **0.000e+00** |
| training a second concept moves the first | **0.000e+00** |

The preservation mechanism is structural, so it carries to a model we did not train without
modification. This is the part of the method that transfers cleanly, and it is worth separating from
the part that did not.

### 12b. Three faults, in the order they were removed

**1. Writing to the model's clock.** Our concept vector is added to the *time* embedding. On our own
UNet that slot was trained to carry a class signal; on an unconditional model the time embedding
encodes only the noise level, so an offset on it says *you are at a different timestep*. Training it
corrupts denoising globally. Held at zero; the per-concept adapter path carries the concept.

**2. Adapting the clock by a different route.** Restricting adapters to `linear` layers — where the
personalization literature puts them — turned out to mean the opposite of what it sounds like:
**32 of this model's 58 Linear modules are `time_emb_proj`**, plus two in the time embedding's own
MLP. "Adapt the linears" was adapting the noise-level path in 34 places. Excluding it leaves the 24
attention projections.

**3. Teaching it our blur.** Even attention-only, any learning rate that moved the model also
degraded it. The cause is a distribution mismatch that has nothing to do with the method: our images
are CelebA (178px aligned, upsampled to 256) and the model is CelebA-HQ. Training at low noise levels
— where fine texture is decided — teaches it our upsampling artifacts, which is exactly what the
"painterly and patchy" samples were.

### 12c. The fix, and the number

Training only at **high noise levels**, where semantics are decided and texture is not:

| training range | guidance w | `Eyeglasses` rate | Vendi | samples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| all t | 1 | 0.00 | 1.54 | already degraded |
| all t | 6 | 0.25 | 1.29 | unrecognizable |
| **t ≥ 500** | 1 | 0.00 | **1.47** | **clean** |
| **t ≥ 500** | 3 | **0.75** | **1.48** | artifacts return |
| **t ≥ 700** | 1 | 0.25 | **1.46** | **clean, glasses present** |
| t ≥ 700 | 3 | 0.50 | 1.36 | stylized but coherent |

*(untouched model: Vendi 1.51)*

So an attribute can be added to a pretrained diffusion model through this bank without damaging it,
provided the adaptation is confined to the noise levels at which the attribute actually lives. The
sampling-time amplification is free: the unconditional branch **is** the frozen model with the
adapter path deactivated, so `eps_frozen + w (eps_adapter − eps_frozen)` needs no CFG dropout and no
unconditional token.

### 12d. What this does and does not license

It does **not** restate the out-of-distribution claim. This model's training set is not ours to
control, so we cannot say it never saw a smiling face in glasses. What is controlled is what **our
concepts** saw: concept A is trained only on `(a=1, b=0)` and concept B only on `(b=1, a=0)`, so
their co-occurrence is held out of both by construction, and composing them is a genuine held-out
combination *at the level of the representations this method builds*.

The general lesson is worth stating separately from this project: **when adapting a pretrained
diffusion model on data from a lower-quality distribution than it was trained on, restrict training
to high noise levels.** Otherwise the adapter spends its capacity transferring your artifacts, and
the failure looks like a capacity or a learning-rate problem when it is neither.

### 12e. The case study, and what it says about our composition operator

`google/ddpm-celebahq-256`, 128 real images per concept, attention-only adapters trained at t ≥ 600,
sampled with w = 2 against the frozen base. `Eyeglasses` and `Smiling` are learned as separate
concepts, each blind to the other (A on `a=1, b=0`, B on `b=1, a=0`), so their co-occurrence is
withheld from both by construction.

| arm | Eyeglasses | Smiling | both | Vendi |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *real* | *1.00* | *1.00* | *1.00* | *1.38* |
| pretrained (untouched) | 0.00 | 0.67 | 0.00 | 1.51 |
| **`Eyeglasses` alone** | **1.00** | 0.50 | 0.50 | **1.58** |
| **`Smiling` alone** | 0.00 | **1.00** | 0.00 | **1.60** |
| composable-diff (score addition) | 0.67 | 0.83 | **0.50** | 1.56 |
| ours (both adapter paths active) | 0.50 | 0.67 | 0.17 | 1.37 |

**Concept acquisition works, cleanly.** Each concept reaches 1.00 on its own attribute at a Vendi
*above* the untouched model's 1.51, and the samples are recognizable, well-formed 256×256 faces. That
is the thing N15 could not do, and it took the three fixes in §12b plus high-noise-only training.

**Our composition operator is the weakest row in the table**, and the reason is structural rather
than incidental. `ours` composes by activating both adapter paths at once, so it *sums two deltas
that were each trained to be applied alone*. Score addition composes the same two concepts by
combining their predictions instead, and gets `both` 0.50 against our 0.17 while keeping Vendi 1.56
against our 1.37.

This is the third independent measurement pointing the same way — §9g, §11c and now here — and it is
the actionable one, because it names a fix rather than a limitation: **a bank of independently
trained adapters should be composed in SCORE space, not by activating several paths simultaneously.**
The LoRA-merging literature exists precisely because summed deltas interfere (ZipLoRA, Orthogonal
Adaptation, Custom Diffusion's constrained merge all address this), and this repository had been
doing the naive thing at the point where it matters most.

Nothing about preservation changes: the paths are still insert-only, ancestors are still frozen, and
§12a certifies bit-exactness on this model. What changes is the recommended way to *read* the bank.

## 13. Conditional vs unconditional, and what a composition inherits (N27)

Score composition behaved in two opposite ways across this repository and the split looked like a
property of the datasets:

| backbone | conditioning channel | embedding composition | score addition |
|---|---|---|---|
| ColorMNIST | one entangled vector per SLOT | F1 0.605 | **0.000** |
| CelebA | one vector per ATTRIBUTE VALUE | pair 0.903 | 0.934–0.965 |
| CelebA-HQ (pretrained) | none | path union 0.17 | 0.50 |

It is not a property of the datasets. N27 tests it directly by **giving the ColorMNIST backbone
per-factor conditionals it does not have** — an adapter concept trained on every SEEN image sharing a
factor value (`fg=f` over all digits and backgrounds, and so on) — and re-running the identical
operator on the identical backbone. Everything stays in-distribution: each factor VALUE appears in
the seen slots, only the COMBINATION is held out.

### 13a. The factor conditionals are real

Each adapter isolates its own factor almost perfectly:

| concept, sampled alone | its own factor | the other two |
|---|---|---|
| `fg0` | fg **1.00** | bg 0.06, digit 0.06 |
| `fg3` | fg **1.00** | bg 0.00, digit 0.09 |
| `bg2` | bg **1.00** | fg 0.00, digit 0.06 |
| `bg3` | bg **0.94** | fg 0.00, digit 0.06 |
| `digit6` | digit **0.94** | fg 0.12, bg 0.44 |
| `digit1` | digit **0.81** | fg 0.31, bg 0.38 |

### 13b. And the operator that produced *nothing* now produces something

3 held-out compositions, 16 samples:

| arm | joint | digit | fg | bg | F1 | Vendi |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *real* | *0.792* | *0.792* | *1.00* | *1.00* | *0.956* | *1.67* |
| **ours** (embedding space) | **0.500** | 0.646 | 0.92 | 0.85 | **0.596** | 1.81 |
| **factor-score** (score addition over FACTOR conditionals) | **0.167** | 0.458 | 0.65 | 0.75 | 0.434 | 1.97 |
| slot-score (score addition over SLOT conditionals) | 0.000 | 0.042 | 0.48 | 0.23 | 0.000 | 2.02 |
| factor-union (same adapters, paths activated together) | 0.000 | 0.104 | 0.12 | 0.15 | 0.000 | 2.73 |

**The operator was never the problem.** Given conditionals at the right granularity, score addition
goes from 0.000 to 0.167 joint accuracy and from F1 0.000 to 0.434 — on the same backbone, the same
data and the same arithmetic. ColorMNIST's 120 conditionals are per-slot, and not one of them means
"cyan foreground", so adding them cannot isolate a factor none of them represents.

**But granularity does not close the gap.** Our embedding composition still wins, 0.500 against
0.167, and the reason is visible in the table above: a conditional trained on "every image with
fg = f" is `p(x | fg = f)`, which carries **the marginal of the other factors along with it**. `fg3`
alone puts bg at 0.00 — it does not merely fail to specify the background, it actively imposes the
background distribution of its own training set. Adding three such conditionals makes each one fight
the other two over factors it was never meant to speak about, and the product only partially resolves
the disagreement.

That is the difference from CelebA, where score addition wins. CelebA's factored head is trained with
conditioning built as a **sum of per-attribute embeddings**, so the model has seen those vectors used
compositionally and `attr[k, v]` genuinely means one attribute and stays silent about the rest.
ColorMNIST's factor adapters are fitted post-hoc to marginals and cannot be silent.

### 13c. The rule this gives

> **A composition inherits the granularity — and the marginals — of the conditionals it composes.**
>
> - **No conditioning channel** (unconditional pretrained model): concepts can only be deltas. Deltas
>   must be composed in **score** space; summing them in parameter space interferes (§12e, and
>   `factor-union` at 0.000 here).
> - **Entangled conditionals** (one per combination): score addition cannot reach a novel
>   arrangement, because no conditional isolates a factor. Composition has to happen in a space where
>   the factor structure is explicit — which is what the ideal-words basis provides (R² 0.826), and
>   why our embedding composition is the operator that works here.
> - **Factored conditionals trained compositionally** (CelebA): score addition is the strongest
>   operator available, and better than ours (§11c).
>
> The practical corollary for a bank: **what you put in it determines what you can compose.** A bank
> of slot concepts supports retrieval; a bank of factor concepts supports composition. That is a
> design decision the routing rule already makes — an *arrangement* costs a basis direction and a
> new *part* costs an adapter — and N27 is the measurement showing why the distinction matters
> beyond cost.

**A caveat this experiment carries.** Its factor concepts are trained from 96 real images each, which
is a stronger supervision than the single query the OOD setting provides. It is a probe of the
operator, not a method: it answers "why does score addition fail here", not "here is a better way to
compose". `ours` remains the operator for ColorMNIST, and it wins by 3×.

## 14. Update rules: "disturb the field least" is the wrong objective (N28)

The insert-only guarantee covers an INACTIVE path. Composition evaluates an adapter on inputs it
never saw, and nothing certifies its behaviour there — so the natural hypothesis is that an update
which changes the frozen field only where it must should compose better than one that changes it
everywhere. N28 measures that directly, on identical data, budget and architecture.

`drift` is how far the model moves on conditionings the concept is *not* for, with its path **active**:
`||eps_path(x,u,c_seen) − eps_frozen(x,u,c_seen)||` over trained classes.

| rule | drift | fg alone | bg alone | digit alone | composed joint | recall | F1 | wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| uniform | 0.0440 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.81 | 0.062 | 0.461 | 0.146 | 52s |
| **high-t** (t ≥ 0.5) | **0.0469** *(worst)* | 1.00 | 1.00 | **0.88** | **0.104** | **0.882** | **0.181** | 52s |
| need-weighted | 0.0406 | 0.98 | 0.94 | 0.81 | 0.042 | 0.684 | 0.140 | 52s |
| residual | 0.0450 | 1.00 | 0.98 | 0.79 | 0.062 | 0.875 | 0.110 | 69s |
| **rehearse** | **0.0106** *(best)* | 0.92 | 0.90 | 0.62 | **0.000** *(worst)* | 0.781 | 0.105 | 111s |

**The hypothesis is false, and cleanly so.** The rule with the *lowest* drift composes *worst*, and
the rule with the *highest* drift composes *best*. `rehearse` suppresses drift by a factor of four —
0.0106 against 0.041–0.047 — and reaches joint 0.000, because a penalty for changing anything also
prevents the adapter from changing what it should: its factor accuracies are the lowest in the table
(digit 0.62 against 0.81–0.88).

So "an update that reflects the existing score field" does **not** mean "an update that disturbs it
least". Drift is not a cost to be minimized; it is the mechanism by which a conditional differs from
the unconditional model at all.

**What does work is choosing WHERE to spend the update.** `high-t` — train only at noise levels above
the midpoint — wins on composed joint accuracy (0.104), on recall (0.882 against uniform's 0.461) and
on F1, at identical wall clock. This is the pretrained-backbone finding (§12b) reproducing on a
backbone we trained ourselves and on a completely different dataset, which is what makes it a
property of diffusion adaptation rather than of that one model's data mismatch:

> A factor's *identity* is decided at high noise. Capacity spent at low noise goes to texture the
> model already renders correctly, and buys nothing.

`need-weighted` — sampling noise levels in proportion to the frozen model's own error — is the more
principled version of the same idea and it does *not* beat the blunt threshold (joint 0.042 against
0.104). Its measured "need" is largest at the noisiest levels where every model does badly, so it
over-weights the extreme tail rather than the semantically decisive middle-to-high band. The crude
rule is better, and that is worth stating rather than presenting the elegant one.

**Recommendation adopted:** train concept adapters at high noise levels. It costs nothing, it is one
line, and it improves composition and coverage on both backbones tested.

## 15. Batch addition on a pretrained backbone (N26), and where its speedup actually comes from

"Do not learn one instance at a time into the manifold" is a requirement, not a preference: a stream
that arrives in groups must be absorbed in groups or the cost of the manifold becomes the cost of its
history. N4 established batch addition on our own 6.8M UNet — **1.7× faster** than K sequential
consolidations at identical accuracy, retention still exactly 1.000, and genuinely shared nodes (6
against 3). N26 asks the same of `google/ddpm-celebahq-256`, and needs its own trainer, because
`consolidate_batch` samples our cosine schedule and a pretrained DDPM has its own.

4 concepts (`Eyeglasses`, `Smiling`, `Wearing_Hat`, `Male`), 96 real images each, 40 epochs:

| arm | wall | attribute rate | Vendi | nodes | shared | **bit-identical** | params |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sequential (4 optimizations) | 777s | **0.75** | 1.46 | 5 | 3 | **1.00** | 0.98M |
| batch (1 optimization) | 777s | 0.58 | 1.42 | 5 | 3 | **1.00** | 0.98M |
| batch-shared (1 optimization, group node) | 783s | 0.67 | 1.45 | 6 | **6** | **1.00** | 1.18M |

**The requirement is met: K concepts go in as one update, and every concept is still bit-identical
afterwards on a 113.7M-parameter model we did not train.** `batch-shared` additionally creates twice
the sharing (6 shared node-uses against 3), which is the thing a sequential run structurally cannot
do — a shared ancestor accumulates gradient from all K members inside one optimization, where
sequential can only hand a later concept a frozen warm start.

**But there is no wall-clock speedup here (1.00×), and that is the more useful finding.** Comparing
against N4's 1.7× locates where batching actually pays:

> Batch addition amortizes the **generative pool sampling**, not the optimizer. On ColorMNIST each
> concept needs a pool sampled from the model — one reverse-diffusion run instead of K is the whole
> saving. Here the concepts are trained on **real images**, so there is no pool, and interleaving K
> concepts' minibatches costs exactly the same number of optimizer steps as running them in sequence.

So the efficiency claim should be stated conditionally rather than generally: batching is a large win
when concepts are distilled from *generated* pools, and a wash when they come from *real* data. What
it buys unconditionally is the shared structure and the preservation guarantee, both of which hold on
both backbones.

Attribute rates (0.75 / 0.58 / 0.67) are over 4 samples × 4 concepts and the differences are within
what that resolution can distinguish; the honest reading is that batching does not *improve* concept
quality, and the case for it rests on cost and sharing.

## 16. How far does batch addition scale, and what does it cost? (N4, K-sweep)

N4 compared batch against sequential at one batch size. This sweeps K with the total held fixed at
**12 concepts**, so `sequential` is the same run throughout (a useful control: it lands at 680–690s
and ACC 0.547 every time) and only the size of each update changes.

| K (concepts per update) | wall | speedup | ACC | FGT | **bit-identical** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sequential (K=1, one at a time) | 686s | — | **0.547** | 0.000 | **1.00** |
| 1 | 561s | 1.23× | 0.370 | 0.000 | **1.00** |
| 2 | 429s | 1.59× | 0.375 | 0.000 | **1.00** |
| 4 | 341s | 2.00× | 0.453 | 0.000 | **1.00** |
| 8 | 297s | 2.29× | 0.396 | 0.000 | **1.00** |
| **12** (all of them in ONE update) | **260s** | **2.64×** | 0.422 | 0.000 | **1.00** |

**Two things scale cleanly.** The speedup grows monotonically with K and does not plateau by K=12 —
all twelve concepts absorbed in a single update run 2.64× faster than twelve updates. And
`retention_exact` is **1.00 at every K**, with FGT 0.000: batch size is a free efficiency knob and
not a safety trade, which is the property the insert-only design was supposed to give and here it is
measured across a 12× range.

**One thing costs.** Batch accuracy sits at 0.37–0.45 against sequential's 0.547 — a real gap, and
one an earlier reading of N4 (8 concepts, batch 4) missed because at that size the two matched at
0.609. The sweep localizes it precisely:

> **The loss is NOT in the batched optimizer.** At **K = 1** the batch path already scores 0.370
> against sequential's 0.547 — and at K = 1 the optimizer is doing identical work in both. Whatever
> costs the accuracy is upstream of consolidation.

Upstream, `learn_concepts_batch` differs from `learn_concept` in **two** ways, and this sweep does not
separate them:

1. **discovery configuration** — the batch path is handed an explicit `disc`
   (`n_starts=16, iters=120`), the sequential path calls `compose(xq)` and gets
   `candidates_ascent`'s defaults. The two modes are therefore not running the same discovery;
2. **pooled sampling** — the batch path draws all K pools from one reverse-diffusion run
   (`sample_teacher_batch`), the sequential path draws each separately.

The first is a **confound in the experiment**, not a property of batching, and it should be removed
before the second can be blamed. §16b does that.

It also explains §15's result rather than conflicting with it: on the pretrained backbone there are
no pools at all (concepts train on real images), so there is neither the 2.64× saving **nor** the
accuracy cost, and batch and sequential come out at 777s with retention 1.00 both ways.
