# Borrowed grounding beats composition for frozen VLAs

No published method in industry or academia makes a robot policy read words it was never trained to contrast. The working answer everywhere is to supply grounding from outside the policy and to add new motion with small weight updates. Industry raises zero-shot rates upstream: web co-training, insulating the VLM from action gradients, hierarchy, subgoal images and periodic retraining on fleet data. Its strongest generalist, pi0.7, still reports **60-80% on unseen tasks against >90% on seen ones**, and no company publishes sequential task addition with a forgetting analysis. In academia, our frozen pi0.5's failure is the best-documented VLA failure of 2025-2026. LangGap measures pi0.5 at **0% when only the target changes in a familiar scene** and **37.7% on LIBERO-Object object swaps**; those two numbers correspond to our 10/10 memorized-object picks and our 43% swap-following. Inference-time classifier-free contrast (CAG, RAS, CPG) adds roughly 4 to 12 points because it amplifies a signal that must already exist, the same ceiling our pre-registered composition test hit (22 vs 9 of 240). The largest frozen-policy gain on record reroutes grounding through geometry. ReGuide (arXiv 2609.38616, posted 29 September 2026) lifts frozen pi0.5 from **39.0% to 94.5%** on LIBERO composition cells using a detector, depth and demonstration statistics, with no training. That makes it our most direct competitor. For continual learning, freezing the backbone makes forgetting free. Published per-task units cost 2-26 MB, and our 18 KB prefix is about three orders of magnitude smaller than any of them. No benchmark runs compositional OOD tasks as a continual stream over a frozen VLA, so defining that protocol is itself a contribution. The best next move combines the factor token with a non-linguistic referent channel and a low-rank fallback for new motion, evaluated as a stream over LIBERO-OOD, LangGap and ReGuide-style single-factor cells.

## LIBERO-trained VLAs ignore words wherever the scene already names the task

At least seven independent 2025-2026 studies find that LIBERO-trained VLAs mostly execute the task the scene implies, whatever the instruction says.

LIBERO-Plus replaced the instruction with an empty string and found OpenVLA-OFT's object-suite success "remained largely unchanged." Swapping the target object for another object in the same scene dropped success "nearly to zero," and the authors call the apparent language robustness "language ignorance" ([LIBERO-Plus](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13626)). LIBERO-PRO fed in strings like "fdsgfdsgsd" and watched OpenVLA, pi0 and pi0.5 "still execute nearly the same action trajectory." With the target removed, the same models "grasp the air" at the memorized location ([LIBERO-PRO](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03827)).

LIBERO-CF gives familiar layouts alternative feasible instructions. pi0.5 grounds only **30.8%** of them and succeeds on **13.2%**, and OpenVLA-OFT executes the trained task instead **83.6%** of the time ([LIBERO-CF](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17659)). A mechanistic study across six VLAs injected one task's activations into another task's episodes: **99.8%** of X-VLA episodes then followed the source task's trajectory. On libero_object, X-VLA succeeds 60-100% "regardless of the prompt" ([Grant et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19233)). LIBERO-Para finds that **79.5-95.5% of paraphrase failures** are the wrong task chosen, not the right task executed badly ([LIBERO-Para](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28301v1)).

**LangGap is the closest published match to our protocol.** It holds the layout fixed and changes only the instruction. pi0.5 falls from **93.8% on the original tasks to 21.4% on the extended ones**. Change Target scores **0.0% across all 13 tasks and 260 episodes**: "the model never redirects to an alternative target location." Change Object scores **29.3%**, and the Object suite drops from **100% to 37.7%** ([LangGap](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.00592)).

Our living-room result is the Change Target regime: the policy picks the trained routine's first object 10/10, whatever is named. Our 43% swap-following in LIBERO-Object layouts sits just above LangGap's 37.7%. LangGap also states the ceiling: with k trained tasks per scene, a language-blind policy reaches at most 1/k. A LIBERO-90 scene with one trained instruction is the extreme case, k = 1.

**The cause is the data, not model capacity.** LangForce formalizes it. Goal-driven collection makes the instruction predictable from the image, H(ℓ|v) ≈ 0, so maximum-likelihood training learns p(a|v) and ignores ℓ ([LangForce](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15197)). CAST calls the same effect posterior collapse ([CAST](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13446)). LIBERO "assign[s] only one task per layout" ([LangGap](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.00592)) and averages **2.6 tasks per scene**, against 6 in LIBERO-X ([LIBERO-X](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06556)). Physical Intelligence's Knowledge Insulation paper adds gradient interference from a freshly initialized action expert. It also concedes that even with insulation, "correlations in the training data still cause the model to sometimes ignore language instructions" ([KI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.23705)).

The text-to-image parallel works the same way. DALL-E 3 traced ignored prompt words to captions that omit objects, positions and counts ([DALL-E 3](https://cdn.openai.com/papers/dall-e-3.pdf)). SD3 found that a 50% synthetic-caption mix raised GenEval position accuracy from **6.5 to 18.0** ([SD3](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03206)). One difference matters for robotics: a new instruction for an old scene needs a new trajectory, not just new text.

**Skill and grounding come apart cleanly.** "Robust Skills, Brittle Grounding" reports that SmolVLA and pi0.5 keep reliable grasp execution while instruction-conditioned success falls ([Emukpere et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24143)). RoboSemanticBench finds that policies pick the semantically correct block at near-random rates once grasp success is controlled for ([RoboSemanticBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02277)). ReGuide concludes that failures "usually stem from incorrect global grounding rather than a loss of local manipulation skills" ([ReGuide](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38616)).

This decoupling is why a frozen policy is worth keeping. It is also why every result we report should split "picked the named object" from "completed the task." Outside LIBERO, cross-task zero-shot is just as poor. On AGNOSTOS, pi0 reaches **21.7% on Level-1 and 12.0% on Level-2** unseen tasks, and "all prior models completely fail on at least eight of the 23 tasks" ([AGNOSTOS](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15660v3)).

## Industry buys generalization upstream, then retrains instead of adding

**Physical Intelligence combines data diversity, VLM insulation and steerability, and still stops at 60-80% on unseen tasks.**

pi0.5 trains on a mix in which 97.6% of first-phase examples are not target mobile-manipulator data. Removing the web data makes it "significantly worse" on out-of-distribution objects ([pi0.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.16054)).

Knowledge Insulation stops action-expert gradients from reaching the VLM and co-trains on FAST tokens and VLM data. In the KI paper's figure, language following on a drawer task rises from roughly 40% for pi0 to roughly 90%. The authors add that "freezing the backbone is not a viable option" during training ([KI](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23705)).

pi0.7 conditions on subtask text, on quality, speed and mistake metadata, on control mode, and on subgoal images from a BAGEL-initialized world model, dropping each signal at random during training. Short unseen tasks run zero-shot from the prompt. Long ones use step-by-step human "coaching," and those coaching episodes then train a high-level policy without new teleoperation ([pi0.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15483v2)).

PI states the limits itself. Unseen tasks land "in the 60-80% range." The model "may well be achieving generalization primarily by 'remixing' skills." "Reverse Fridge to Microwave," which contradicts training correlations, needed generated subgoal images ([pi0.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15483v2)). The Decoder found two training episodes of a robot closing an air fryer behind the "unseen" air-fryer demo ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/physical-intelligence-shows-robot-model-with-llm-like-generalization-flaws-included/)).

PI's continual improvement is batch retraining. pi\*0.6's Recap loop conditions the policy on advantage. Each iteration fine-tunes both value function and policy "from the pre-trained checkpoint, rather than the policy and value function from the last iteration," to avoid drift ([pi\*0.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.14759v1)).

**DeepMind leans on Gemini's semantics and on hierarchy.** Gemini Robotics 1.5 pairs an embodied-reasoning orchestrator with a "thinking" VLA. The combined stack reaches about **80% progress on long-horizon tasks against 44%** for the VLA alone ([GR 1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03342)). New tasks still come from small fine-tunes: **>70% on 7 of 8 tasks with at most 100 demonstrations** in 1.0 ([GR 1.0](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.20020)), and fewer than 200 examples for a new embodiment with On-Device 2 ([GR 2](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-robotics-2-brings-whole-body-intelligence-to-robots/)). Earlier, RT-Trajectory conditioned on a 2D trajectory sketch instead of language. It reached **63% on 41 unseen tasks, against 29%** for RT-2 ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/shaping-the-future-of-advanced-robotics/)).

**NVIDIA gets language following from freezing the VLM and new behaviors from synthetic data.** GR00T N1.5 with a frozen Eagle 2.5 VLM raised real GR-1 language following from **46.6% to 93.3%** ([N1.5](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/gear/gr00t-n1_5/)). For new behaviors NVIDIA generates data rather than prompting. DreamGen taught a humanoid 22 new behaviors from teleoperation of one pick-and-place task ([DreamGen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12705)). GR00T N1's 827 hours of neural trajectories cost about **105k L40 GPU-hours** ([GR00T N1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14734)). NVIDIA's own caveat: "multi-task language following and out-of-distribution task generalization continue to be challenging" ([N1.6](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/gear/gr00t-n1_6/)).

**TRI's Large Behavior Model study is the rigorous counterweight to startup claims.** It used blind evaluators, 50 real rollouts per condition and Bayesian posteriors. The non-finetuned multitask diffusion LBM was "statistically indistinguishable from single-task in simulation," and worse than single-task in real nominal conditions. Its value was data efficiency: fine-tuned LBMs beat single-task baselines with **15-30% of the data** ([TRI LBM](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05331)).

**The startups disagree on how to specify a new task. Everything in this paragraph is company-reported.** Skild S1 prompts frozen weights with one human video and reports **66% on unseen tasks against 9%** for a language-prompted VLA at 100k hours. It values a single in-context demonstration at "roughly 380 post-training examples," but conventional VLAs still win at 2,000 post-training demos (86% vs 66%) ([Skild S1](https://www.skild.ai/blogs/s1)). 1X's world-model policy raises tissue pulling from 30% to 45% with best-of-8 generations, at 11 s per generation ([1X](https://www.1x.tech/discover/world-model-self-learning)). Generalist GEN-1 builds new tasks from about an hour of robot data, on top of half a million hours of wearable human data ([GEN-1](https://generalistai.com/blog/gen-1)). Dyna reached new tasks with 0.7% of its dataset and a reward model in the loop ([Dyna](https://www.dyna.co/research/dyna-1)). Figure's Helix auto-labels clips with hindsight instructions, which amounts to industrial-scale recaptioning ([Helix](https://www.figure.ai/news/helix)).

| Skild S1 | One human video in context | No weight change ([Skild](https://www.skild.ai/blogs/s1)) |

No company describes online, task-by-task addition to one deployed model with a forgetting measurement. In industry, "lifelong" means a growing dataset plus periodic retraining, or a stable of specialists. That leaves cheap, non-destructive task addition open in public work.

Two industry levers, VLM insulation and web co-training, already shaped the openpi pi0.5 base. The 40-task LIBERO fine-tune then re-created the k = 1 shortcut. The lever that carries over to a frozen policy is industry's last one: a grounded intermediate (subgoal image, trajectory sketch, video) wherever language cannot override training correlations.

### Score sums and CFG amplify a signal but cannot create one

**Training-free contrast on pi0.5 gives modest gains, and its authors explain why.**

CAG takes the unconditional prediction and adds ω times the gap to the conditional one: π_uncond + ω(π_cond − π_uncond). The unconditional branch is the same model with language dropped. CAG raises pi0.5 grounding on LIBERO-CF from **30.8% to 40.5%** and success from **13.2% to 16.8%**. A separately trained vision-only prior (30k steps) reaches 46.3% grounding and 21.7% success. The authors note that CAG "assumes language signals exist but are underweighted; it may not address cases where language is entirely absent from training" ([LIBERO-CF](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17659)).

The same extrapolation applied to pi0 (RAS) moves success from 52.37% to 64.46% ([RSS](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04052)). DeLock's CPG uses the trained prompt as the negative. That is the variant closest to our setting, since we know each scene's trained instruction. Combined with weight-drift regularization during post-training, it reached **11-19/20 on novel-prompt tasks, against 0-6/20** for RETAIN ([DeLock](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23121)).

Score composition across separately trained policies holds up only "within a range" of hand-tuned weights ([PoCo](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02511v3)). GPC's searched convex score combinations add **2.22 to 7.55** points ([GPC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01068)). Under CAG's stated limitation, our pre-registered main read was the expected outcome: 22 vs 9 of 240, with the gain concentrated in one task.

**Factored Diffusion Policies explain why our sums failed and our shared token worked.** One score network trained with per-factor null-token dropout composes additively and reaches **90% on held-out factor pairs**. Composing K separately trained networks with the same formula collapses to **3%**. The authors conclude that "parameter sharing rather than the additive formula is the mechanism" ([FDP](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22596v1)).

pi05_libero was never trained with per-factor dropout over object or relation words, so its prompt conditionals do not factor. A token fitted jointly on two tasks that share an object is a learned shared parameter. Its 0.9 binding transfer to a third task is the FDP mechanism at 18 KB scale.

Text-latent interpolation shows the other side of the boundary. On LIBERO-OOD, every pick and place location appeared in training and only their pairing is new. There, interpolating pi0's text latents raises success from **0.09 to 0.83 with no retraining** ([Li](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03500)). Both factors were readable on LIBERO-OOD; in our held-out LIBERO-90 scenes they are not. "Was the factor contrasted in training" is the line our own composition experiments drew.

**Retraining with contrast fixes the root cause, but brute-force same-scene data gets diluted.** LangForce trains a vision-only prior next to the language posterior and maximizes their likelihood ratio, gaining 11.3 points on SimplerEnv without new data ([LangForce](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15197)). CAST synthesizes counterfactual instructions and actions and doubles manipulation success under distractors, from 19/60 to 35/60 ([CAST](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13446)). CounterAlign turns counterfactual relabeling into an offline-RL reward and lifts pi0.5's LIBERO-PRO Object-Pos score from 0.19 to 0.51 ([CounterAlign](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21740)).

LangGap is the warning. About 150 scripted demos per new task lift a single task from 3.75% to 90%. A 6-task set rises from 0% to 28%, but adding the 40 original tasks dilutes it to **4%**, and Change Target reaches only about 5-6% after training ([LangGap](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.00592)). A per-task token escapes that dilution by construction, because no other task trains its parameters. That makes contrast-trained tokens a better fit for a stream than contrast-trained shared weights.

### Visual and geometric referents deliver the largest frozen-policy gains

**ReGuide shows that a frozen pi0.5 keeps its motor skill and that WHERE is the bottleneck.** It trains no network. A detector with depth flags when the policy's predicted endpoint heads for the wrong referent ("semantic rebinding"). It then moves the end effector into a pre-contact set derived from the demonstrations ("geometric rebinding") and hands control back to the policy.

The benchmark is 16 single-factor LIBERO cells (target region, target object, unseen object and task scene, with 50 initial states each). On these cells frozen pi0.5 rises from **39.0% to 94.5%**, OpenVLA-OFT reaches 70.8% (+56.8 pp) and GR00T N1.7 climbs from 13.0% to 69.1%; on a real xArm6, pi0.5 goes from 15.0% to 90.0%. When the frozen policy's actions after hand-back are replaced with generic commands, success drops to **28.0-38.1%** ([ReGuide](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38616)). The paper is two days old, unreviewed and has no code link.

**Object-centric conditioning shows the same effect in retrained policies.** Point-VLA conditions pi0.5 on a first-frame bounding box, co-trained 1:1 with text, and averages **92.5% on six real tasks against 32.4%** for text alone ([Point-VLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.18933v1)). OBEYED-VLA has a frozen VLM mask out distractors, which moves pi0 from about 10% to about 85% with four distractors present ([OBEYED](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.22519)). MolmoAct's visual-trace steering beats open-ended language steering by 33% ([MolmoAct](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.07917)).

Three controlled studies give the same ordering. VLA-OS finds that "visually grounded planning representations are generally better than language planning representations" ([VLA-OS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17561)). ERVLA finds "high-level reasoning alone brings only marginal gains" next to end-effector motion descriptions and image-space trajectories ([ERVLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03784)). HAMSTER's VLM-predicted 2D paths give a 20% average gain over OpenVLA across seven generalization axes ([HAMSTER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05485)).

**Hierarchy helps our failure only when the intermediate it passes down is not language.** Hi Robot lists a low-level policy "temporarily ignoring instructions" among its own failure modes ([Hi Robot](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19417)). A text planner over pi05_libero would pass down the same words the policy already ignores.

PrimitiveVLA lifts pi0.5 from 56.0% to 75.5% on 8 unseen LIBERO-90 tasks, but only after fine-tuning the VLA on primitive-segmented data ([PrimitiveVLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28634v1)).

SARL does keep pi0.5 frozen. It Q-learns which atomic command to send and lifts five of ten held-out LIBERO-10 tasks from near 0% to about 80% in 60-100 episodes. Four tasks remain unsolved. SARL requires "VLAs that produce diverse behaviors when prompted with diverse instructions" ([SARL](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31958)). Its base was fine-tuned on all 90 LIBERO-90 tasks, so it reads far more commands than our 40-task checkpoint.

VLA Grounder RL-trains an instruction rewriter for frozen policies but reports no LIBERO numbers ([VLA Grounder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04517)). Training-free constraint planners succeed zero-shot only by discarding the learned policy: OmniManip reaches 68.3% on rigid and 61.7% on articulated real tasks, against 15.0% and 16.7% for VoxPoser ([OmniManip](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.03841)).

### Context, retrieval and prefixes steer known behaviors but add no motion

**Off-the-shelf VLAs ignore demonstrations placed in context.** RICL starts from the premise that "VLAs pre-trained with imitation learning objectives do not naturally acquire ICL abilities." It needs 400 post-training demos before in-context adaptation works. After that, pi0-FAST-DROID reaches **31.25% in context, against 2.5% zero-shot and 31.67% for a 20-demo fine-tune**, and it struggles with substantially new motions ([RICL](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02062v1)).

FOSIL compares the two routes on pi0.5 directly. In-context variants reach 21-25% on LIBERO-PRO perturbations, while a single gradient step reaches 49%. Ten steps from one demo lift LIBERO-10 compositions from **4.6% to 51.6%**, and at rank 1, pi0.5 keeps 96% of the gain on LIBERO-Object and 87% on LIBERO-10 ([FOSIL](https://fosilvla.github.io/); under review, not peer-reviewed).

RA-VLA freezes GR00T N1.5 but trains a retriever and an action head that cross-attends to retrieved segments. It reaches **38.5% on unseen LIBERO tasks from 3 demos, against 20.9%** for the best baseline and near zero for the vanilla model. Its retrieved features take about 2 MB per 16-step segment ([RA-VLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25585)). ReCAP adds a learned residual to retrieved action chunks on Cosmos Policy and reaches **31.5% on unseen RoboTwin tasks, against 4.0%**. Pure replay of the retrieved trajectory already scores 26.0% ([ReCAP](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15631)).

The strongest one-demo numbers come from systems built for in-context use or explicit geometry. Instant Policy reaches 88.75% on 16 real tasks from 1-2 demos after pretraining on about 700K procedurally generated pseudo-demonstrations ([Instant Policy](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12633)). MT3 learned 1,000 tasks from one demo each at **78.25% on seen and 68% on unseen tasks**, using pose estimation and open-loop replay ([MT3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10110)). X-ICM has a 72B LLM read text-serialized demos of other tasks and reaches 37.6% and 20.3% on the two AGNOSTOS levels ([AGNOSTOS](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15660v3)).

Retrieve-then-train is weaker than it looks. Plain fine-tuning beat STRAP at fixed K on LIBERO-10 (**51.7% vs 45.6%**), and every new task costs STRAP a 35-minute training run plus access to the prior corpus ([STRAP](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15182)).

**Our numbers sit where this literature predicts.** From one demo, our LoRA reaches 252/420 (60.0%), our STRAP implementation 168/420 (40.0%) and the 18 KB prefix 140/420 (33.3%). The prefix matches the LoRA where the failure is reading and trails it where the motion is new. The same pattern shows in RICL, whose in-context learning roughly equals fine-tuning on pick-and-place but struggles with new motions, and in TAIL, whose prefix trails LoRA on forward transfer, 0.51 vs 0.70 ([TAIL](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05905)). Our 8-entry tokens, which barely help on motion, are a third instance.

The closest frozen-VLA input optimizers all report on training tasks or under distribution shift, never one-demo held-out tasks. MAP-VLA's additive stage prompts add 7.0 points on LIBERO-Long training tasks ([MAP-VLA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.09516)). TTT-VLA's test-time latent prompt moves pi0.5 on SimplerEnv from 51.1% to 67.4% ([TTT-VLA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03127)). X-VLA learns soft prompts per embodiment, not per task ([X-VLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10274)).

**A one-demo held-out result for a learned prefix on a frozen VLA is unpublished territory that we already occupy.** FOSIL's rank-1 retention suggests our 18.7 MB rank-16 LoRA would shrink to roughly 1.2 MB at rank 1 with modest loss. That figure is our own arithmetic and is untested on our protocol.

### RL and test-time search need a base that already succeeds sometimes

**Per-task practice on held-out LIBERO-90 tasks works on frozen pi0-family policies, at budgets far above ours.** DSRL's SAC noise actor lifted one unseen LIBERO-90 task from about 20% to about 100% in roughly 10k online samples, trained on an 8 GB GPU ([DSRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799v2)). QPILOTS ran six held-out LIBERO-90 tasks on frozen pi0.5-LIBERO at 5×10^5 environment steps, and mean success went from 26 zero-shot to **58 with DSRL, 40 with best-of-5 and 76 with critic-gradient guidance** ([QPILOTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14801)). Golden Ticket searches for a single constant noise vector; it beats Gaussian noise on 46 of 51 tasks and "recover[s] most of the gains of DSRL" with no new networks ([Golden Ticket](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15757)).

Weight-changing RL goes further from a single demo. RIPT-VLA turned "an unworkable SFT model (4%)" into a 97% policy within 15 iterations of 160 rollouts ([RIPT](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17016)). SimpleVLA-RL lifted one-trajectory LIBERO-Long from 17.3% to 91.7% on 8 A800s. It also documents the floor: a 0% base "remains at 0%" because "below this threshold, exploration is ineffective" ([SimpleVLA-RL](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09674v1)).

**Our DSRL result fits that floor.** QPILOTS's tasks start at 26% zero-shot; our held-out set starts at 6/420. SARL's characterization fits too: DSRL "can filter to find good actions from the base policy's distribution, but cannot synthesize fundamentally new ones" ([SARL](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31958)).

The cheap diagnostic is pass@k under the original prompt. SVA shows frozen VLAs rising "from 33% at pass@1 to 92% at pass@32" ([SVA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03751)). The converse matters for us: on a task where none of k samples touches the named object, noise RL, best-of-N and residual RL have nothing to select.

Test-time verifiers are expensive and do not transfer. RoboMonkey's LLaVA-7B verifier, trained on 20M synthetic comparisons, lifts real OOD success from 35% to 60% at about 650 ms per 16 candidates ([RoboMonkey](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17811v2)). CoVer pairs instruction rephrasings with a 1B verifier and moves pi0.5 on PolaRiS from 3.8% to 13.1% success. It also reports that RoboMonkey's verifier "fails catastrophically" on pi0, because it was trained on another policy's samples ([CoVer](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12281v2)).

The synthesis for us is an ordering. First, the token turns zero-support tasks into low-support ones. Only then do the cheap searches have something to select. Those include a Golden Ticket, or a per-task Q head trained on about 20 episodes per iteration, which lifted LIBERO-10 from 93 to 99 in Q-Planning ([Q-Planning](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21204)).

### World models and data generation widen motor coverage at GPU-farm cost

**Video and world models mostly buy motor and visual coverage, and their LIBERO numbers are in-distribution.** GR-1 raised CALVIN zero-shot unseen-scene success from 53.3% to 85.4% ([GR-1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.13139)). Cosmos Policy reports 98.5% on LIBERO with 50 demos per task, all in-distribution ([Cosmos Policy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16163)). V-JEPA 2-AC plans pick-and-place toward image goals after under 62 hours of unlabeled robot video ([V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985)). DreamGen and 1X's world-model policy do produce genuinely new behaviors, but at large cost, and they target verbs a robot never performed rather than words it never read.

**Demonstration generation multiplies one demo along pose axes, not word axes.** DemoGen turns one demo into 243-432 synthetic ones at 0.01 s each and lifts real success from 11.0% to 74.6%. Its authors list spatial-only generalization and "no new motion primitives" as limits ([DemoGen](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16932)). CP-Gen reaches 77% against 50% from a single expert trajectory ([CP-Gen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03944)). MimicGen produced 50K+ demos from about 200 ([MimicGen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17596)).

Generative scene editing could manufacture what a frozen policy never saw: the same scene with the named object or relation changed, and the trajectory changed with it. Examples are ROSIE's instruction-targeted inpainting ([ROSIE](https://diffusion-rosie.github.io/)) and GenAug's 40% generalization gain ([GenAug](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.06671v2)). That is our hypothesis. No paper tests such edits as training data for a prefix token.

## Frozen backbones make forgetting free, so bytes and forward transfer decide

**The 2023 LIBERO baselines set up a trade-off that pretrained VLAs have since dissolved.** In the original lifelong protocol, sequential fine-tuning had the best forward transfer but forgot badly. ER, EWC and PackNet all "hurt forward transfer." Supervised pretraining on LIBERO-90 hurt downstream lifelong learning in 15-26M-parameter models ([LIBERO](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03310)).

Pretrained VLAs still forget without replay. pi0's NBT on LIBERO-Object (positive values mean forgetting) was:

| Training setup | pi0 NBT on LIBERO-Object |
|---|---|
| Sequential fine-tuning | 0.696 |
| EWC | 0.608 |
| Experience replay | −0.007 |

Retraining an old task recovers its peak success in **6.6% of the original steps**, against 136% for a from-scratch BC-Transformer. The VL backbone is the dominant source of the forgetting ([Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03818)). A real-world 10-task stream found that well-configured replay beats joint multitask training at equal compute ([Zhu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26820)). RoboCasa365's lifelong protocol, by contrast, sees atomic-task success collapse from 41.5% to 10.6% across four phases ([RoboCasa365](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04356)).

**Parameter isolation is already the standard route to near-zero forgetting; routing and unit size remain open.** TAIL adapted a 172M policy across LIBERO suites, and forward transfer on LIBERO-10 ranked **LoRA 0.70 > prefix 0.51 > full fine-tuning 0.48**, with no backward loss for the isolated units ([TAIL](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05905)). CORAL gives a frozen VLA one rank-16 LoRA per task, selected by the instruction itself, and reaches **99.3% on all 40 LIBERO tasks** at about 26 MB per expert ([CORAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09298v1)). CLARE adds adapters only where layer similarity demands one and routes without task IDs, reaching AUC 75.1 with NBT 1.9 on LIBERO-Long against ER's NBT of 22.7 ([CLARE](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09512)). IsCiL pairs skill prototypes with rank-4 LoRAs and can fill a task's missing sub-goals from other tasks ([IsCiL](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.22658)), and SCE adds a pi0 expert per new skill to reach 82.9 AUC on LIBERO-Goal ([SCE](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15685)).

Merging cannot replace routing. Standard merging of VLA experts yields "zero success rate," because more than 75% of LoRA parameters are "selfish," relevant to exactly one task. MergeVLA recovers to 90.2% only with sparse task masks and a test-time router ([MergeVLA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18810)). PolicyWeave raises merged success from 17.0% to 52.8% by fixing a shared action interface ([PolicyWeave](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33125)). RETAIN's pretrained/fine-tuned weight interpolation gives about 40% higher OOD success on DROID ([RETAIN](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08333v1)).

| Per-task unit | Approximate size | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Replay buffer of 100-1,000 image samples | Tens to hundreds of MB (estimated from sample counts) | [Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03818) |
| Published LoRA or adapter | ~2-26 MB | [TAIL](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05905); [CORAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09298v1) |
| Our rank-16 pi0.5 LoRA | 18.7 MB | ours |
| Retrieval features (RA-VLA) | ~2 MB per 16-step segment | [RA-VLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25585) |
| Rank-1 LoRA (from FOSIL's retention figures; our arithmetic) | ~1.2 MB | [FOSIL](https://fosilvla.github.io/) |
| Our key/value prefix token | 18 KB | ours |
| Our composition code | 9 B | ours |

**A credible continual claim from us needs three things none of these papers reports.**

The first is size. No published robot continual-learning method stores a per-task unit in the kilobyte range. The closest are the prompt pools in PPL and L2M and DMPEL's coefficient replay, and none of them states bytes per task ([PPL](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.00420v1); [DMPEL](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05985v1)).

The second is sharing: no paper shows per-task cost falling as factors accumulate. Mendez and Eaton demonstrated zero-shot recombination of known modules only in pre-VLA lifelong RL ([Mendez & Eaton](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.00429)). An Oxford study found that a modular world model nearly eliminates forgetting, but only with a frozen pretrained encoder ([compositional continual WMs](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22055)).

The third is headroom. With CORAL near ceiling on the 40 standard LIBERO tasks, our claim must rest on held-out compositional tasks and bytes per task, not on success over training tasks.

## Two LIBERO splits isolate binding, and none streams it

**Standard LIBERO no longer measures generalization.** A 0.54M-parameter MINERVA policy scores 95.1%, and permuting task IDs drops it "to near chance," so the instruction acts as a task selector ([MINERVA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03715)). The official pi05_libero checkpoint averages 96.85% ([openpi](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/blob/main/examples/libero/README.md)). In every comparable paper (ReGuide, TLI), "frozen" means no updates after that LIBERO fine-tune, which matches our setting.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| LIBERO-OOD (Spatial-OOD, Goal-OOD) | Seen pick + seen place, unseen pairing | 35.5 / 46.6 ([AC-VLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.15714)) | TLI with pi0: 0.09 → 0.83 ([Li](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03500)) | High: drop-in, public code |
| ReGuide cells (16 single-factor) | Target region / object / unseen object / scene | 94.0 in-distribution → 39.0 | ReGuide 94.5 ([ReGuide](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38616)) | High, but no code yet |

**Five protocol gaps stand between our results and a credible claim.** The largest is that **no benchmark presents compositional OOD tasks as a continual stream over a frozen VLA, and none labels tasks by factor novelty**; RoboCasa365's lifelong phases come nearest, and they are ordered by horizon, not by novelty ([RoboCasa365](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04356)). The second is that baselines do not reproduce across papers. pi0 on SimplerEnv appears as roughly 52.7 in one paper and 71.4 in another ([LatBot](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.23034); [Xiaomi-Robotics-0](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12684)), and the RoboCasa365 paper disagrees with its own leaderboard, so we should re-run pi0.5 on LIBERO-OOD in our harness rather than trust one paper's 35.5 / 46.6. The third is that no paper splits its gains into grounding repairs and motor repairs on the same task set, which argues for reporting grounded-pick rate next to execution success, as "Robust Skills, Brittle Grounding" proposes ([Emukpere et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24143)). The fourth is that no paper reports bytes per task, or demos per task as a function of task index. The fifth is that no benchmark requires a control for the policy being bypassed. ReGuide's generic-command ablation is the model to follow, and our own 2026-10-01 bindings measurement, which found that the guide can drive the arm without the policy, makes a no-policy control on every split mandatory.

The protocol we can define runs one sequential stream on frozen pi05_libero, drawing tasks from LIBERO-OOD, the LangGap extended tasks and our held-out LIBERO-90 tasks. Each task is labelled as a recombination of read factors, an unread word, a new relation or a new motion. The stream reports success and grounded-pick rate, FWT, NBT and AUC, demos and bytes per task, and the language-blind ceiling of 1/k per scene. Its baselines are zero-shot, CPG and CAG-TF, a CORAL-style one-demo LoRA library, a TAIL-style prefix, ER at 2% replay, and ReGuide once its code is released.

## Ranked next moves and the work that competes with them

The ranking weighs expected gain on the held-out set against two risks: being scooped, and measuring a bypass instead of the policy. The top three moves combine into one system. The token carries the task's binding, a referent channel carries WHERE, and a low-rank unit carries new motion only when a diagnostic calls for it.

| Rank | Option | Evidence | Expected payoff | Main risk | Cost per task |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pair the factor token with a non-linguistic referent channel: a detector/depth pre-contact pose handed back to the frozen policy, or image-side highlighting | ReGuide 39.0→94.5 ([ReGuide](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38616)); Point-VLA 92.5 vs 32.4; visual plans beat language plans ([VLA-OS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17561)) | Large on reading failures, which dominate our 420 episodes | ReGuide already owns "frozen + external WHERE." We must show the token adds cross-task transfer, KB storage and coverage of motion-needing tasks, and run no-policy and generic-command controls | Detector inference; 18 KB plus demo statistics |
| 2 | Route between the token (reading) and a rank-1 LoRA or few-step update (new motion), triggered by a pass@k / grounded-pick diagnostic | FOSIL rank-1 keeps 87-96% of the gain ([FOSIL](https://fosilvla.github.io/)); TAIL LoRA > prefix; our 140 vs 252 split | Closes much of the 112-episode gap, spending ~1.2 MB only where needed | Rank-1 is untested on our protocol; routing errors; weakens the "KB only" headline | Seconds to minutes of GPU; KB or ~1 MB |
| 3 | Fit the token against contrast: same-scene counterfactual instructions with generated trajectories (MimicGen/DemoGen replay or scene edits), using the trained prompt as a CPG-style negative during fitting | CAST doubling; CounterAlign 0.19→0.51; CAG-VA beats CAG-TF ([LIBERO-CF](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17659)); per-task units escape LangGap's dilution | Pushes transfer past binding 0.9; makes the token encode the word, not the demo | LangGap's Change Target stays at 5-6% even with training data; new placements may need motion we cannot generate | Generation plus fitting; still KB |

| 5 | Add practice once the token gives nonzero success: Golden-Ticket noise search, a per-task Q head, or SARL-style prompt Q-learning | QPILOTS 26→76 on frozen pi0.5 ([QPILOTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14801)); SARL ~0→80 on 5 of 10 tasks ([SARL](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31958)) | High where the token yields partial success | 10^4 to 5×10^5 steps per task; sim-only; zero-support tasks will not move | Noise vector or small Q head |
| 6 | Report the cheap inference-time contrasts (CPG with the trained prompt as negative, CAG-TF) and pass@k | CAG-TF +9.7 grounding points; pass@k bounds every selection method ([SVA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03751)) | Small, but they are mandatory baselines, and pass@k decides where option 5 can apply | Low | 2× inference |

The remaining options rank lower for clear reasons. A language-only hierarchical planner hands unread words to a policy that ignores them ([Hi Robot](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19417)). World-model and video policies target motor coverage at roughly 10^5 GPU-hours ([GR00T N1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14734)). Full RL fine-tuning and VLM co-training both abandon the frozen premise, and merging per-task units without a router collapses to 0% ([MergeVLA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18810)).

**The two ReGuide IDs are two different papers that share a name, and both should be cited by full title so they are not conflated.** arXiv **2609.38616** is "Correcting WHERE, Preserving HOW: Compositional Generalization for Vision-Language-Action Models via Referential Guidance" by Zhang, Liu, Li, Song, Hariri, Ganguly, Yang, Ye, Grant, Chaudhary and Yin, submitted 29 September 2026. It is the training-free frozen-VLA wrapper described above, and its abstract reports gains of up to 56.8 pp in simulation and 75.0 pp on real robots ([abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38616)). arXiv **2606.28939** is "ReGuide: From Test-Time Guidance to Self-Improving Diffusion Policies" by Lin, Shakkottai, Kalathil and Kumar, submitted 27 June 2026. In that paper, phase-conditioned guidance steers corrective rollouts toward drifted but recoverable states, and fine-tuning then absorbs those rollouts back into the diffusion policy, for 1.3-7.7× success over the base policy on robomimic Can, Square, Transport and Tool Hang ([abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28939)). It is not a VLA method, its policy is not frozen and it does not address composition, so it competes only with a guide-then-absorb practice loop.

**The other close competitors fall into four groups.** LangGap ([LangGap](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.00592)), LIBERO-CF/CAG ([LIBERO-CF](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17659)) and DeLock/CPG ([DeLock](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23121)) own the diagnosis and the inference-time contrast baselines. Text-latent interpolation ([Li](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03500)), QPILOTS ([QPILOTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14801)) and SARL ([SARL](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31958)) report frozen-policy results on held-out LIBERO tasks. FOSIL ([FOSIL](https://fosilvla.github.io/)) and RA-VLA ([RA-VLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25585)) sit closest to our one-demo LoRA and token, and CORAL ([CORAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09298v1)) and MAP-VLA ([MAP-VLA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.09516)) sit closest to a token library. None of them reports a KB-scale per-task unit, a one-demo held-out result for a learned prefix, or cross-task transfer of a unit fitted on shared factors. Those three results form our defensible core.

## Conclusion

Industry and academia converge on a three-way split, and our results reproduce it. **Context selects among behaviors the policy already reads** (prompts, prefixes, retrieval, score sums); **external grounding fixes WHERE when the words go unread** (detectors, boxes, traces, subgoal images); and **weight changes add new motion** (LoRA, few-step updates, RL). Composing a frozen policy's own conditionals sits entirely in the first bucket. That is why it works on LIBERO-OOD, where both factors were contrasted in training, and fails in our k = 1 scenes. The factor token is more interesting than a composition device: Factored Diffusion Policies show that compositionality comes from sharing parameters, not from adding scores. A unit fitted on shared factors that transfers to a third task is exactly that sharing, compressed to 18 KB and grown one task at a time.

ReGuide should be treated as a component to beat or absorb, not a reason to stop. It shows that the frozen policy's HOW is worth preserving and that a WHERE channel unlocks it. It says nothing about bytes, streams, cross-task transfer or tasks that need new motion. The system to build would store WHERE plus binding in a few kilobytes per task, fall back to a rank-1 update only for new motion, and be measured on a compositional stream with no-policy controls. No published work, industry or academic, has built or measured that system.
