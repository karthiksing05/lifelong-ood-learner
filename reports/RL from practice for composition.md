# Failed rollouts hold the missing learning signal

At tens of rollouts per task, the methods with evidence behind them do not run policy-gradient RL through pi0.5. They do three things instead: reuse every recorded rollout, failures included, through a likelihood-free flow-matching loss on the per-task adapter; select or steer the frozen sampler with a cheap value probe; and spend new rollouts only where a composition stalls. Published on-policy RL for flow and token VLAs (RIPT-VLA, SimpleVLA-RL, piRL/RLinf) lifts one-demo LIBERO checkpoints to 92-99%. It takes roughly 10^3-10^5 rollouts per task to do so, on tasks and init-state pools the checkpoint was already fine-tuned on, and piRL's pi0.5 run trains the whole shared 300M action expert. No paper reports held-out LIBERO-90 tasks learned from a frozen prior at 100 rollouts or fewer. Our own ledger splits the first-try failures into two problems. **41 of 164 (25%) succeed on a fresh-noise retry**, which puts them within reach of selection (the zero-training composition's pass@3 is **0.707** against a pass@1 of **0.61**). **123 of 420 instances (29%) fail all three tries**, and **105 of those sit in 9 of the 21 tasks**, which points to the composition's geometry, not to bad luck. Success-only self-imitation reaches neither group. It throws away the negatives that break comparable plateaus elsewhere (PA-RL: 50% vs 90%; Q-Planning: 55%/30% vs 90%/80%), and LoRA rank 64 and full fine-tuning do no better than rank 16, so capacity is not the limit at LoRA size. The first experiment needs no new practice rollouts: retrain the same LoRA and the 258 KB fold with a DiffusionNFT-style negative-aware loss, with negatives localized by the alignment pointer, and score both against the round-2 LoRA (0.669) on init states 30-49. Statistical power fixes the design. At 20-30 rollouts, a per-task comparison only resolves effects of 26-37 points. Every hypothesis must therefore be tested pooled over the 21 tasks, where 420 paired episodes resolve about 7-8 points, and per-task adaptation must be limited to a handful of arms.

## The plateau hides two failure populations, and capacity is not one of them

**The failures split into a stochastic quarter and a structural remainder.** The zero-training composition solved **256 of 420** practice instances on its first try. We reran the 164 failures with two fresh policy seeds. Seed-0 reruns replay the episode exactly, because the simulator and sampler are deterministic, so they cannot serve as retries. The fresh seeds rescued **41 instances**, lifting pass@3 to **297/420 = 0.707** ([ledger m5_learn_stats](../ledger/m5_learn_stats/); pass@3 recomputed from the same outcomes). The other **123 instances failed all three tries**, and they cluster by task: tasks 42, 32, 1, 35, 40, 36, 52, 62 and 41 hold 18, 16, 12, 12, 11, 10, 9, 9 and 8 of them out of 20 each, **105 in total**. Four tasks have none. The first population is one a better sampler or selector reaches. The second is concentrated in whole tasks, which fits the ledger's earlier diagnosis that the composition "follows the demo's trajectory" while LoRA "learns closed-loop behaviour". An instance that fails three times from the same geometry needs new behavior, not more samples of the old one.

**Adding parameters does not help, and adding success-only rounds barely helps.** On the 14 tasks where all three were run from one demo, rank-16 LoRA scored **165/280**, rank-64 **167/280** and full fine-tuning **161/280** on unseen init states ([ledger m3_final](../ledger/m3_final/)). Practice then lifted the rank-16 LoRA from 252/420 (one demo) to **274/420** (demo plus round-0 successes) and to **281/420** after round 1's successes. That last step is 7 episodes out of 420, far below any detectable effect. The 258 KB adapter trained on the same data reached only **213/420** and **204/420**, so at the fold's size capacity does bind. Any gain from better signal has to be shown at both sizes. The shape is typical. On a real Cup-to-Rack task, iterated filtered BC stayed at **50%** while ranking with a Q-function and distilling reached **55%→90%** ([PA-RL](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06685)). Q-Planning reports filtered SFT on successes stalling at **55% and 30%**, while a Q-function retrained on both successes and failures, steering a frozen BC policy, reached **90% and 80%** with 20 episodes per iteration ([Q-Planning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21204)). In LLM RL, positive-only reinforcement raises pass@1 but loses diversity at high k, whereas negative-only reinforcement keeps it ([NSR vs PSR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01347)). MimicGen notes that generation success and the trained policy's performance are "not necessarily correlated" ([MimicGen](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.17596)). Imitating the program's own successes cannot teach behavior the program never produces on hard instances.

**Group-relative RL would discard most of our data.** Take the three composition outcomes per instance (first try plus two retries) as a group. **202 instances are all-success, 123 are all-fail, and only 95 (23%) are mixed.** RIPT-VLA and SimpleVLA-RL drop zero-advantage groups by design ([RIPT-VLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17016); [SimpleVLA-RL](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09674v1)), and SimpleVLA-RL reports that outcome-reward RL "yields only marginal improvements" when initial success is very low. Group RL at our budget therefore learns from under a quarter of the instances and never from the persistent 29%. Useful signal has to come from three other sources. The first is chunk-level progress inside failed rollouts, which the alignment pointer gives for free. The second is new rollouts started near the stall. The third is selection among samples the composition already produces.

## On-policy RL for flow VLAs costs 10^3-10^5 rollouts per task

The strongest LIBERO numbers come from likelihood-based on-policy RL, and every one of them starts from a checkpoint fine-tuned on the tasks it is then trained on. Starting from one demo per task, **SimpleVLA-RL lifts OpenVLA-OFT from 48.9 to 96.9** on average and LIBERO-Long from 17.3 to 91.7. It runs RL on 500 scenarios per suite, which appear to be the evaluation init states ([SimpleVLA-RL](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09674v1)). **piRL lifts pi0.5 from 77.1 to 98.3** (Flow-Noise) after SFT on 40 trajectories for 40 tasks. It evaluates on all 500 init states per suite without stating a train/eval split, and it freezes the VLM while fully training the 300M action expert ([piRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25889v3)). RIPT-VLA's one-demo cross-scenario result, **about 4% to 97% in 15 iterations**, is the closest analogue to our regime. It implies roughly 2,400 rollouts for one task (the notes' arithmetic from B = 160), which is 50-100 times our budget ([RIPT-VLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17016)).

The mechanics fit poorly as well. A deterministic 10-step Euler sampler gives no likelihood. piRL gets one either by making each Euler step Gaussian with a learned noise head (Flow-Noise, from [ReinFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22094)) or by converting the ODE to a marginal-preserving SDE with σ_t = a·sqrt(t/(1−t)) (Flow-SDE, from [Flow-GRPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05470)). The PoE guide would then have to enter every transition mean. Exploration noise also costs practice success: pi0's stochastic success fell from **62.3% to 46.6%** as noise rose from 0.2 to 0.8, and low noise was unstable, reaching 73.1 after 100 RL steps at 0.2 against 98.1 at 0.8 ([piRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25889v3)). piRL's pi0.5 configuration (predict 10, replan every 5) matches ours, so its chunk-level credit assignment carries over; its budget does not. Forgetting sets a further constraint. Single-task SFT cut pi0.5's prior LIBERO skills from **0.77 to 0.23**, with PPO clipping preserving more ([ConSFT](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08879)). Sequential GRPO through LoRA forgot almost nothing (**NBT 0.3**), against 40.9 without LoRA ([Simple Recipe](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11653v3)). The per-task LoRA or fold is the right container. On-policy RL is the wrong update rule at tens of rollouts.

None of the results below is on held-out LIBERO-90 tasks from one demo with a frozen prior.

## Likelihood-free, failure-aware losses drop into the fold unchanged

Methods that reuse the flow-matching loss avoid the likelihood problem entirely. They need only the recorded observation, the executed chunk and an outcome label, so the guided Euler sampler stays as it is. **DiffusionNFT** defines implicit positive and negative velocities around the reference, v⁺ = (1−β)v_old + βv_θ and v⁻ = (1+β)v_old − βv_θ. It trains r‖v⁺−v‖² + (1−r)‖v⁻−v‖² with r ∈ [0,1], needs no likelihood and no CFG, works with any sampler, and reached GenEval 0.98 in 1k steps where FlowGRPO needed over 5k ([DiffusionNFT](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16117)). With r ≡ 1 the loss reduces to our current filtered-BC objective, which makes it the smallest change that adds failures. **AWM** weights the same loss by advantage ([AWM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25050)). **FPO** puts exp(ΔCFM loss) inside a PPO clip, so it too is indifferent to how the rollout was sampled ([FPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21053); [FPO++](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02481)). Robotics evidence at small budgets points the same way. **ExpReS-VLA** added a failure buffer and a contrastive loss to an OpenVLA LoRA and went **77.9→88.7 with 50 rollouts per task**, our budget ([ExpReS-VLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.06202)). **RedFlow** scored each chunk by local progress change plus outcome and pulled negative chunks toward the centroid of positive chunks at matching progress. On pi0 it went **56.2→68.2 with 1,536 rollouts per suite**, about 150 per task, 5.9 points above AWR, and matched PPO, GRPO and DDPO runs that used 13-24K rollouts ([RedFlow](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27782), unreviewed).

**The alignment pointer replaces the most expensive part of advantage conditioning.** RECAP (pi\*0.6) conditions the policy on "Advantage: positive/negative". Its value function is a 670M distributional model of negative steps-to-completion, normalized per task, and the advantage is binarized at a per-task 30th percentile ([RECAP](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.14759v1)). Our pointer gives (demo length − pointer)/demo length at every query at no cost, so a chunk's pointer advance minus its expected advance is RECAP's advantage without a value model that tens of rollouts could never train. CFGRL supplies the inference-time knob: with π ∝ π̂·p(optimal)^w, raising w improves return beyond filtered BC and AWR until a task-specific optimum ([CFGRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23458)). DIPOLE and AFIL turn this into positive and negative velocity fields combined by (1+w)v⁺ − wv⁻. AFIL does it on pi0.5 with an adaptive strength α(1−cos(v⁺,v⁻)) and reports LIBERO **96.9→98.4** from a full-data start ([DIPOLE](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.00898v3); [AFIL](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08434v1), unreviewed).

Negatives have to be localized. Most chunks in a failed LIBERO rollout (the approach, the pre-grasp) match chunks from successful ones, so a trajectory-level negative penalizes good behavior. RedFlow and PCM both localize before penalizing ([PCM](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16154)). The stall is not always the cause, either. RePO-VLA separates "terminal drift" from the causal prefix, U-GROW truncates at the first high-uncertainty state, and UR-VC finds normalized demo time a noisy progress proxy in contact-rich tasks ([RePO-VLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09410v1); [U-GROW](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22879); [UR-VC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12892)). The practical rule is to label a window of one to three chunks before the stall as negative, sweep the window width, and leave the rest of each failed rollout unlabeled. We have **about 557 recorded failed episodes**: 164 from round 0, 202 from round 1 and 191 from round 2. The fresh-seed retries kept only their outcomes. The recorder stores the 8-d robot state, so the pointer can be recomputed offline for LoRA rollouts too. Two caveats remain unmeasured. First, the rollouts come from the guided sampler, so they are off-policy for the adapter; this is equally true of today's success-only training. Second, no source says how many negatives a rank-2 or rank-16 adapter needs before v⁻ is reliable.

## Steering and selection reach the quarter of failures that luck already fixes

Frozen generative policies contain far more success than their first sample shows. **SVA measured pass@1 33% against pass@32 92%** for frozen VLAs ([SVA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03751)). Our composition's pass@3 of 0.707 against pass@1 of 0.61 is the same effect on a smaller scale. Selection methods convert that headroom into success without touching weights, and their whole difficulty is the scorer. On our exact checkpoint family, **QPILOTS** kept the public pi0.5-LIBERO checkpoint frozen on 6 held-out LIBERO-90 tasks and raised mean success from **26 to 76** by adding a rescaled critic gradient, taken at the Tweedie clean-action estimate, at every Euler step. DSRL reached 58, best-of-5 with the same critic 40 and EXPO 39, all after **5×10^5 environment steps** ([QPILOTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14801)). The ranking at tens of rollouts is unknown. A per-task critic cannot be trained from 20-30 rollouts. The cheapest credible scorer is a probe on frozen pi0.5 features. Such probes predict success-like value with **R² ≈ 0.74** on a demo split and about 0.55 on a task split, order same-task pairs correctly about 92% of the time, and lifted a push-plate task from **26.7% to 44.3%** by selecting among K = 16 candidates ([frozen-VLA probe](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28527)). SAFE finds that VLA features encode success and failure generically across tasks ([SAFE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09937)).

**Noise steering is the lowest-budget RL with pi-family results, but our guide threatens its lever.** DSRL trains a small SAC actor whose action is the sampler's initial noise, one 32-d vector tiled across the chunk. It lifted `pi0_libero` on a held-out LIBERO-90 task from **about 20% to about 100% in about 10,000 online samples** (read from a figure; roughly 25-70 episodes by the notes' inference), and real Franka tasks in 65-80 episodes ([DSRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799v2); [dsrl_pi0](https://github.com/nakamotoo/dsrl_pi0)). SARL steers pi0.5 through cached language prompts and reports held-out LIBERO-10 tasks rising from near 0 **to as much as 80% in 60-100 episodes**. It also finds action-space steering works only "when the base policy already exhibits close to successful behaviors" ([SARL](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31958)), which at 61% describes most of our tasks. StructRL, however, shows that noise injected into the flow chain gets "diluted" by the remaining denoising steps ([StructRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15139)). Our PoE guide pulls every Euler step toward a teacher with variance 1e-3, so an initial-noise change is likely to be pulled out in guided dimensions. How much the executed chunk moves when the initial noise changes has to be measured before any noise actor is trained. Two more cautions apply. BOKBO finds that disagreement among K perturbed samples correlates at 0.98 with the noise hyperparameter and only at the noise floor with actual safety violations ([BOKBO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30660)), so sample spread is not a confidence score. And our ledger shows that **rerunning a success with its stored noise seed replays it 98.4% of the time** (252/256, [ledger m5_learn_stats](../ledger/m5_learn_stats/)), a simulator-only trick: any per-instance selection has to be judged on unseen init states with fresh seeds.

Residual RL is out of budget. ResFiT needs 75-150k simulation steps, PLD 250k steps per LIBERO-90 task, and DSRL reports that ResiP could not improve pi0 at all ([ResFiT](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19301v2); [PLD](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.00091v1)). Learning sampler knobs is cheap, however. ELASTIC trained a meta-policy over a frozen pi0.5's denoising stride and sample count with **10 offline plus 20 online rollouts per task** ([ELASTIC](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31132)). GPC's composition-weight search needed **200 rollouts per weight** to show gains of 2-7.6 points ([GPC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01068)), which is why knob tuning has to be pooled across tasks. ITPS found that product-form steering by MCMC keeps actions in distribution better than sum-form gradient guidance ([ITPS](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16627v2)). A value signal should therefore enter our sampler as one more factor in the product, and not as an added gradient.

## Program repair only works once the program shrinks to a few choices per phase

Our program assigns each frame and each action dimension one of 8 leaf chunks. That is thousands of discrete variables, and binary feedback from tens of rollouts cannot identify them. Reward-driven search succeeds at this budget only with few parameters started from a demonstration. PoWER learned ball-in-a-cup on a real arm in **about 75 iterations** from an imitation initialization ([PoWER](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2008/file/7647966b7343c29048673252e490f736-Paper.pdf)). Carvalho et al. show that local search with edits defined in program space beats CEM in a learned program embedding, which stops at local maxima more often ([Carvalho et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12166)). For us the native edits are one phase's leaf rank, one phase's guide weight and one fold-window edge. The pointer names the phase to edit, much as a failing unit test names the function to debug in program repair ([REFLECT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15724); [Self-Debugging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05128)). Choosing among a few discrete arms is a best-arm identification problem, where top-two Thompson sampling reaches the optimal posterior convergence rate ([Russo](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.08448)), and logged rollouts can warm-start the posteriors as off-policy evaluation warm-starts AOPS ([AOPS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10251)). The notes' binomial arithmetic sets the limit: separating 0.1 from 0.4 takes about 32 rollouts per arm, while 0.6 against 0.7 takes about 356. Per-task search can only find rescues. Shared knobs have to be tuned pooled across tasks.

**Resets let practice attack the persistent 29%, but the recorder must change first.** Several methods restart practice where it fails: branching from the first high-uncertainty state (U-GROW on pi0.5: **LIBERO average 89.1 vs 87.7** with uniform starts), a reverse curriculum from demo states (RFCL), hybrid rollouts that capture recovery behavior (PLD), and shared-prefix groups cloned from simulator state (Z-1) ([U-GROW](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22879); [RFCL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03379); [PLD](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.00091v1); [Z-1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31846)). Each produces nonzero success on sub-problems where full-episode success is zero, and those sub-problems are where group RL and self-imitation get no signal. `experiments/libero_client.py` records the 8-d end-effector state, both images and the executed actions, but not the MuJoCo simulator state, so a mid-episode state cannot be restored directly from the recordings. Seed-0 reruns replay episodes at 98.4%, so replaying a recorded action sequence from its init state should rebuild the state at any step. That needs a one-line check against the recorded 8-d states, and the recorder should start saving the simulator state. Resets are a practice-time affordance of simulation. The learned artifact never uses them.

**Geometry is the lever the persistent failures point to, and our own DemoDiffusion result warns against the easy version.** Every one-demo method that generalizes across object poses makes the contact segments object-relative. It applies T_new = T_obj,new · T_obj,demo⁻¹ · T_ee,demo to the contact segment and replans or interpolates the free-space motion. DemoGen raised one-demo real-world success from **11.0% to 74.6%** this way ([DemoGen](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16932); [MimicGen](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.17596)). DemoDiffusion reports **83.8%** for a retargeted prior refined by a pretrained diffusion policy, against 13.8% for the policy and 52.5% for retargeting alone ([DemoDiffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20668)). Our DemoDiffusion baseline on held-out LIBERO-90 scored only **36/420, and 58/420 in its state variant** ([ledger m3_final](../ledger/m3_final/)). That arm fed the demo path as the prior without any object-pose transform, so it tests "demo as prior", not "object-relative demo as prior". The object-relative version remains untested here. It needs object poses, which sim state provides as privileged information. A paper would have to disclose that or estimate poses from images (as DINOBot and IMOP do).

Two routes to library growth look attractive and need guarding. PRISE's BPE over quantized action codes raised 5-shot LIBERO-Long from **43.5 to 52.7** ([PRISE](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.10450)), which suggests compressing successful leaf-ID sequences into macro-leaves. Voyager-style libraries store only verified programs ([Voyager](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16291)). Our ledger shows, though, that consolidating stored successful compositions left new-instance success at the first composition's level, and that recall memory cost 0.045. Library growth has to be judged on unseen init states from the start. Hindsight relabeling applies in LIBERO-90 with exact labels. Scenes host several tasks, so a failed rollout's final simulator state can be checked against sibling tasks' BDDL predicates and against the target's sub-goal predicates, avoiding the "semantically unrelated" relabels that limit VLM-based LfH ([LfH](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09042v1)).

## Seven experiments, ranked by information per rollout

**Power decides what can be claimed.** With paired binary outcomes, the minimum detectable difference at α = 0.05 (two-sided) and 80% power is about sqrt(7.84ψ/n), where n is the number of paired episodes and ψ the fraction of discordant pairs. Our comparisons show ψ between 0.23 and 0.35 (for example 59/256 discordant for consolidated vs retry). The table is our arithmetic, uses a normal approximation that is rough at n = 20, and ignores the extra variance from clustering by task that the two-level bootstrap in `m3_stats` accounts for.

|---|---|---|---|
| One task, 20 init states | 20 | 31 pts | 37 pts |
| One task, 30 init states | 30 | 26 pts | 30 pts |
| The 9 concentrated-failure tasks | 180 | 10 pts | 12 pts |

The ledger agrees with this table. One-demo LoRA to demo-plus-successes LoRA was +0.052 at 420 pairs with p = 0.069, right at the edge, and round 2 over round 1 was +0.017, invisible. Per-task tuning at 20-30 rollouts can only detect rescues of about 30 points. Every method claim therefore needs a pooled, paired, fresh-seed comparison on states 30-49. LIBERO fixes 50 init states per task, which caps held-out n at 20 once 30 go to practice. Sampling new placements from the BDDL regions and saving the simulator state would lift that cap, but it needs checking against LIBERO's reset. Costs below assume our logged throughput: 30-episode runs took 746-1,182 s on one A40 ([ledger m6_round2](../ledger/m6_round2/)), about **120 episodes per A40-hour**, so 420 evaluation episodes cost about 3.5 A40-hours. Adapter training times are estimates. The seven experiments total roughly 80 A40-hours, about one day on the lab's 4 GPUs.

### 1. Retrain the same adapters with localized negatives, using data already on disk

This is the cheapest direct test of the question behind every learning-from-practice result so far: whether failures carry the missing signal. Take the recorded episodes of round 0 and round 1 (1,050 episodes, 366 of them failures), the same rounds the round-2 LoRA learned from. Recompute the pointer offline from the recorded 8-d states. Label as positive the demo and every chunk of a successful episode. Label as negative the chunks in a window of one to three queries before each failed episode's stall, and leave the rest of each failed episode unlabeled. Train with the DiffusionNFT loss against the frozen reference velocity. It equals the current filtered-BC loss when r ≡ 1, so the control is exact. Train three arms: rank-16 LoRA, 258 KB fold, and an AWM variant weighted by the pointer advance relative to its per-task mean. The existing `lora_r16L2` scores (281/420) are then the exact success-only control, provided seeds and init states match. A version that adds round 2's rollouts (1,680 episodes, about 557 failures) needs the success-only `r16L3` control completed on all 21 tasks, since it has been scored on only 2. The hypothesis passes if the LoRA arm exceeds 0.669 by at least the 7-8-point MDE. The fold arm is scored against the 204/420 of the equal-bytes adapter trained on the same data, which tests whether negatives close the 15-18-point storage gap. The design follows ExpReS (+10.8 at 50 rollouts per task), RedFlow (+12 at about 150 per task) and Q-Planning's stall-breaking. Two results would each be informative. A null would say that the missing signal lives in states practice never visits, which is Experiment 2's territory. A gain would name the plateau's cause. The window width is a nuisance parameter: choose it on the development task split and freeze it before scoring states 30-49.

### 2. Branch at the stall to locate what rescues the persistent 29%

First confirm that replaying a recorded action sequence from its init state reproduces the recorded 8-d states, and add simulator-state saving to the recorder. Then, for each of the 123 all-fail instances, restore the state one to two queries before the stall and run eight short branches: four fresh noise seeds, guide weight ×0.5 and ×2, the next-ranked leaf for the stalled phase, and the guide released to the prior. A branch counts as a rescue if the pointer passes the stall point and the episode then succeeds. The rescue rate per variant, measured on 123 instances to about ±9 points, is a direct diagnosis. Fresh-noise rescues send the budget to Experiments 1 and 3. Leaf or weight rescues send it to Experiment 6. No rescue from nearby states points to geometry (Experiment 5) or to an earlier cause, which is exactly the lag between cause and stall that RePO-VLA and U-GROW describe. Rescued branch segments then enter Experiment 1's training set as recovery positives, with failed branches as localized negatives, and are tested on the 9 concentrated tasks' states 30-49 (180 paired, MDE about 11 points). This is the PLD, U-GROW and RaC pattern without a human. It costs about 3 A40-hours for the branches because each covers only the tail of an episode.

### 3. Select among the composition's own samples with a frozen-feature probe

Fit a logistic or small-MLP probe on frozen pi0.5 features (vision encoder first, then the VLM prefix) to predict whether the pointer advances in the next query, trained on all recorded episodes. Gate on offline AUROC of at least 0.7 on held-out practice episodes before spending evaluation rollouts. At each query, draw N = 8 candidates through the unchanged guided sampler with fresh initial noises and execute the argmax, or Q-Planning's softmax-weighted average. Run three arms: composition plus fold with selection, the round-2 LoRA with selection, and a zero-noise (ε = 0) control from FPO++. Our earlier M1 finding that pi0.5's conditional is nearly deterministic predicts a small effect for ε = 0, but episode outcomes clearly depend on the seed, so the arm is worth its 3.5 A40-hours. Episode-level pass@3 (0.707) bounds what perfect whole-episode selection over three samples would give. Chunk-level selection can exceed that bound because it composes choices across queries, but it inherits the probe's errors. The hypothesis is a gain of at least 7 points for the composition-plus-fold arm, which would make selection the storage-cheap path, since a probe costs kilobytes. Report per-task probes and one shared probe separately. SAFE suggests the shared probe generalizes, but our shared fold failed badly, so the shared version has to earn its place.

### 4. Condition the fold on outcome and tune one guidance weight per task

Keep the round-2 LoRA as the positive adapter A⁺. Fit a negative adapter A⁻ on Experiment 1's localized negatives. Sample with v = v⁺ + w(v⁺ − v⁻) inside the PoE, or with AFIL's adaptive strength α(1−cos(v⁺,v⁻)). This costs one extra action-expert pass per Euler step while the VLM prefix is shared. Sweep w ∈ {0, 0.5, 1, 2} plus the adaptive form on a fixed development split of tasks (for example the 6 development tasks, 120 episodes per setting). Confirm the chosen setting on the other 15 tasks (300 paired). CFGRL predicts gains that rise with w and then fall, so an interior optimum is itself a test of the theory. The stored cost is a second adapter and one scalar. A RECAP-style alternative appends "Advantage: positive/negative" to the prompt and trains with 10% dropout, but it doubles the VLM pass, and the two-adapter form isolates the same effect more cheaply. The main risk is a poorly fit v⁻: with a few hundred negatives, a large w amplifies noise. Run this after Experiment 1 so that it reuses the negative labels and shows whether guidance adds anything beyond the loss.

### 5. Retarget contact-window guide targets to the current object pose

In the contact windows (−30/+10 frames around gripper events), integrate the borrowed deltas to absolute end-effector poses and apply T_new = T_obj,new · T_obj,demo⁻¹ · T_ee,demo, with the manipulated object read from sim state. Difference the result back to deltas and use it as the guide target. In free space, lower w or interpolate to the transformed segment's start. Test on the 9 concentrated tasks (180 paired, about 11 points detectable), and add a zero-rollout variant that picks leaves by nearest source-object pose instead of maximum coverage, following MimicGen's note that source-segment choice matters for some tasks. The hypothesis passes if the persistent failures drop and a 420-episode confirmation holds. The privileged-pose caveat has to be stated in any paper. Our DemoDiffusion arm's failure (36/420) is not evidence against this experiment, because that arm never transformed by object pose. Experiment 2's diagnosis should decide whether to build this at all: if nearby-state branches already rescue these instances, geometry is not the bottleneck.

### 6. Repair the stalled phase with top-two Thompson sampling

Reparameterize each program into three to five phases (gripper events, or AWE-style waypoints). Each phase gets one leaf rank tied across dimensions or within translation, rotation and gripper groups, a guide-weight multiplier from {0.5, 1, 2}, and fold on/off. Freeze every phase before the stall and run top-two Thompson sampling over at most three to six arms for the stalled phase, pruned by Experiment 2's rescue data and warm-started from logged outcomes. Stop when the best arm's posterior exceeds 0.8 or after 30 rollouts per task. Tune shared continuous knobs (teacher variance, window edges, stall threshold) with CMA-ES pooled across tasks, where each evaluation is a batch of about 20 random (task, init) pairs, since effects of 5-10 points need hundreds of rollouts per configuration, as GPC's did. The stored cost is bytes per task, the cheapest artifact in this report. At 30 rollouts per task, only rescues of about 30 points or more can be identified, so this experiment sits behind the diagnostics that would say whether such rescues exist.

### 7. Steer the initial noise only where the guide lets it matter

First measure how far the executed chunk moves per unit change of the initial noise, under the guide at w = 1 and with the guide released, on recorded observations. This costs no rollouts. If guided dimensions barely move, confine steering to released windows and fold windows. There, run CEM or a small SAC actor over one tied 32-d noise vector per phase with DSRL's box bound, warm-started by inverting teacher chunks to noise in the manner of FlowDAgger. Give it about 40 rollouts per task on the 9 concentrated tasks and evaluate on 180 paired episodes. DSRL is the most on-target precedent (a held-out LIBERO-90 task, frozen pi0), but it reported about 10k steps from a 20% base, and QPILOTS placed DSRL below critic guidance on pi0.5. The dilution risk is specific to our guide. This ranks last for those reasons, and it is the one experiment whose stored artifact (32 floats per phase) could replace the fold outright.

**What not to run at this budget.** Flow-SDE PPO/GRPO or RIPT-style LOOP needs 10-100× the rollouts. Updating the shared action expert damages the prior (0.77→0.23 under SFT on pi0.5). World-model RL runs on 4-32 GPUs ([WMPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09515)). VLM progress models should not serve as labels, because simulator success is ground truth and paraphrases can flip reward-model verdicts ([ROBORMBENCH](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05401)). LLM-driven program repair does not fit a program made of numeric leaf choices.

## Conclusion

The literature's large one-demo gains come from rollout volume. They do not come from any algorithm we could run at 20-30 rollouts per task, and the methods that work at our scale share one property: they extract more signal from each rollout. Our pipeline already holds the pieces that make on-policy RL expensive elsewhere. The alignment pointer is a free value function where RECAP trains a 670M model. The composition is a proposal distribution with measurable headroom (pass@3 0.707), which is what selection methods need. The deterministic simulator allows branching from any recorded step once the recorder saves state. The plateau at 0.669 is therefore most likely a labeling problem: 557 recorded failures have never entered training, and the ledger rules out capacity as the explanation at LoRA size.

The persistent 29% is a different problem. It is concentrated in nine tasks, it survives fresh noise, and neither round of self-imitation touched it. It needs behavior the composition does not currently propose, either recovered from nearby states or produced by retargeting the contact geometry. The storage claim at the center of the thesis depends on these experiments as well. Selection with a probe, per-phase repair and noise steering all store kilobytes or less, while the LoRA that currently leads stores 18.7 MB. If negatives let the 258 KB fold close part of its 15-18-point gap, practice becomes evidence for the thesis's cheap-storage claim, not an argument against it.
