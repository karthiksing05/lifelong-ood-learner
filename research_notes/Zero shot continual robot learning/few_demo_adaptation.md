# Few-demonstration adaptation of robot manipulation policies to new tasks (2023-2026)

Reference setting used throughout ("our setting"): a frozen pi0.5 VLA fails held-out LIBERO-90 tasks, mostly because it does not read object/relation words it was never trained to contrast in that scene. One-demo results on 420 episodes: rank-16 LoRA (18.7 MB) 252/420 = 60.0%; 18 KB learned prefix (key/value) token 140/420 = 33.3% (matches LoRA where the failure is reading, falls short where the task needs new motion); STRAP retrieval (own implementation) 168/420 = 40.0%. These numbers are the user's own and are not re-sourced here.

Caveat for the report writer: no paper found uses our exact protocol (frozen pi0.5, one demo, held-out LIBERO-90 tasks). Every comparison below crosses suites, base models, demo counts or held-out definitions, so treat cross-paper numbers as orientation, not head-to-head.

## Key Question 1: Per family, what is the core idea, which works represent it, what do they report, what limits them, what does a new task cost, and how does it relate to our setting?

### Takeaway
Five families exist. Weight-free in-context and retrieval methods only work after a VLA has been trained to use context (RICL, RA-VLA, ReCAP). With that training they roughly match small-data fine-tuning on familiar motions and fail on new motions. Data-generation methods multiply one demo into hundreds, but only along spatial and object-pose axes. Few-step or low-rank weight updates on pi0.5 work well from one demo (FOSIL). Naive merging of VLA LoRAs collapses to zero success.

### Cited Findings

#### A. Retrieval-augmented policies (retrieve prior data, then train)
- STRAP core idea: it encodes prior trajectories and the target demos with a vision foundation model (DINOv2), cuts them into sub-trajectories, matches them with subsequence DTW (S-DTW), and trains a policy on the union of retrieved and target data. Authors are Memmel, Berg, Chen, Gupta and Francis (ICLR 2025). — [STRAP arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15182); [ICLR 2025 PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/a06e129e01e0d2ef853e9ff67b911360-Paper-Conference.pdf); [project page](https://weirdlabuw.github.io/strap/)
- STRAP LIBERO setup: 5 target demos per LIBERO-10 task, with retrieval from 4,500 LIBERO-90 trajectories. Average LIBERO-10 success: BC (target only) 37.9%, fine-tuning (FT) 51.7%, multi-task (MT) 37.7%, Behavior Retrieval (BR) 33.4%, FlowRetrieval (FR) 33.1%, D-S 28.4%, D-T 41.4%, STRAP (K=100) 45.6%, STRAP (best K per task) 58.1%. Fine-tuning (51.7%) beats STRAP at fixed K=100 (45.6%). STRAP wins only with per-task K tuning. — [STRAP arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15182)
- STRAP real world: 3 target demos per task. With DROID retrieval it scores 56.81 / 63.04 / 45.45% on Table / Sink / Stove, against FT 28.0 / 8.69 / 22.72%. — [STRAP arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15182)
- STRAP cost: embedding DROID (18.9M timesteps) with DINOv2 takes ~26 h, a one-time cost. S-DTW retrieval over 76k trajectories takes ~300 s per deployment. Training a policy takes 35 ± 4 min per model, so every new task needs a newly trained policy and access to the full prior dataset. Sub-trajectory retrieval adds +4.1% over full-trajectory retrieval. — [STRAP arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15182)
- Behavior Retrieval learns a latent space of state-action pairs and retrieves the prior samples nearest the target data. SAILOR learns skill-based latent embeddings and retrieves the nearest prior data. FlowRetrieval retrieves by optical-flow motion similarity and also uses flow to guide policy learning. — [Data Retrieval with Importance Weights (OpenReview PDF)](https://openreview.net/pdf/10de019e4cee5f7f9ef71903e696bf42906ca575.pdf); [FlowRetrieval arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.16944); [Importance-weighted retrieval arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.01657)
- COLLAGE (CoRL 2025) fuses several retrieval cues (visual, shape, language). Each cue's weight comes from how well a policy trained on that cue's subset predicts the target demo actions. It reports +5.1% over existing retrieval and multi-task approaches across 10 LIBERO tasks, and +16.6% across 6 real tasks with DROID retrieval. — [COLLAGE arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01131)
- DataMIL (ICLR 2026) uses datamodels (regression or metagradient estimators) to score each prior sample by its influence on target-task policy success, then selects the top samples. It reports gains over prior data-selection methods on 60+ sim and real tasks, including selection from Open X-Embodiment. — [DataMIL arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.09603); [project page](https://robin-lab.cs.utexas.edu/datamodels4imitation/)

#### B. Retrieval at inference / memory-augmented VLAs (no retraining per task)
- REGENT (ICLR 2025 oral) is a semi-parametric agent: a transformer policy trained on sequences of queries and their retrieved nearest neighbours. It adapts to unseen robotics and game environments in context, without fine-tuning. A plain 1-nearest-neighbour retrieval agent is described as "a surprisingly strong baseline". REGENT claims up to 3x fewer parameters and an order of magnitude fewer pre-training datapoints than prior generalist agents. — [REGENT arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04759); [project page](https://kaustubhsridhar.github.io/regent-research/)
- RICL (CoRL 2025) post-trains pi0-FAST-DROID for in-context learning, because "VLAs pre-trained with imitation learning objectives do not naturally acquire ICL abilities". Post-training uses 400 demos across 20 pick-and-place tasks. At test time, 10–20 demos of the new task fill a retrieval buffer. DINOv2 retrieves demo chunks into the context, and an action-interpolation layer blends retrieved actions by embedding distance. — [RICL arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02062v1); [RICL project](https://ricl-vla.github.io/)
- RICL results (real Franka, full-task success): pi0-FAST-DROID zero-shot 2.5%; RICL in-context with no weight update 31.25%; pi0-FAST fine-tuned on 20 demos 31.67%; RICL fine-tuned on 20 demos 61.67%. Retrieval takes <1 ms with FAISS, and inference is 1.33x slower than the base model. Stated limits: it covers mostly pick-and-place-like tasks, struggles with substantially new motions (e.g., a tennis forehand), and cannot handle large viewpoint or scene changes relative to the retrieval data. — [RICL arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02062v1)
- RA-VLA (arXiv 2608.25585) keeps the GR00T N1.5 backbone frozen and trains a 2-layer transformer retrieval encoder plus an action head. Retrieved 16-step expert segments go into 8 cross-attention layers, one segment per layer, instead of the prompt. A DTW-based behavioural-alignment loss trains the retriever, and a margin loss forces the policy to use the retrieved context. — [RA-VLA arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25585)
- RA-VLA results: on LIBERO (4 suites, 3 demos per unseen task) it reaches 38.5% average success, against 20.9% for the best baseline (RICL-style with an off-the-shelf SigLIP 2 retriever). The vanilla VLA scores near zero (reported as "0.017"). On a real UR5e (4 tasks, 4 demos each, leave-one-out) it reaches 56.25%, against 35.42% for RICL and 8.33% for the vanilla VLA. Storage is ~1 KB key + ~2 MB multimodal features + ~1 KB actions per 16-step segment. Retrieval takes ~0.18 ms on an A100, and latency stays flat in K, whereas RICL/RAEA grow linearly. Stated limits: absolute success stays low compared with in-distribution, results are sensitive to hyperparameters, and only flow-matching VLAs were tested. — [RA-VLA arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25585)
- ReCAP, "Retrieve, Don't Retrain" (arXiv 2606.15631), builds on Cosmos Policy, a world-action model. The action is the retrieved pool action chunk plus a learned embodiment-specific residual, and the pool can be human-hand demos. New tasks are added by appending demos, with no parameter updates; fine-tuning Cosmos Policy costs "roughly 24 GPU-hours per task". Results: RoboTwin 2.0 unseen tasks 31.5% (vs 4.0% no-pool baseline, 10.0% co-training, 26.0% retrieval-only replay); PushT unseen angles 34.9% (vs 6.0%; pi0.5 + retrieval 25.1%); real robot with 10 human-hand demos per held-out task 80% / 30% (vs 10% / 0%). Limits: query and pool must share an end-effector action space, and training requires paired data. — [ReCAP arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15631)
- RAEA puts retrieved expert segments directly into the policy input, according to RA-VLA's baseline description. — [RA-VLA arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25585)
- MemoryVLA (ICLR 2026) adds a perceptual-cognitive memory bank (retrieval, gate fusion, token-merge consolidation) for temporal context within an episode. It reaches 96.5% across 5 LIBERO suites, including 95.6% on LIBERO-90 and 93.4% on LIBERO-10, and 84.0% on 12 real tasks. These are trained-task results, not new-task adaptation. — [MemoryVLA arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.19236); [project page](https://shihao1895.github.io/MemoryVLA/)
- MAP-VLA freezes a pi0 model (first LoRA-fine-tuned on LIBERO) and learns stage-specific soft prompts. Each prompt V_k is added element-wise to the base prompt tokens and trained with the flow-matching loss on demos. At run time, trajectory-similarity matching retrieves the matching stage prompt. LIBERO-Long: 83.4% vs pi0 76.4% (+7.0). Real long-horizon complete success: 48.3% vs 23.3%. The tasks are the training tasks, not held-out ones. — [MAP-VLA arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.09516)
- MT3 ("Learning a Thousand Tasks in a Day") splits each task into an alignment phase (pose estimation + motion planning) and an interaction phase (open-loop replay of the retrieved demo). It learned 1,000 tasks from one demo each in under 24 h of demonstrator time, with 78.25% success on seen and 68% on unseen tasks. With 3 demos per task it beats methods given 50, and it shows "an order of magnitude improvement in data efficiency" below 10 demos per task. Failures split into retrieval 22.3%, pose estimation 23.9%, segmentation 19.5% and grasp 20.2%, and closed-loop or millimetre-precision tasks are hard for it. — [MT3 arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10110)

#### C. In-context imitation / prompting with demonstrations
- ICRT is a causal transformer trained by next-token prediction on sensorimotor trajectories, with no language and no reward. At test time, human teleoperation demos of the new task serve as the prompt, and the parameters stay fixed. It reports 76.7% on pick-and-place and 93.3% on poking on a Franka robot (secondary summary), and outperforms robot next-token models on unseen tasks. — [ICRT arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15980); [summary with numbers](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2408.15980)
- Instant Policy (ICLR 2025) puts the demos, the current point-cloud observation and future actions into one heterogeneous graph and denoises only the action nodes by diffusion. It is trained on ~700K procedurally generated "pseudo-demonstrations" (2.5M steps, ~5 days on one RTX 3080-Ti; 69M–178M parameters). At test time it uses 1–2 demos. RLBench (24 tasks): 0.71 with pseudo-demos only and 0.82 with extra domain data, vs BC-Z* 0.36/0.43, Vid2Robot* 0.38/0.37, GPT2* 0.34/0.31. Real (16 tasks): 88.75% vs 34.38–40.63%. Limits: it needs segmented point clouds, handles short-horizon tasks, and lacks precision for very tight tolerances. — [Instant Policy arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12633); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=cD4Qop8N8X)
- KAT (RSS 2024) turns visual keypoints and action trajectories into text tokens for an off-the-shelf LLM (GPT-4 Turbo), with no training. It learns behaviours from as few as 10 demos and matches or beats diffusion policies in the low-data regime on real tasks. — [KAT arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19578); [project page](https://www.robot-learning.uk/keypoint-action-tokens)
- RoboPrompt (ICRA 2025) prompts a text-only LLM with keyframes, end-effector actions and object poses written as text, with no training. It reports stronger results than zero-shot and ICL baselines in sim and real; numbers were not extracted. — [RoboPrompt arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12782)
- X-ICM / AGNOSTOS (NeurIPS 2025): an LLM sees K=18 demos of seen tasks, chosen from 3,600 by a dynamics-diffusion similarity model and written as text (object positions on a 100^3 grid, key actions, 7D end-effector states). It then predicts actions for unseen tasks, with zero demos of the target task. AGNOSTOS covers 23 unseen RLBench tasks (13 Level-1 that share some semantics, 10 Level-2 that are fully novel), trained on 18 seen tasks × 200 demos. Overall: X-ICM-72B 30.1%, X-ICM-7B 23.5%, pi0 17.5%, VoxPoser 15.6%. Level-1: 37.6% vs pi0 21.7%. Level-2: 20.3% vs SAM2Act 15.9%. "All prior models completely fail on at least eight of the 23 tasks." Limits: vision is reduced to text, long-horizon tasks reach ~5%, and the 60.4% in-domain vs 30.1% unseen gap remains. — [AGNOSTOS arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15660v3); [GitHub](https://github.com/jiaming-zhou/X-ICM); [NeurIPS 2025](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/cc92809cd8dfbd035801966ab4896741-Abstract-Conference.html)
- FOSIL (anonymous, under review for ICLR 2027; not peer-reviewed) compares in-context and few-step adaptation on the same pi0.5 architecture. In-context variants reach 21% and 25% on LIBERO-PRO task perturbations, against 25% zero-shot and 49% after one gradient step. Models trained with scene prediction alone ignored the demos, and designed "relation-support" pretext behaviours raised in-context success to 45.6% (context of this figure not fully clear from the page). — [FOSIL project page](https://fosilvla.github.io/)
- PALO (CoRL 2024) leaves the policy weights unchanged and optimises the language instead. A VLM samples candidate subtask decompositions of the new instruction, and PALO keeps the decomposition with the lowest validation error of the language-conditioned policy on the few demos (a "calibration set"). It reports beating pre-trained generalist policies and methods with the same demos on long-horizon real tasks. — [PALO arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.16228); [project page](https://palo-website.github.io/)
- An in-context VLA named "MoLe" could not be found (see Gaps).

#### D. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning, prompt/prefix tuning, inversion analogs
- OpenVLA-OFT raises OpenVLA's LIBERO 4-suite average from 76.5% to 97.1% with 26x throughput, using parallel decoding, action chunking, continuous actions and an L1 objective. It uses LoRA at rank 32 (8 GPUs, batch 64), with full per-task demo sets, not few-shot. — [OpenVLA-OFT (HF papers)](https://huggingface.co/papers?q=OpenVLA-OFT); [arXiv 2502.19645 listing](https://awesomepapers.io/multimodal/papers/2502.19645); [AWS LoRA blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/physical-ai/fine-tuning-openvla-on-amazon-sagemaker-ai-with-lora/)
- X-VLA (ICLR 2026) learns one soft-prompt embedding set per data source (embodiment), not per task. Adapting to a new domain first updates only the soft prompts and action head for 1,000 iterations, then warms up and trains jointly with LoRA. The README lists LIBERO PEFT (LoRA) checkpoints at 96.2 / 96.0 / 94.4 / 83.2% (Spatial / Object / Goal / Long; mean ≈ 92.5%) vs 98.1% for full fine-tuning. Secondary summaries say PEFT tunes ~9M parameters (~1%) and reaches 93% on LIBERO. — [X-VLA arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10274); [GitHub README](https://github.com/2toinf/X-VLA); [alphaXiv overview](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2510.10274v1); [ICLR 2026 PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/630d293833e09e1ecd892a898a20b074-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- FOSIL updates all pi0.5 (and MolmoAct2) parameters from one demo for 1–30 gradient steps of the model's own training loss (1 step for environmental, 10 for compositional, 30 for skill tasks), without meta-training or reward. LIBERO-10 compositions: 4.6% → 51.6% with 10 steps. LIBERO-PRO perturbations: 25% → 49% with 1 step. LIBERO-Object with demo refinement: 52% → 76%. Low rank: "At rank k = 1, pi0.5 keeps 96% of the gain on LIBERO-Object and 87% on LIBERO-10. Without robot pretraining (pi0.5-VL), it keeps only 41% and 52%." Real tasks: table bussing 75%, throwing 61.7%, lentil scooping 60%. — [FOSIL project page](https://fosilvla.github.io/)
- SimpleVLA-RL: after SFT on a single demo per task, RL raises LIBERO-Long success from 17.1% to 91.7%. This needs a simulator or reward and many on-task rollouts. — [SimpleVLA-RL arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.09674); [ICLR 2026 PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/cbfbcb4da14235bd69b134070898ae9d-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- TTT-VLA (ByteDance Seed, arXiv 2606.03127) trains a latent prompt alongside a proxy task. At test time it optimises only the latent prompt on self-supervised proxy signals from interaction data, with the policy frozen. On SimplerEnv tables, pi0.5 goes 51.1% → 63.5% with the latent prompt → 67.4% with TTT, and 67.5% → 68.9% → 72.4% in another table. The paper addresses distribution shift, not new tasks, and says gains come from "correcting a small number of critical decisions". — [TTT-VLA arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03127)
- Visual Attentive Prompting ("Bring My Cup!") is a training-free perceptual adapter for frozen VLAs. It highlights the target object instance in the image and rewrites the instruction, given a few reference images, for personalised commands ("my cup" among similar cups). It reports beating generic policies and token-learning baselines (textual-inversion-style) in success and correct-object rate on Personalized-SIMPLER, Personalized-VLABench and a real tabletop. Numbers were not extracted. — [VAP arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20014)
- RETAIN (robust fine-tuning via parameter merging) interpolates fine-tuned and pretrained weights, with α ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 0.75} and the best around 0.5–0.75. Setups: pi0-FAST-DROID with ~50–100 demos (real) and pi0 with ~45 demos per task (LIBERO). The merged model beats both endpoints on out-of-distribution variants of the new task and matches the pretrained model on generalist evaluations (44 DROID tasks, 20 LIBERO pretraining tasks). Approximate figure readings on the DROID whiteboard task: fine-tuned ID ~80% / OOD ~30–50%, RETAIN-co-FT ID ~75% / OOD ~80%. LoRA did "slightly worse on whiteboard". — [RETAIN arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08333v1); [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08333)

#### E. Demonstration generation from one or a few demos
- MimicGen (CoRL 2023) produced 50K+ demos across 18 tasks from ~200 human demos by replaying object-centric segments relative to new object poses. — [MimicGen arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17596)
- SkillMimicGen produced 24K+ demos from 60 human demos, with a 75.4% generation success rate vs 40.7% for MimicGen (secondary summary). — [SkillMimicGen arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.18907); [summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/skillmimicgen)
- DexMimicGen produced 20K+ bimanual dexterous demos from 60 source demos across 9 tasks. Downstream policies reach, e.g., Can Sorting 97.3%, Tray Lift 88.7%, Transport 83.3%. — [DexMimicGen project](https://dexmimicgen.github.io/)
- DemoGen (RSS 2025) turns 1 source demo into 243–432 synthetic demos per task by 3D point-cloud editing and trajectory adaptation, at 0.01 s per trajectory (22 s total for the real experiments, vs an estimated ~2.1 min per trajectory for MimicGen), with a DP3 policy. MetaWorld: 1 demo + DemoGen 91/88% (max/avg) vs 10 human demos 70/68% and 25 human demos 94/91%. Real (8 tasks): 74.6% vs 11.0% with the one demo alone. Limits: single-view "visual mismatch", segmented point clouds required, spatial generalisation only, and no new motion primitives. — [DemoGen arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16932); [GitHub](https://github.com/TEA-Lab/DemoGen)
- CP-Gen generates demos with new object geometries and poses from "a single expert trajectory", using keypoint-trajectory constraints. Over 16 sim + 4 real tasks it averages 77% vs 50% for the best baseline, with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. On pose-only variants it scores 85% vs 63% for MimicGen data. — [CP-Gen arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03944); [search-indexed PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03944)
- RialTo (RSS 2024) builds a digital twin from a quick scan, brings real demos into sim by "inverse distillation", and robustifies the policy with RL in sim. It reports a >67% increase in robustness across 8 real tasks. — [RialTo arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03949); [project](https://real-to-sim-to-real.github.io/RialTo/)
- ROSIE uses text-guided inpainting to change only instruction-relevant scene regions, so policies can do novel tasks and resist distractors. — [ROSIE project](https://diffusion-rosie.github.io/)
- GenAug uses depth-guided diffusion to change backgrounds, textures and object categories and to add distractors. Trained from 10 demos in one environment, it reports a 40% generalisation gain. Secondary press numbers: 85% in unseen environments vs 38% without augmentation, and 46–54% vs 8–10% on new objects. — [GenAug arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.06671v2); [project](https://genaug.github.io/); [AI Business](https://aibusiness.com/meta/meta-s-genaug-using-generative-ai-to-teach-robots-new-tasks)
- DreamGen (NVIDIA) generates "neural trajectories" from video world models. A humanoid performed 22 new behaviours in seen and unseen environments, with teleop data from only one pick-and-place task in one environment. GR00T-Dreams / Cosmos generate trajectories from a single image + language prompt. — [DreamGen](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/gear/dreamgen); [arXiv 2505.12705](https://bytez.com/docs/arxiv/2505.12705/paper); [NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/enhance-robot-learning-with-synthetic-trajectory-data-generated-by-world-foundation-models)

#### F. Model merging / task arithmetic for policies
- MergeVLA found that standard merging of VLA experts fine-tuned on LIBERO suites gives "zero success rate" for all merged variants. With only four tasks, >75% of LoRA parameters are "selfish" (relevant to exactly one task). Even with the VLM merged perfectly, averaging VLA-Adapter action experts gives 0%. A single-suite expert scores 0% on other suites. Task arithmetic on OpenVLA fails, merging only the vision + projector reaches 44.2%, and adding a task mask reaches 62.4%. MergeVLA (sparse task-masked LoRA, cross-attention-only action expert, test-time task router from the initial observation) reaches up to 90.2% average with TIES/WUDI. — [MergeVLA arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18810); [project](https://mergevla.github.io/)
- RETAIN shows that interpolating pretrained and fine-tuned weights (a two-model merge) keeps generalist skills while adding the new task (see D). — [RETAIN arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08333v1)
- PoCo composes diffusion policies at inference by combining score predictions (sampling from the product distribution) across data domains, modalities and tasks. Policy Merging (PoMe) merges separately trained policy weights for fleet learning. — [PoCo arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.02511); [PoMe arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01362)

### Inferences
- Family A (STRAP-style retrieve-then-train) costs a full policy training run per task and needs the prior corpus at deployment. On LIBERO-10, plain fine-tuning (51.7%) already beats STRAP at fixed K (45.6%). Our own STRAP number (40.0%) falling below our LoRA (60.0%) matches that pattern.
- Families B and C (weight-free) only work when the backbone was trained to read context (RICL post-training, RA-VLA's contextual-adherence loss, Instant Policy's pseudo-demo training, ICRT's prompt-format training). FOSIL shows pi0.5's in-context variants at 21–25% vs 49% after one gradient step. A frozen pi0.5 trained on narrow LIBERO data has no reason to attend to a demo placed in context. Optimising the context, as our 18 KB prefix does, is the gradient-based workaround.
- RICL's in-context result (31.25%) roughly equals 20-demo fine-tuning (31.67%) on pick-and-place-like tasks and degrades on new motions. Our prefix matches LoRA where the failure is reading and trails it where new motion is needed. Both suggest context can steer which known behaviour runs but cannot add new motor skills.
- FOSIL's rank-1 result on pi0.5 (96% / 87% of the gain on LIBERO-Object / LIBERO-10) implies our rank-16 LoRA (18.7 MB) likely compresses about 16x to roughly 1.2 MB at rank 1 (simple arithmetic: 18.7 / 16) with modest loss. That would sit between the 18 KB prefix and the current LoRA. Not tested on our protocol.
- Family E fixes spatial/pose coverage, not word reading. Generative visual augmentation in the ROSIE/GenAug style could create counterfactual scenes where the named object or relation changes and the action changes with it. That supplies exactly the contrast the frozen VLA never saw, and could train a prefix to read the word. This is a hypothesis, not a published result.
- Family F: naive averaging of per-task VLA LoRAs fails outright (MergeVLA), so a per-task adapter library needs routing (select one), not merging. A prefix-per-task library with a router mirrors MergeVLA's test-time router at far lower storage.

### Gaps
- No published paper evaluates a frozen pi0.5 with one demo on held-out LIBERO-90 tasks. The closest are FOSIL (pi0.5, LIBERO-10/Object/PRO, under review), RA-VLA (GR00T N1.5, 3 demos, 4 LIBERO suites) and STRAP (LIBERO-10, 5 demos, non-VLA policy).
- An in-context VLA named "MoLe" could not be identified. The only "MoLe-VLA" known from memory is a layer-skipping efficiency method, which was not verified in this session.
- Exact numbers for RoboPrompt, PALO, ICRT prompt length, VAP and the token-learning baselines were not extracted (abstract pages lacked them).
- The original Behavior Retrieval and FlowRetrieval numbers were not extracted; only STRAP's re-runs on LIBERO-10 are reported. SAILOR (CoRL 2022) predates the 2023 window.
- RETAIN numbers are approximate figure readings by the fetch tool, not table values.
- No work was found that applies textual-inversion-style token learning to a VLA for a new task, beyond VAP's mention of "token-learning baselines".

## Key Question 2: Which methods reach high success on a new task from ONE demo, and at what storage and compute cost?

### Takeaway
High one-demo success comes from methods that either (i) use strong structure plus open-loop replay (MT3 68–78%, Instant Policy 71–89% with 1–2 demos, DemoGen 74.6% real) or (ii) take a few gradient steps on a robot-pretrained VLA (FOSIL pi0.5 51.6% on LIBERO-10 compositions; SimpleVLA-RL 91.7% with RL). Pure in-context VLAs on LIBERO stay at ~20–40% even with 3 demos. Per-task storage ranges from kilobytes (prompts, text, open-loop demo) to megabytes (LoRA, retrieval feature banks) to a full policy (STRAP, DemoGen).

- Our setting, for comparison (user's numbers): rank-16 LoRA 60.0% at 18.7 MB; prefix KV token 33.3% at 18 KB; STRAP 40.0%. All use one demo, a frozen pi0.5 and held-out LIBERO-90 tasks.

### Inferences
- In bytes per task, our 18 KB prefix sits below RA-VLA (~2 MB per 16-step segment; a LIBERO demo of a few hundred steps would hold many segments, depending on stride) and far below LoRA (MBs) and STRAP/DemoGen (a whole policy). Among weight-free methods only text-space methods (PALO, X-ICM, KAT, RoboPrompt) store less, and none of them run inside a VLA's action head.
- Our LoRA's 60% from one demo on held-out LIBERO-90 tasks is in the same range as FOSIL's 51.6% (pi0.5, 10 steps, LIBERO-10 compositions) and above RA-VLA's 38.5% (3 demos). This only holds as orientation, given the different suites.
- The strongest one-demo real-robot numbers (MT3, Instant Policy, DemoGen) all rely on explicit object geometry (point clouds, pose estimation, segmentation). Their failure modes (pose, segmentation, retrieval) differ from a VLA's word-reading failure.

### Gaps
- Per-task bytes are rarely reported. RA-VLA is the only retrieval-VLA paper with a per-segment storage figure, and FOSIL does not report the storage of its updates.
- No paper gives one-demo LoRA vs prefix vs retrieval on the same VLA and protocol.

## Key Question 3: Which methods avoid weight changes entirely (pure prompting or retrieval), and how well do they work for VLAs trained on narrow data?

### Takeaway
Weight-free adaptation needs a backbone trained to read context, and even then it reaches roughly the level of small-data fine-tuning, not beyond. Off-the-shelf VLAs ignore in-context demos (FOSIL; RICL's premise), and VLAs trained on narrow data reach near zero on unseen LIBERO tasks without adaptation (RA-VLA). Methods that keep the VLA frozen but optimise or edit its input (soft prompts, latent prompts, language decomposition, visual prompting) are the closest weight-free analogs to our prefix token.

### Cited Findings
- RICL's motivation: "VLAs pre-trained with imitation learning objectives do not naturally acquire ICL abilities". It needs a post-training stage (400 demos, 20 tasks) before in-context adaptation works. After that, 31.25% in-context vs 2.5% zero-shot vs 31.67% fine-tuned on 20 demos, with failure on new motions. — [RICL arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02062v1)
- FOSIL on pi0.5: in-context variants reach 21% / 25% vs 49% after one gradient step on LIBERO-PRO perturbations. Scene-prediction-only training made the models ignore demonstrations. — [FOSIL](https://fosilvla.github.io/)
- RA-VLA: the vanilla VLA scores near zero on unseen LIBERO tasks (reported "0.017"). With a frozen backbone plus a trained retriever and action head, it reaches 38.5% from 3 demos. The authors note that absolute success "remains low relative to in-distribution performance". — [RA-VLA arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25585)
- MergeVLA: single-suite LIBERO experts score 0% on other suites, which "indicates that existing VLA models lack cross-skill generalization". — [MergeVLA PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18810)
- AGNOSTOS: "all prior models completely fail on at least eight of the 23" unseen tasks, including pi0, OpenVLA and RDT. LLM in-context prompting (X-ICM) reaches 30.1%, without touching any VLA. — [AGNOSTOS arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15660v3)
- ReCAP: on a frozen world-action model, a retrieved action chunk plus a learned residual reaches 31.5% on unseen RoboTwin tasks vs 4.0% without the pool. Pure replay of the retrieved trajectory gets 26.0%, so most of the gain comes from retrieval itself. — [ReCAP arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15631)
- Frozen-VLA input optimisation: MAP-VLA learns additive soft prompts per task stage (+7.0 on LIBERO-Long, training tasks only) ([MAP-VLA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.09516)). TTT-VLA optimises only a latent prompt at test time (pi0.5 51.1→67.4% on SimplerEnv, distribution shift) ([TTT-VLA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03127)). X-VLA uses per-embodiment soft prompts, updated alone for the first 1,000 adaptation iterations ([X-VLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10274); [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2510.10274v1)). VAP does training-free visual highlighting and instruction rewriting for instance-specific commands, beating token-learning baselines ([VAP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20014)). PALO picks a language decomposition by validation loss on a few demos ([PALO](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.16228)).
- Models built for in-context use from the start (Instant Policy, ICRT, KAT, REGENT) reach high one-to-few-demo success, but none is a language-conditioned VLA trained on narrow data like LIBERO. Instant Policy trains on ~700K procedurally generated demos; KAT relies on GPT-4's text pretraining. — [Instant Policy](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12633); [ICRT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15980); [KAT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19578); [REGENT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04759)

### Inferences
- For a frozen pi0.5 trained on narrow LIBERO data, putting the demo in context will likely fail without ICL post-training (RICL, FOSIL). Optimising a prefix (our 18 KB KV token) is the weight-free-at-inference route that does not need such post-training. The literature closest to it is MAP-VLA, TTT-VLA and X-VLA's soft prompts, and none of those evaluates one-demo held-out tasks.
- Our failure diagnosis (the model does not read object/relation words) matches the input-side methods: VAP edits the image and rewrites the instruction, and PALO rewrites the language. That suggests text or visual-prompt optimisation as a cheaper, zero-byte complement to the prefix for reading failures, with LoRA kept for the new-motion cases. This is a hypothesis.
- Across RICL, ReCAP, FOSIL and our own numbers, context and prompts steer selection among known behaviours, while new motion needs weight change (or open-loop replay of the demo, as in MT3 and ReCAP's retrieval-only arm).

### Gaps
- No published one-demo, held-out-task result exists for prefix or prompt tuning on a frozen VLA. MAP-VLA covers training tasks and TTT-VLA covers distribution shift.
- Whether RICL-style ICL post-training on LIBERO-90 would let a frozen pi0.5 read new object/relation words from a demo is untested.
- REGENT's robotics results are on non-VLA benchmarks, and the exact numbers were not extracted.
