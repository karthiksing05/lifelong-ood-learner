# Improving a FROZEN flow-matching VLA without changing its weights: noise steering, value-guided sampling, test-time search, residual policies, guidance-weight learning, verifiers (as of 2026-09-27)

Scope note: every arXiv ID below was opened on arxiv.org (abs page or HTML) on 2026-09-27 and its title/authors/date matched. Venues are marked "verified" only when an arXiv comment, a project-page BibTeX, a proceedings page, or a conference virtual page stated them; otherwise they are marked unverified. Numbers are quoted from the papers (HTML full text or abstract) unless marked as inference.

Our setting, for reference: frozen pi0.5 (`pi05_libero`, PyTorch), 10-step Euler flow sampling, 10-step x 7-dim action chunks, 320-dim initial noise per chunk (10 x 32 padded dims); a product-of-experts guide mixes a demo-derived teacher into the predicted velocity at every Euler step; a gated LoRA "fold" (~258 KB) in contact windows; sparse binary success on ~20-30 init states per held-out LIBERO-90 task; tens of practice rollouts per task; zero-training composition solves ~61%; imitation of successes plateaus at ~0.66 on unseen inits.

## Q1. DSRL (Diffusion Steering via RL): mechanism, parameters, rollout budget, pi0 LIBERO and real-robot numbers, one-demo settings, successors

### Takeaway
DSRL keeps pi0 frozen and trains a small SAC actor whose "action" is the sampler's initial noise; on pi0 it steers a single 32-dim noise vector that is tiled across the whole 50-step chunk, and lifts one held-out LIBERO-90 pick-and-place task from about 20% to about 100% in about 10,000 environment steps, and two real Franka tasks in 65-80 episodes. It is the closest published RL method to our budget. The strongest direct evidence for pi0.5 comes from QPILOTS: on 6 held-out LIBERO-90 tasks with the frozen `pi0.5-LIBERO` checkpoint, DSRL raises mean success from 26 to 58 and critic-gradient steering raises it to 76. That result used 5x10^5 environment steps, far beyond tens of rollouts. The 2026 successors mostly change how the noise actor gets supervised: action-to-noise inversion from human corrections, trust regions, DCT subspaces, noise combined with a residual, and failure gating.

### Cited Findings
**Identity and venue**
- "Steering Your Diffusion Policy with Latent Space Reinforcement Learning", Wagenmaker, Nakamoto, Zhang, Park, Yagoub, Nagabandi, Gupta, Levine; arXiv 2506.15799 (v1 2025-06-18). Venue CoRL 2025, verified from the official repo's BibTeX. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15799); [dsrl_pi0 README](https://github.com/nakamotoo/dsrl_pi0)
- Code: the general DSRL code is at github.com/ajwagen/dsrl. The pi0 code is at github.com/nakamotoo/dsrl_pi0 (JAX, built on openpi, MIT license, with LIBERO / Aloha / Franka-DROID scripts). The project page is diffusion-steering.github.io. — [project page](https://diffusion-steering.github.io); [dsrl_pi0](https://github.com/nakamotoo/dsrl_pi0)

**Mechanism**
- A latent-noise policy pi^W chooses the noise w that the frozen diffusion/flow policy maps to an action. It replaces sampling w ~ N(0,I). Only the small latent-space actor and critic train, and the diffusion policy stays frozen. — [DSRL HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799v2)
- DSRL-NA ("noise aliasing") keeps two critics. Q^A lives on the action space and Q^W on the noise space, and Q^W distills Q^A "by sampling w~N(0,I) and generating the corresponding A-action". This exploits the fact that many noises map to the same action, so off-policy action data trains the noise critic. — [DSRL HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799v2)
- Chunk handling: DSRL does not learn a critic over the full chunked noise space W=(R^d)^C. It trains "a single-step actor pi_single(s) and critic Q_single(s, w_single) where W_single = R^d" and reuses that one noise across the chunk. Each chunk counts as one RL step, and reported timesteps are raw environment steps. — [DSRL HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799v2)
- In the pi0 code, the SAC action space is `Box(-1, 1, shape=(1, 32))` ("32 is the noise action space of pi 0"). The 1x32 noise is repeated to fill all 50 rows before `agent_dp.infer(obs, noise=noise)`. The critic uses a sparse -1/0 reward. — [dsrl_pi0 train_sim.py / train_utils_sim.py](https://github.com/nakamotoo/dsrl_pi0)
- The LIBERO run (`run_libero.sh`) uses the `pi0_libero` checkpoint on `libero_90` task_id 57, with `pixel_sac`, batch 256, discount 0.999, 20 gradient steps per update, 500 initial steps, 64x64 images, `action_magnitude 1.0` (tanh bound on the noise), `query_freq 20` (20 env steps executed per chunk), and `hidden_dims 128`. The real-robot script uses hidden 1024, 128x128 images, `action_magnitude 2.5`, query_freq 10, and 2 Q-functions. — [run_libero.sh](https://github.com/nakamotoo/dsrl_pi0/blob/main/examples/scripts/run_libero.sh); [run_real.sh](https://github.com/nakamotoo/dsrl_pi0/blob/main/examples/scripts/run_real.sh)
- Common hyperparameters: 3 actor and 3 critic layers, 2 critics, learning rate 3e-4, tanh activation, target entropy 0. Robomimic uses hidden size 2048 and noise bound b_W 1.5. The paper gives no parameter counts. — [DSRL HTML, Tables 3-4](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799v2)

**Numbers with pi0**
- On LIBERO (one pick-and-place task from LIBERO-90), DSRL improves pi0 "from ~20% to ~100% after only ~10,000 online samples" (3 seeds). The same text reports that "neither ResiP nor V-GPS are able to significantly improve the performance of pi0" on the LIBERO and AlohaTransferCube tasks. — [DSRL HTML Sec. 5.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799v2)
- Real Franka with the pi0-DROID checkpoint and 0-1 success reward: "Turn on toaster" went from 5/20 to 18/20 after 80 online episodes (~11,000 steps). "Put spoon on plate" went from 15/20 to 19/20 after 65 episodes (~10,000 steps). — [DSRL HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799v2)
- Other settings: DSRL reports at least a 5-10x sample-efficiency gain over DPPO, IDQL, DQL, DIPO, and QSM on Robomimic/Gym. A real WidowX multi-task Bridge policy went from 20% to "nearly 100%" while RLPD failed. — [DSRL HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799v2)
- Few-demo setting: the single-task real Franka base policy was trained on "10 human demonstrations". No one-demo base-policy experiment appears. — [DSRL HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799v2)

**Successors and close relatives (all keep the base frozen unless noted)**
- **QPILOTS** (arXiv 2606.14801, 2026-06-11; venue unverified) keeps "the public pi0.5-LIBERO checkpoint frozen and learn[s] only a task-specific critic online". At every denoising step it projects the intermediate state to a clean-action estimate (Tweedie: `clip[x_t + (1-t) v(x_t,t,s), -1, 1]`), evaluates the critic gradient there, and rescales that gradient to the magnitude of the base drift. Table 4 covers LIBERO-90 tasks 26/31/38/59/60/64 after 5x10^5 env steps. Mean success: zero-shot pi0.5 26, DSRL 58, best-of-N (N=5, highest-Q) 40, EXPO 39, CGQL-LinEx 31, QPILOTS-U 76, QPILOTS-M 72. — [QPILOTS HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14801)
- **GR-RL** (ByteDance; arXiv 2512.01801). The final online stage performs "online RL by learning a latent space noise predictor" on a VLA, after offline-RL progress filtering. It reports 83.3% success on autonomous shoe lacing. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01801)
- **UniSteer** (arXiv 2605.10821, 2026). It combines noise-space SAC with human corrections: fixed-point Euler inversion (M=16 iterations per step) maps each corrective action to a noise target. The actor MLP is [1024,1024,1024], the reward is sparse terminal, and the paper mentions a pi0-architecture checkpoint. Across 4 real tasks success went from 20% to 90% in 66 minutes on average, against 55% for DSRL and 60% for DAgger. There are no simulation results and no code URL. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10821); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10821)
- **FlowDAgger** (arXiv 2607.08877; CoRL 2026 per project page). Action inversion ("per-step fixed-point iteration", M=5) turns expert actions into noise targets. A lightweight latent policy (observation encoder + MLP) is trained by regression, with no RL. Base models are pi0.5, Cosmos-Policy, and GR00T N1.7. On 12 MetaWorld tasks with pi0.5, mean success went from 0.53 to 0.78, against 0.64 for Residual-DAgger and 0.55 for DSRL. Real tasks used 5-20 intervention episodes, and Toolbox Packing rose from 13% to 80% with 10 interventions. Held-out skills were preserved (mean 0.88). — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08877); [project](https://microsoft.github.io/FlowDAgger)
- **LP-DS**, Lagrangian Perturbation Diffusion Steering (arXiv 2606.01151; "Accepted ... at ICML 2026" per arXiv comment). It learns "a compact noise-space perturbation" under a Lagrangian trust region that bounds deviation from the latent prior, and it keeps higher action entropy than unconstrained noise steering. It was tested on a frozen pi0 on the LIBERO-90 task "pick up the cream cheese and put it in the tray" (numbers only in a figure) and on a real Franka. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01151); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01151)
- **HAF-Steer** (arXiv 2608.16837). Latent offline-to-online RL on a frozen flow VLA that "leverages flow-matching invertibility and DCT-based dimensionality reduction to restrict RL optimization to a compact noise subspace", with a regularized SAC. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16837)
- **RFS**, Residual Flow Steering (Su, Westenbroek, Nagabandi, Gupta; arXiv 2602.01789; venue unverified). It jointly optimizes "a residual action and a latent noise distribution". The paper frames DSRL as input modulation and residual RL as output modulation, and argues DSRL "remain[s] constrained to the demonstration manifold". — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01789); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01789)
- **Symmetry-aware steering of equivariant diffusion policies** (arXiv 2512.11345) shows the latent-noise MDP of an equivariant diffusion policy is group-invariant. Equivariant steering improves sample efficiency and prevents value divergence "even when EDPs are trained from extremely limited demonstrations". — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11345)
- **RL^2-VLA** (arXiv 2607.26991; code github.com/marmotlab/RL2-VLA) trains an offline-RL policy on VLA action-expert latents and composes its flow velocity with the frozen VLA's velocity. The steering activates "only when failure is predicted", with gains up to +17.3% out-of-domain on SIMPLER and PolaRiS. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26991); [project](https://rl2-vla.github.io)
- **StructRL** (arXiv 2608.15139) names a failure mode, "Structured Noise Dilution": "noise added at an intermediate flow time can be weakened by the remaining denoising steps before execution". Its fix moves exploration noise into action space, which trains the flow decoder (not frozen). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15139)
- **SOE** (arXiv 2509.19292; code github.com/EricJin2002/SOE) learns a compact latent of task-relevant factors and restricts exploration to the valid-action manifold as a plug-in module. It reports higher success and fewer rollouts than baselines over self-improvement rounds. Exact numbers were not extracted. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19292); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19292)

### Inferences
- DSRL's pi0 recipe carries over structurally to our 320-dim noise. The published trick of one 32-dim noise tiled across the chunk shrinks the RL action to 32 dims (or 7 if only the unpadded dims matter, though padded dims can still act through the action-input projection). A state-independent per-phase noise vector costs 32 floats = 128 B per phase in fp32. A DSRL-style MLP actor with hidden 128 is on the order of 10^4-10^5 parameters plus its encoder. That is an estimate: the paper reports no counts.
- The ~10,000 LIBERO env steps in DSRL come to roughly 25-70 episodes, assuming LIBERO-90 episodes of ~150-400 steps; per-episode length was not reported. The real-robot runs used 65-80 episodes. DSRL is therefore at "tens to ~100 episodes per task", the low end of RL budgets but still above "tens of rollouts". Our base succeeds ~61% of the time, against pi0's ~20% in DSRL, so the sparse reward is dense in our case, which should help.
- StructRL's dilution result bears directly on us. Our PoE guide pulls every Euler step toward the teacher, so an initial-noise perturbation may be partly washed out. Any DSRL-style knob should be tested for how much the executed chunk changes as the noise varies (d chunk / d w) before RL.
- UniSteer and FlowDAgger show that a demo can be inverted into a noise target, and our teacher already is a demo. A zero-RL variant would invert the successful practice chunks, or the teacher chunks, to initial noise and regress a tiny noise actor. It is still imitation of successes, which plateaued for us in weight space (see Q2 for evidence that failure data is what breaks such plateaus).
- QPILOTS is the only paper found that measures DSRL, best-of-N, EXPO, and critic guidance on frozen pi0.5-LIBERO on held-out LIBERO-90 tasks, our exact checkpoint family. Its ranking (critic-gradient steering > DSRL > BoN) holds only at 5x10^5 steps. How the ranking behaves at tens of rollouts is unknown.

### Gaps
- No paper found runs DSRL (or any noise-steering RL) from a ONE-demonstration composed base, or with fewer than ~50 episodes on LIBERO.
- DSRL gives no parameter counts for its actor or critic, and no per-episode length for its LIBERO runs.
- Which LIBERO-90 task is DSRL's `task_id 57` was not checked. The paper only says "a pick-and-place task".
- A WebFetch summary described QPILOTS's pi0.5 as "33B-parameter". That is almost certainly a summarization or typo error (pi0.5 is ~3B) and was not verified.
- UniSteer's base model is not named explicitly in its main text.

## Q2. Value-guided sampling, Q/classifier guidance, best-of-N with verifiers, ITPS, evolutionary / CEM noise search, and tree search over chunks

### Takeaway
Weight-free selection methods split into two groups. Some rerank N samples with a value or verifier: V-GPS, IDQL, Hume, Q-Planning, RoboMonkey, CoVer, FOREWARN, SeeQ. Others inject a value gradient or run MCMC inside the sampler: QPILOTS, Guided Action Flow, QGF, ITPS, EVE. Frozen VLAs contain the right behavior far more often than pass@1 shows (33% to 92% at pass@32 in SVA), so selection has large headroom, but only if the scorer is good. Plain best-of-N with a weak Q gains little on pi0.5-LIBERO-90 (26 to 40). The most budget-relevant result is Q-Planning: a frozen BC policy plus a Q-function retrained on 20 episodes per iteration broke a plateau in which SFT on successes stalled. Tree search (VLAPS, V-VLAPS, SVA, VLA-Reasoner) needs a simulator or world model, which fits LIBERO practice but not deployment unless it is distilled into a value model.

### Cited Findings
**Value-guided reranking of samples from a frozen policy**
- **V-GPS** (Nakamoto, Mees, Kumar, Levine; arXiv 2410.13816; CoRL 2024, verified from arXiv comment). It samples K actions and executes a_t ~ Softmax(Q(s,a_i)/beta), or the argmax when beta -> 0. The value function is Cal-QL (or IQL), 25.6M params, with a ResNet-34 and FiLM language conditioning, trained on Bridge V2 + Fractal with sparse +1 on the last H=3 transitions. One value function is shared across Octo-small/base, RT1-X, and OpenVLA, and policy weights are untouched. Real WidowX improvement is +82.8% overall. SIMPLER gains range from +0.07 to +0.26 success rate. K=50 inference is 1.59x slower. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13816v2); [project](https://nakamotoo.github.io/V-GPS)
- **IDQL** (arXiv 2304.10573). It samples from "a diffusion parameterized behavior policy" and uses "weights computed from the critic" to importance-sample the intended policy. The actor is the frozen behavior diffusion and only critics train. Code: github.com/philippe-eecs/IDQL. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10573)
- **Hume** (arXiv 2505.21432; venue unverified; project hume-vla.github.io, code github.com/hume-vla/hume). A pi0-style VLA with a Cal-QL value-query head samples N=5 candidate chunks (H=30) at different noise levels and executes the argmax-Q chunk. It reports LIBERO 98.6% average (LIBERO-Long 96.7%, "+11.5% over pi0"). The value head is trained jointly (System 2 frozen after stage 1), so this is not a post-hoc frozen-base method. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21432v4)
- **Q-Planning** (arXiv 2608.21204, 2026-08-21). A frozen large BC policy (FastWAM) is paired with a small off-policy Q-function (~500M Q-decoder) over action chunks. At inference it draws N=64 (LIBERO) or N=32 (RoboTwin) candidates with 3-step flow draws and executes a softmax Q-weighted average (temperature lambda=1, chunk H=32, first 10 steps executed on LIBERO). Only the Q-function is retrained online, on both successes and failures. On LIBERO-10, success rose from 93% to 99% over 10 iterations. On real bimanual tasks it started from 100 demos and used 20 online episodes per iteration for 5 iterations: stack-cups went from 40% to 90% and insert-wallet from 25% to 80%, while "SFT on successful rollouts alone stalls at 55% and 30%". "Under an identical online budget Q-Planning is the only method, among Best-of-N, filtered SFT, IBRL, DSRL, and DAWR, that improves stably from failures." — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21204); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21204)
- **SeeQ** (arXiv 2609.22085). Subtask-elicited Q-functions predict the active subtask in language and then estimate its value, which shortens TD horizons. The SeeQ value function "substantially improves best-of-N policy steering" on 4 real bimanual tasks. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22085)
- **LPS**, Latent Policy Steering (arXiv 2507.13340). A world model pretrained on optical flow plus a value function evaluates action candidates from a BC policy and picks the best. It reports +10.6% on Robomimic, and on real robots a 70% relative gain with 30-50 demos and 44% with 60-100 demos. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13340)

**Gradient / guidance inside the sampler (frozen base)**
- **QPILOTS**: the critic gradient is evaluated at the clean-action estimate at each Euler step (details in Q1). On frozen pi0.5-LIBERO it reaches 76 mean across 6 LIBERO-90 tasks, against 58 for DSRL and 40 for BoN N=5. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14801)
- **Guided Action Flow** (arXiv 2607.02092) keeps SmolVLA frozen and adds action-chunk-critic gradients to reverse-time flow sampling. The critic is trained with offline IQL "on 100 real-robot rollouts". With beta=2, success went from 19/40 (47.5%) to 34/40 (85.0%). With a distractor it completed 6/12, against 0/11 for frozen SmolVLA. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02092)
- **QGF**, Q-Guided Flow (arXiv 2606.11087): "policy optimization entirely at test time". It pretrains a BC flow and a critic and then uses the value gradient at test time. It reports being competitive with training-time offline RL on OGBench-type benchmarks. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11087)
- **EVE** (arXiv 2512.21430) wraps a frozen generative policy with zero-shot VLM verifier agents. "an action incorporator uses classifier guidance to fuse aggregated verifier feedback into action denoising", with no training. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21430)
- **SVDD**, soft value-based decoding (arXiv 2408.08252; code github.com/masa-ue/SVDD). Derivative-free guidance integrates soft value functions for intermediate noisy states into standard inference, with no fine-tuning and no differentiable reward. The domains are images, molecules, and DNA/RNA, not robots. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08252)

**Verifier-based best-of-N / test-time scaling for VLAs**
- **RoboMonkey** (Kwok et al.; arXiv 2506.17811; CoRL 2025, PMLR vol. 305 pp. 3200-3217, verified from project BibTeX). It samples N=5 actions, fits a Gaussian to translation/rotation with a majority vote on the gripper, resamples K=16, and scores with a LLaVA-7B-based verifier trained on 20M synthetic preference comparisons. Base is OpenVLA. SIMPLER in-distribution improves +9% (47.5%). Real out-of-distribution goes from 35% to 60%, and LIBERO-Long improves +6.7%. 16 candidates take ~650 ms on an H100. Code: github.com/robomonkey-vla/RoboMonkey. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17811v2); [project](https://robomonkey-vla.github.io)
- **CoVer-VLA** (arXiv 2602.12281; same first author). It jointly scales rephrased instructions and sampled actions and uses a contrastive verifier. It reports +22% in-distribution and +13% out-of-distribution on SIMPLER against scaling policy pretraining on the same data, and "a further 45% improvement in real-world experiments". — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12281)
- **MG-Select** (arXiv 2510.05681; "Accepted to ICLR 2026" per arXiv comment) is verifier-free. It uses KL divergence from a reference distribution produced by the same VLA with masked state and language as a confidence score for choosing among candidates. It needs condition-dropout training for the best reference. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05681)
- **FOREWARN** (Wu, Tian, Swamy, Bajcsy; arXiv 2502.01828; RSS 2025, verified from project page). It draws 100 diffusion-policy samples, clusters them to 6 plans, imagines each plan's outcome with a DreamerV3-style latent world model, and has a fine-tuned Llama-3.2 VLM select a plan. Real results: Cup 0.30 to 0.80, Bag 0.20 to 0.70, Fork 0.10 to 0.70. Selection takes 3.7 s. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01828v3); [project](https://yilin-wu98.github.io/forewarn/)
- **SVA** (arXiv 2607.03751): "frozen VLAs already contain competent behaviors in their output distribution, with overall success rates rising from 33% at pass@1 to 92% at pass@32". It runs MCTS in simulation, distills returns into a lightweight Q model, and at deployment picks the uncertainty-regularized best candidate without simulator access. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03751)
- **E-TTS** (arXiv 2606.27268; "Accepted to ECCV 2026" per arXiv comment) does history-aware iterative refinement with VLM verifiers. It reports up to +33.14% in simulation and +26.62% on real robots without retraining. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27268)
- **SDN**, Selected Diffusion Noise (arXiv 2606.14084), is training-free noise selection for pi0 and GR00T-N1.5/N1.6: candidate noises far from a reference set that give coherent trajectories. It reports +8% in simulation and +10% on real robots. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14084)
- **BOKBO** (arXiv 2605.30660) is a caution on best-of-K. With perturbation-based K-sampling, the confidence proxy and K-sample disagreement "correlate at 0.98 with the action-noise hyperparameter sigma, while correlating at the noise floor with actual safety violations". It adds conformal abstention. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30660)

**ITPS (inference-time policy steering)**
- ITPS (Wang et al.; arXiv 2411.16627; ICRA 2025, verified from arXiv comment) compares six steering methods on a frozen Diffusion Policy: random sampling, output perturbation, post-hoc ranking (N=32), biased initialization, guided diffusion, and stochastic sampling (annealed MCMC, M=4 MCMC steps per diffusion step). Stochastic sampling approximates the PRODUCT of policy and objective, while guided diffusion approximates their SUM. Real kitchen results as alignment / constraint satisfaction: random 38%/90%, GD 37%/82%, SS 71%/73%, OP 89%/37%. Alignment trades against in-distribution success. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16627v2); [project](https://yanweiw.github.io/itps/)

**Evolutionary / population / CEM search over noise or chunks**
- **Diffusion-ES** (Yang et al.; arXiv 2402.06559; CVPR 2024, verified via CVF open access). It samples trajectories from a diffusion model, scores them with a black-box (non-differentiable) reward, and mutates high scorers through truncated diffusion (renoise then denoise), which keeps them on the data manifold. Domain is nuPlan driving. Code: github.com/bhyang/diffusion-es. — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Yang_Diffusion-ES_Gradient-free_Planning_with_Diffusion_for_Autonomous_and_Instruction-guided_Driving_CVPR_2024_paper.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06559)
- **INSPO** (arXiv 2609.21220, 2026-09-18) does noise-space trajectory optimization for one-step generative policies with "population-based particle optimization" and a regularizer that keeps the noise in-distribution. It covers Push-T, Can, and LIBERO-Spatial with generalist VLAs, and "improves task success and constraint satisfaction over best-of-N sampling and action projection". — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21220)
- Image-domain precedent: Ma et al., "Inference-Time Scaling for Diffusion Models beyond Scaling Denoising Steps" (arXiv 2501.09732). Only the title and ID were verified here; the abstract was not re-read. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09732)
- **SAILOR** (arXiv 2506.05294; NeurIPS 2025 spotlight per NeurIPS virtual site) learns a world model and a reward model from demos and searches at test time to recover from mistakes. Code: github.com/arnavkj1995/SAILOR. — [NeurIPS](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/115876); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05294)

**Tree search over action chunks**
- **VLAPS** (arXiv 2508.12211) is MCTS biased by VLA action priors and run with a model of the environment. It reports success gains "by as much as 67 percentage points" over VLA-only. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12211)
- **V-VLAPS** (arXiv 2601.00969) adds a value head on VLA features trained on offline rollouts' Monte-Carlo returns. Across five LIBERO suites it "matches value-free planning ... at the default search budget". With a larger budget it gains +6 pp on LIBERO-Object and +4 pp on LIBERO-10. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00969)
- **VLA-Reasoner** (arXiv 2509.22643; "Accepted by ICRA 2026" per arXiv comment) runs MCTS over world-model-imagined futures, seeded by VLA predictions, with KDE confidence sampling and offline value estimates. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22643)
- **ELASTIC** (Li, Swamy, Bisk, Bajcsy; arXiv 2606.31132) learns with SAC a state-dependent schedule of denoising stride and number of parallel samples for a frozen policy. The verifier is a V-GPS critic fine-tuned with CQL (LIBERO) or Cal-QL (real). For pi0.5 on LIBERO it used "10 offline rollouts per task and 20 online rollouts". On real Franka pi0.5 (base 45%) it matches best-of-10 success with 34% lower latency. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31132)

### Inferences
- Our sampler is already a product of experts (teacher x prior). ITPS's result that product-style MCMC beats sum-style gradient guidance on alignment favors keeping the PoE form and adding the value as another factor, sampled with a few Langevin/MCMC corrector steps per Euler step. Simply adding a critic gradient is the weaker, sum-style option.
- A best-of-N over compositions (N initial noises and/or N alternative teacher leaf choices per chunk) is weight-free. Its whole difficulty is the chunk-level scorer. With 20-30 init states and tens of rollouts per task, a from-scratch per-task Q is data-starved; V-GPS-style shared critics were trained on Bridge/Fractal scale. The cheapest credible scorer is a small probe on frozen pi0.5 features: SAFE (Q5) shows VLA features encode success and failure across tasks, and Q-Planning shows a Q retrained on 20 episodes per iteration is enough to help.
- In LIBERO practice we can save and restore simulator state. That makes chunk-level search (MCTS / Diffusion-ES-style renoise-and-denoise mutation, scored by actual continuation success) possible during practice only. The SVA pattern is to search in simulation and distill into a light Q for deployment, and in our case the search output could be distilled into the fold or into a noise policy.
- The SVA pass@k gap (33% to 92%) and our own gap (61% zero-training vs ~0.66 imitation ceiling) suggest the composition's sample distribution already contains many successes. Selection or steering, not new weights, is the likely lever.

### Gaps
- No paper found reports verifier-based best-of-N on pi0.5 with a per-task scorer trained from only tens of rollouts. ELASTIC's pi0.5 LIBERO numbers (10 + 20 rollouts per task) concern compute allocation at matched success, not success gains over best-of-10.
- Exact numbers for V-VLAPS per suite, VLA-Reasoner, SDN per task, and LP-DS's pi0 LIBERO curve were not extracted (abstracts only, or figure-only).
- Venues unverified: Hume, QPILOTS, Guided Action Flow, QGF, EVE, SVA, V-VLAPS, VLAPS, INSPO, CoVer-VLA, SDN.

## Q3. Residual RL over a frozen base (Policy Decorator, residual policy learning, ResiP, EXPO, PA-RL, ResFiT, PLD, ...)

### Takeaway
Residual RL adds a small bounded correction network on top of a frozen base and trains it with SAC or PPO on sparse reward. It works reliably, but the published budgets are large. They range from about 10^5 steps per task (ResFiT, PLD at 250k steps per LIBERO-90 task) to millions (Policy Decorator, ResiP). Real-robot runs need 134-343 episodes (ResFiT) or 1-1.5 hours (HiL-ResRL, EXPO-FT), usually with human help. DSRL reports that ResiP could not improve pi0 on its LIBERO task. Residual RL fits poorly with tens of rollouts, except as a very low-dimensional, bounded correction inside a window, which the gated fold already approximates.

### Cited Findings
- Origins: Residual Policy Learning (Silver, Allen, Tenenbaum, Kaelbling; arXiv 1812.06298) and Residual RL for Robot Control (Johannink et al.; arXiv 1812.03201). — [arXiv 1812.06298](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06298); [arXiv 1812.03201](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.03201)
- **Policy Decorator** (Yuan et al.; arXiv 2412.13630; ICLR 2025, verified from project page; code github.com/tongzhoumu/policy_decorator). A SAC residual is bounded to (-alpha, alpha), and a progressive exploration schedule mixes base and residual over H steps. For example: StackCube H=1M, alpha=0.03; PushChair H=4M, alpha=0.2. Bases were Behavior Transformer (~1M params) and Diffusion Policy (~4M). Fine-tuning baselines (SAC, Cal-QL, RLPD) "generally failed", and JSRL / vanilla residual RL were inconsistent. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13630v1); [project](https://policydecorator.github.io)
- **ResiP** (Ankile et al.; arXiv 2407.16677; ICRA 2025 per IEEE Xplore listing). A PPO per-step residual sits on a chunked diffusion BC policy with sparse reward. It reaches 98% on one_leg from 50 demos, "an 18 percentage point improvement over BC with 100K demonstrations". A one-step residual needs ~75M env steps, and a chunked residual ~250M steps to reach ~85%. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16677v4); [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel8/11127273/11127223/11127442.pdf); [code](https://github.com/ankile/robust-rearrangement)
- **ResFiT** (Ankile et al.; arXiv 2509.19301; venue unverified; code github.com/amazon-far/residual-offpolicy-rl). An off-policy residual (UTD > 1, n=3, LayerNorm, ensemble Q, 50/50 demo/online sampling) on a frozen action-chunked BC policy, with sparse binary reward. It reaches >90% on Can and Square in ~75-150k steps, and reports ~200x fewer steps than PPO residual (200k vs 40M). On a real 29-DoF humanoid: WoollyBallPnP 14% to 64% after 134 rollouts (~15 min), PackageHandover 23% to 64% after 343 episodes (~76 min). — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19301v2)
- **PLD**, Probe-Learn-Distill (Xiao et al.; arXiv 2511.00091; ICLR 2026 per project page). It freezes the VLA (pi0 or OpenVLA) and trains lightweight residual actors, with the residual scaled to [-xi, xi] (xi=0.5 on LIBERO, 0.1 on SimplerEnv). The Q is warm-started with Cal-QL, and hybrid rollouts run the base for a random number of steps before the residual takes over. With sparse reward over 250k online steps on 8 LIBERO-90 tasks, it reaches ">95% performance on every task" it fine-tunes. The successes are then distilled into the VLA by SFT (weights change at that stage). It also tests fine-tuning on LIBERO-90 and one-shot evaluation (one human demo per task) on LIBERO-10, where PLD data beat self-bootstrapped data but "still falls short" of human demos. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.00091v1); [project](https://wenlixiao.com/self-improve-VLA-PLD)
- **EXPO** (Dong, Li, Sadigh, Finn; arXiv 2507.07986; code github.com/pd-perry/EXPO). A small Gaussian edit policy (bounded +-beta) is trained to maximize Q, and an on-the-fly policy picks the highest-Q action among base and edited samples. The base is NOT frozen: it is fine-tuned online with an imitation loss. It reports "up to 2-3x" sample efficiency. Venue conflict: a search summary says NeurIPS 2025, but ML Anthology lists it under ICLR 2026. On frozen pi0.5-LIBERO-90 (QPILOTS Table 4) EXPO reached a mean of 39, against 26 zero-shot. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07986v3); [ML Anthology](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2026/dong2026iclr-expo/); [QPILOTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14801)
- **EXPO-FT** (arXiv 2605.25477) does RL fine-tuning of VLAs (weights change), reaching 30/30 on all tasks within an average of 19.1 minutes of online robot data. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25477)
- **PA-RL** (Sobol Mark et al.; arXiv 2412.06685; ICLR 2025 Workshop FM-Wild per ML Anthology, main-conference status unverified; code github.com/MaxSobolMark/PolicyAgnosticRL). It samples actions, reranks them with Q ("global"), takes gradient steps on the actions ("local"), and then distills the result into the policy, so base weights DO change. OpenVLA went from 40% to 70% after 40 minutes of online RL. A diffusion policy went from 50% to 90% with 40 episodes. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06685v1); [ML Anthology](https://mlanthology.org/iclrw/2025/mark2025iclrw-policyagnostic/)
- **DAWN** (arXiv 2602.10539) identifies two bottlenecks in residual-RL value learning, "cold start pathology" and "structural scale mismatch" (the residual is dwarfed by the base action). Its fixes are base-policy transitions as a value anchor and critic normalization. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10539)
- **Foresight Residual RL** (arXiv 2607.16506; "Accepted at IROS2026"). Residual RL over a frozen VLA with per-subtask sparse rewards improves each subtask but not the chained task. Weighting rewards by a learned "foresight value" of the terminal state reaches 85.6% full-task success, against 54.5% for standard subtask residual RL. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16506)
- **HiL-ResRL** (arXiv 2606.22860): a model-agnostic residual on VLA actions with human-in-the-loop, ">95%" average real success "within only 1.5 hour". **BORA** (arXiv 2605.30226): an offline critic on VLM tokens and action chunks, then a frozen VLA with a HiL chunk-wise residual, +33% absolute on 5 real dexterous tasks. — [HiL-ResRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22860); [BORA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30226)
- DSRL reports that ResiP "[was not] able to significantly improve the performance of pi0" on its LIBERO and Aloha tasks. — [DSRL HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799v2)

### Inferences
- The residual literature's reported budgets (≥10^5 steps in simulation, or ≥100 real episodes with human intervention) are 1-3 orders of magnitude above tens of rollouts per task. The evidence does not support a from-scratch residual RL per held-out task at our budget.
- The Foresight Residual RL result maps onto our phases. If reward is shaped per phase (for example per contact window), the handoff state quality matters: a phase-level "success" that leaves a bad state for the next phase gains nothing.
- A residual on the composed chunk is still worth one cheap variant: a per-phase constant bias, or a bias on a few DCT coefficients, bounded like PLD's xi, and tuned as a bandit on episode success (Q6). That is a residual with ~10-70 parameters, not a network.

### Gaps
- No residual-RL paper found reports results with fewer than ~50 episodes per task on LIBERO, or with a one-demo base.
- Exact per-task PLD LIBERO-90 curves at low budgets (<20k steps) were not extracted; the figure only shows curves.
- Venues unverified: ResFiT, RFS, SOE, EXPO (conflicting).

## Q4. Learning or tuning guidance weights / guidance schedules with rewards (learned CFG weights, reward-guided flow at inference)

### Takeaway
Theory backs treating a guidance weight as a policy-improvement knob. CFGRL shows pi ∝ pi_ref · p(optimal|s,a)^w, and raising w improves returns up to a point and then declines. Learning sampler parameters (guidance scales, strides, numbers of samples) with RL on a frozen generator has been done for pi0.5 (ELASTIC, compute schedule) and for text-to-image (LeSAMP, guidance and noise schedules). Our PoE guide weight per phase is a low-dimensional knob of exactly this kind. No robotics paper was found that learns a per-phase guidance weight for success with tens of rollouts.

### Cited Findings
- **CFGRL** (Frans, Park, Abbeel, Levine; arXiv 2505.23458; OpenReview forum exists, venue unverified; code github.com/kvfrans/cfgrl). pi(a|s) ∝ pi_hat(a|s) · p(o|s,a)^w. It trains a conditional + unconditional policy on binary optimality labels (advantage ≥ 0), with 10% unconditional dropout. The tradeoff "can be adjusted without retraining", and performance saturates or declines when w is too high. Examples: walker-stand 782 vs 603 for AWR; pointmaze-giant 30 vs 2 for GCBC. It needs optimality-conditioned training, so the policy weights are trained, but w is set at test time. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23458v1); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=PwaUsAk4i6)
- **RCFG**, Reward-Weighted CFG (arXiv 2604.15577): "reward weighted classifier-free guidance ... can act as a policy improvement operator ... approximating tilting the sampling distribution by the Q function". Autoregressive models and molecules, not robots. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15577)
- **Policy Gradient Guidance** (arXiv 2510.02148) brings CFG-style conditional/unconditional interpolation to policy-gradient RL as a "test-time control knob". — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02148)
- **ELASTIC** (arXiv 2606.31132) casts compute allocation as a meta-MDP over a frozen policy's denoising process. SAC learns the per-sample stride (0 prunes a sample), and the reward is Q_task minus compute costs. It used 10 offline + 20 online rollouts per task for pi0.5-LIBERO. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31132)
- **LeSAMP** (arXiv 2607.23488) is RL over prompt-conditioned, timestep-varying sampling parameters (CFG scale, negative prompts, noise schedules) for Flux.1 / SD3.5, rewarded by preference models and a VLM judge. It reports up to 68.12% win rate. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23488)
- Guidance-strength evidence from steering papers: Guided Action Flow uses beta=2 critic guidance (47.5% to 85.0%). QPILOTS rescales the critic gradient to the base-drift magnitude instead of using the theoretical sigma_t^2/2 coefficient. ITPS found that stronger steering raises alignment but lowers constraint satisfaction (Q2 numbers). — [Guided Action Flow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02092); [QPILOTS HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14801); [ITPS HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16627v2)
- **RL^2-VLA** gates steering by predicted failure: action diversity helps when the base is likely to fail and "can unnecessarily perturb already-accurate actions when success is likely". — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26991)

### Inferences
- Our guide weight per phase (and possibly per Euler step) is the analog of CFGRL's w: it trades teacher adherence (optimality proxy) against the pi0.5 prior. With 3-5 phases, w is a 3-5-dim continuous knob. Bayesian optimization, CEM, or a Thompson-sampling bandit over it, with episode success as reward, is feasible at 20-30 inits x a few rollouts each. The stored cost is bytes per task. This follows ELASTIC and LeSAMP in spirit (RL over sampler parameters of a frozen generator) but has not been published for robot success.
- CFGRL's non-monotone curve (gains, then decline at high w) predicts an interior optimum per phase. This is a testable prediction: sweep w per phase on held-out init states before any RL.
- The RL^2-VLA gating result suggests conditioning the guide weight on a predicted-failure score, with more teacher pull or more diversity only where a failure probe fires. That is a contextual bandit with one scalar context.

### Gaps
- No robotics paper found that learns a CFG or guidance weight (or a per-phase schedule) from sparse task success with a frozen VLA. LeSAMP is image-only, and ELASTIC learns compute, not guidance strength.
- "Guidance Is Not a Hyperparameter: Learning Dynamic Control in Diffusion Language Models" (arXiv 2605.07701) and "Hierarchical Variational Policies for Reward-Guided Diffusion" (arXiv 2605.21661) appeared in search listings only; neither was read.

## Q5. Success / progress detectors and value functions usable with only tens of rollouts; reliability

### Takeaway
In LIBERO simulation the binary success signal is ground truth, so the open problem is chunk-level credit assignment, not success detection. For chunk values, the most data-efficient published options are small probes on the frozen VLA's own features (SAFE: VLA features encode success and failure generically across tasks) and small Q-functions retrained each iteration (Q-Planning, 20 episodes per iteration). Pretrained VLM progress models (GVL, VLAC, Robo-Dopamine 2.0) offer one-shot in-context progress, with order-correlation up to ~0.95-0.99 on benchmarks. They are fragile to paraphrase and camera, and a 2026 survey finds that "credibility falls as availability rises".

### Cited Findings
- **SuccessVQA** (Du et al.; arXiv 2303.07280) frames success detection as VQA on Flamingo, fine-tuned with human reward annotations. It generalizes to unseen language and visual changes in simulation and on real robots, and struggles on in-the-wild egocentric video. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07280)
- **GVL** (Ma et al.; arXiv 2411.04549; ICLR 2025, verified from project page). Gemini-1.5-Pro predicts completion percentages on shuffled frames. Value-Order Correlation (VOC) is the rank correlation with chronological order. On ALOHA the median VOC is 0.12 zero-shot and 0.37 one-shot (single in-context example). Success detection accuracy is 0.71 zero-shot and 0.75 one-shot, against 0.62-0.63 for SuccessVQA. Failure cases include poor camera angles (remove-gears VOC 0.19) and periodic tasks. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04549v1); [project](https://generative-value-learning.github.io)
- **VLAC** (arXiv 2509.15937; venue unverified; code github.com/InternRobotics/VLAC, weights on HF) is a pairwise progress-delta and done-signal model (InternVL 2B/8B) trained on >4,000 h of data. It "supports one-shot in-context transfer to unseen tasks" and reaches 0.95 VOC-F1 on unseen RT1. Used as the reward for real-world PPO (actor weights trained), it lifts success "from about 30% to about 90% within 200 real-world interaction episodes". — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15937v1)
- **Robo-Dopamine 2.0** (arXiv 2608.15680) is a history- and OOD-aware pairwise process reward model. Mean VOC rises from 0.967 to 0.986 with reference panels, and OOD-robust VOC from 0.906 to 0.958. Downstream RL reaches 86.8% on RoboTwin and 71/80 real insertions. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15680)
- **ReWiND** (arXiv 2505.10911; "CoRL 2025 Oral" per arXiv comment) learns a language-conditioned reward from a small demo set and uses it to adapt to unseen task variations. It reports "up to 2.4x in reward generalization", 2x in simulation adaptation, and a 5x real-world improvement. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10911)
- **SAFE** (arXiv 2506.09937; "NeurIPS 2025 camera ready" per arXiv comment). "VLAs have sufficient high-level knowledge about task success and failure, which is generic across different tasks". SAFE learns a scalar failure score from VLA internal features, trained on successful and failed rollouts, and tested on unseen tasks with OpenVLA, pi0, and pi0-FAST, using conformal prediction for timing. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09937)
- **AHA** (arXiv 2410.00371) is a VLM for detecting and reasoning about manipulation failures. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00371)
- **GR-RL**: "by directly applying offline RL with sparse reward, the resulting Q-values can be treated as a robust progress function". — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01801)
- **pi*0.6 / RECAP** (Physical Intelligence; arXiv 2511.14759) does advantage-conditioned RL from experience and corrections. It "more than doubles task throughput and roughly halves the task failure rate" on the hardest tasks. It trains the VLA weights. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14759)
- **UR-VC** (arXiv 2607.12892): normalized time within a demonstration, the usual progress proxy, "is only a noisy proxy" in contact-rich tasks where progress can be lost. It corrects the labels by retrieving similar states across episodes. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12892)
- **ROBORMBENCH** (arXiv 2609.05401) evaluates 2,390 real trajectories with 21,673 paraphrases. Paraphrasing the instruction "can even flip identical robot behavior between failure and success", scale and reasoning do not reliably help, and trajectory-grounded reward models are more stable. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05401)
- **"No Free Checker"** survey (arXiv 2609.09250) covers ~150 verifiers: "credibility falls as availability rises ... there is no free checker". It proposes nine metrics for checkable verifier claims. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09250)
- **SeeQ**: subtask-level Q shortens TD horizons and improves best-of-N steering (Q2). **FIERCE** (arXiv 2609.18651) is a progress-failure evaluator calibrated on target-task rollouts, used for shaping. — [SeeQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22085); [FIERCE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18651)

### Inferences
- For our simulator-based practice, the most reliable chunk-level scorer at tens of rollouts is a logistic or small-MLP probe on frozen pi0.5 features (prefix and/or action-expert state), trained on all practice rollouts. Label each chunk with its episode's final success, or with a Monte-Carlo return using a discount. This follows SAFE's cross-task finding. Retrain it each practice iteration as in Q-Planning. It stores a few KB to a few hundred KB per task (estimate), or shared parameters if trained across tasks.
- VLM progress models (GVL/VLAC) are best kept as tie-breakers or shaping, not as the success label. In simulation they add noise the ground-truth signal does not have, and ROBORMBENCH shows instruction-paraphrase flips. If used, keep the instruction string fixed and give the one demo as the in-context example (GVL: VOC 0.12 to 0.37 with one example).
- Because our demo defines phases, a per-phase value (SeeQ's subtask idea) with phase-completion labels read from simulator predicates would shorten the credit-assignment horizon at no labeling cost.

### Gaps
- No paper found reports calibration of a success or value probe trained on only 20-60 rollouts of a single held-out task. SAFE and Q-Planning use more data, or data pooled across tasks.
- Exact VOC and accuracy numbers for SuccessVQA on robot data, and SAFE's AUROC values, were not extracted.

## Q6. Catalogue: budget, frozen or not, stored bytes/params, code, and concrete adaptation to our pipeline

### Takeaway
The candidates closest to our budget (tens of rollouts, 20-30 inits, sparse binary success, frozen pi0.5, a composed PoE sampler) are:
- (a) a bandit or CEM over a handful of sampler knobs per phase: guide weight, noise scale, tied noise, fold gate. This costs bytes per task and has no published robotics precedent at this budget.
- (b) Q-Planning-style best-of-N or a Q-weighted average over N composed samples, scored by a small probe on frozen pi0.5 features and retrained every practice round on successes and failures. It is the published method that broke a success-imitation plateau.
- (c) DSRL with a tied 32-dim noise, the lowest-budget RL method with pi0 results (65-80 episodes).
- (d) QPILOTS-style critic-gradient guidance, added as one more expert inside our Euler steps. It is the best on pi0.5-LIBERO-90, but only at 5x10^5 steps.

### Inferences: concrete adaptations to our pipeline, ordered by fit to "tens of rollouts, bytes per task"
1. **Bandit or CEM over sampler knobs (weight-free, ~10-100 bytes per task).** Parameterize per phase k: guide weight w_k (CFGRL analog), an initial-noise scale s_k, and optionally one tied 32-dim noise z_k (DSRL's tiling). Optimize with CEM or a Thompson/GP bandit on episode success over the 20-30 practice inits, in rounds of ~10-20 rollouts. Keep a held-out init split to catch overfitting. Precedent is indirect: ELASTIC trained a sampler-schedule meta-policy for pi0.5-LIBERO with 10 + 20 rollouts per task, LeSAMP learned guidance schedules with RL, and CFGRL explains why w improves the policy. Test d(chunk)/d(z) first because of StructRL's dilution result.
2. **Best-of-N or a Q-weighted average over compositions, scored by a frozen-feature probe (weight-free on pi0.5; probe ~KBs).** At each chunk, draw N compositions (different initial noises and/or alternative teacher leaves) and score each with a probe on pi0.5 features, trained on all practice rollouts, failures included (SAFE, Q-Planning). Execute the argmax, or the softmax Q-weighted average (lambda around 1). Retrain the probe every practice round. Q-Planning is the direct precedent for breaking a "SFT on successes stalls" plateau (55% vs 90%) with 20 episodes per iteration. BOKBO warns that sample disagreement is a bad safety or confidence proxy, and QPILOTS warns that BoN with a weak Q gains little (26 to 40). Practice-time, simulator-assisted search (restore state, try K chunk candidates, keep the successful continuation: the MCTS/SVA pattern) can generate labels for this probe far more cheaply than full episodes.
3. **DSRL on the composed sampler (tiny actor; tens of KB to ~1 MB).** A SAC actor outputs a tied 32-dim noise, or a few DCT coefficients per dimension (HAF-Steer), from pi0.5 features, with a bound (`action_magnitude` ~1) and optionally LP-DS's trust region toward N(0,I). This is the lowest-budget RL with published pi0 numbers (65-80 real episodes, ~10k LIBERO steps). It warm-starts well because our base is at ~61%, not 20%. UniSteer/FlowDAgger inversion of the teacher chunk gives a supervised initializer for the actor.
4. **Critic guidance as an extra expert in the Euler loop (QPILOTS / Guided Action Flow).** Add beta * grad Q(Tweedie clean estimate) to the PoE velocity, rescaled to the drift magnitude, with ITPS-style MCMC corrector steps if a product form is wanted. It is the best on pi0.5-LIBERO-90, but its critic budget is 5x10^5 steps. Guided Action Flow's 100 rollouts with offline IQL is the closer data point.
5. **Residual on the composed chunk.** Published residual RL budgets are too large (≥10^5 steps). A bounded per-phase constant bias tuned with option 1 is the budget-compatible version, and the gated fold already plays this role in contact windows.
6. **Failure-gated steering (RL^2-VLA).** Apply extra teacher weight, diversity, or best-of-N only where the probe predicts failure. This protects the ~61% of inits the composition already solves.

### Gaps
- No method in the catalogue was evaluated with (one demo) + (frozen pi0.5) + (≤ ~50 rollouts per task) together. Every budget match above is an inference.
- Parameter counts for DSRL's actor and critic, SAFE's probe, and the PLD residual are not reported in the sources read, so the stored-bytes figures above are estimates.
- Code was not found for QPILOTS, Guided Action Flow, Q-Planning, ELASTIC, UniSteer, or INSPO.
