# Tables

Generated by `python experiments/make_paper_tables.py` from `results/aggregate.json`.
Every cell is mean ± standard error over seeds; `--` means the arm was not run in that
experiment. Do not edit by hand -- regenerate.

**Notation.** In each column, **bold** is the best score among the methods and <u>underline</u> is the second best.

Three qualifications, because a highlight is a claim:

* **Direction is per metric, not always "highest".** Columns where lower is better -- `FGT`, `drift`, `params`, `wall_seconds`, `memorized`, `tv_ratio`, `gen_rmse`, and the router's leak counts -- are ranked upward, so the bold cell is the smallest value.
* **Some columns are deliberately not ranked at all.** `vendi`, `diversity`, `content_ratio` and `novelty` carry no highlight, because for them the target is to MATCH real data rather than to maximise: our CelebA content diversity of 1.068 against real data's 1.007 is a good result, and a larger number would not be a better one. Bolding their maximum would assert something the metric does not say.
* **Reference rows never win.** Rows like `real`, `real (ceiling)` and oracle arms are held-out data or natively-trained references, not competitors, so they are excluded from the ranking and never highlighted -- a ceiling topping its own table is not a result.

Ties share the bold, and the underline then goes to the next distinct value.
